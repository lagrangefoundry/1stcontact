## Symptom

Production, 2026-11-08, the first production deploy with the job container (EPIC-32 / DOC-86, deployed after BUG-378). Any message to the consultant ends with *"The work stopped before it finished. Press Carry on to pick it up from there."*, and Carry on ends the same way.

Workers Logs for both turns (`turn_e6cf0f4e…` at 19:21:52 UTC, `turn_aa041d1f…` at 19:22:40 UTC):

- `round` step: the container logs `TypeError: fetch failed` within about 1 s, and the Workflow logs `StepFailure: fetch failed at runJobStepWhereItsComputeIs`. Three attempts.
- `abandon` step: fails the same way, three attempts.
- Then `job_lost … its runner reports it gone`, and the builder shows "the work stopped".

No model call is ever made. The container starts and answers `/health` (the `AgentContainer.jsrpc` calls succeed), but its first outbound call fails.

## Likely cause (to confirm)

`apps/control-app/src/agent-container.ts` starts the container with `enableInternet: true`, then registers the platform intercept:

    await this.container.interceptOutboundHttp?.(PLATFORM_ADDR, this.platform)

The container sends every platform call to `http://10.0.0.1` (`PLATFORM_URL`). That address only works if the intercept is registered. The call is **optional (`?.`)**, so a runtime that doesn't provide `interceptOutboundHttp` skips it silently. The container then calls an address nobody answers, and every step fails with `fetch failed`. Nothing else in the repo exercises the API against a real runtime, and `compatibility_date` is `2025-07-01`. Check, on production: whether `ctx.container.interceptOutboundHttp` exists for this Worker (it may need a later compatibility date or a flag); whether it must be registered before `start()`; and whether the failing call is the platform call or a direct internet call (the model) instead.

## Fix — LOGGING ONLY (rescoped by the operator, 2026-11-08)

This ticket adds the logging needed to diagnose two things on production, and changes no behaviour. The operator deploys it, reruns a turn, and decides the fix from what the logs show. Every log line is one JSON object, as the existing `job_step_failed` lines are, and carries the job id. None logs a token, an `Authorization` header, a model key, a query string or a request body.

### 1. Why the container's calls fail ("fetch failed")

- **The container names every outbound call that fails.** When a fetch made by the container's host fails (thrown, not a non-2xx answer), it logs `container_fetch_failed` with: the method; the target, as host plus path and no query string; whether that is the platform address (`10.0.0.1`) or an external host; and the error's whole `cause` chain (each level's `name`, `code`, `errno`, `syscall`, `address`, `port` and `message`, where present). Undici's "fetch failed" keeps the real reason in `cause`, which `job_step_failed` drops today.
- **`job_step_failed` carries the cause chain too**, from the container and from the Worker's `turn-job.ts`, alongside the existing message and stack.
- **The Durable Object logs whether the platform intercept was registered.** When `ready()` starts a container, it logs `job_container_intercept` with: whether `interceptOutboundHttp` exists on the runtime's container object; the outcome of calling it (`registered`, `absent`, or `failed` plus the error message); the address; and what kind of value the binding is (for example whether it has `fetch`). The optional call stays optional. This ticket logs its absence and doesn't change the behaviour.
- **`PlatformApi` logs every request it receives**: `platform_api_request` with job id, method, path, status and duration. This shows whether an intercepted call ever reaches the Worker. If the container logs `container_fetch_failed` for the platform address and no `platform_api_request` appears for that job, the intercept or its binding is the problem.

### 2. How long a reply spends starting its container (response time)

- **Each step logs its timing in the Durable Object**: `job_container_step_timing` with: the job and step kind; `cold`, which is true when the step had to start a container; `start_ms`, from `start()` until the health check first answers (0 when warm); `intercept_ms`; `step_ms`, from sending `/step` until the answer; `total_ms`; and whether the step succeeded.
- **The Workflow logs how long a step waited to start**: `job_step_queued` with `queued_ms`, from when the job was created (the client's message accepted) until its first step began, and from one step's end to the next step's start after that. Together with the timing line, this splits a reply's time into scheduling, container start and the step's own work.

### Not in this ticket (decide after the logged rerun)

The earlier fix items are deferred until the logs are read:
- Production turns run (switching the binding to `ctx.exports.PlatformApi(...)` and raising `compatibility_date`, if the logs confirm it).
- Making a missing intercept fail loudly (removing the `?.`).
- The `abandon` step ending the job from the Worker side when the container can't reach the platform.

## Findings from production logs (EPIC-32 session, 2026-11-08)

- **It is the platform call that fails, not the model.** Workers Logs over the last four hours show 18 container-side lines, all `job_step_failed … TypeError: fetch failed`: 6 `round`, 6 `abandon` and 4 shop `import` (`shop-import-imp_ce9079a3…`, 19:10 UTC). `abandon` and `import` steps never call the model, so the call that fails goes through the intercepted platform address `10.0.0.1:80`. BUG-380's shop imports are broken on production by the same cause.
- **The intercept call did not throw.** Had `interceptOutboundHttp` rejected, `ready()` would have thrown and the step message would read "the job's container stopped: …". It reads `fetch failed`, which is the container's own error. So either the method is absent and `?.` skipped it, or it resolved and the binding it was handed does not answer.
- **The container logs too little to tell which.** `job_step_failed` records only `err.message`. Undici's `fetch failed` keeps the real reason (ECONNREFUSED, a timeout, a reset) in `err.cause`, which is dropped. The "names the call that failed" item in the Fix must log `cause` (code and message) and the URL.
- **The likeliest difference from dev is the binding.** Cloudflare's documentation (developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/container/) types the binding as `Fetcher` ("Worker entrypoint or service binding"), but every example, and `@cloudflare/containers` itself, passes a loopback entrypoint `ctx.exports.<Entrypoint>({ props })`. We pass `env.PLATFORM`, a `[[services]]` binding from the Worker to itself, which local workerd accepts. `ctx.exports` needs a compatibility date later than our `2025-07-01`.
  - **Suggested fix order:** first, log `cause`, remove `?.`, deploy, read one failure. Then, if confirmed, switch to `ctx.exports.PlatformApi(...)` and raise `compatibility_date` in every environment, checking the date's other behaviour changes. That would also let the job's id and token travel as `props` instead of headers.
  - **Ruled out:** the order of the calls. Cloudflare documents that `interceptOutboundHttp` may be called "before or after starting the container" and applies to open connections. `@cloudflare/containers` registers before `start()` by choice only. Registering before `start()` costs nothing, though, and removes the doubt.
- **The production service binding name is correct.** `[env.production] name = "1stcontact-control-app"` matches `service = "1stcontact-control-app"`, so the binding does not point at another Worker.

## What landed (logging only, 2026-11-08)

All lines are one JSON object with the job id. None holds a token, `Authorization`, a model key, a query string or a body.

- **`container_fetch_failed`** (container, `src/job-diagnostics.ts` `loggingFetch`, wrapping the process's direct `fetch` in `container/server.ts`): `method`, `target` (host + path), `platform` (the hostname is `PLATFORM_URL`'s), `error` (`name: message`), `cause` (the chain, outermost first: `name`, `code`, `errno`, `syscall`, `address`, `port`, `message` where present). A call that answers, whatever its status, is not logged. The error is rethrown unchanged.
- **`job_step_failed`** in the container and in `turn-job.ts` now has `cause` (the same chain; empty on the Worker side, where the failure is a `StepFailure` built from the container's message).
- **`job_container_intercept`** (`JobContainer.ready()`, when it starts a container): `available`, `outcome` (`registered` / `absent` / `failed` + `error`), `addr`, `binding` (`type`, `hasFetch`, `constructor` name). Behaviour is unchanged: absent is still skipped, and a failed registration still throws after logging.
- **`platform_api_request`** (`servePlatformApi` in `platform-wire.ts`, which `PlatformApi.fetch` now calls; the 403 for another host moved into it): `job` (null without the header), `method`, `host`, `path`, `status`, `duration_ms`. Every request, including refused ones.
- **`job_container_step_timing`** (`JobContainer.runStep`, every step): `kind`, `cold`, `start_ms` (start() to first healthy answer; 0 warm), `intercept_ms`, `step_ms` (from sending `/step`; null if never sent), `total_ms`, `ok`.
- **`job_step_queued`** (`runConsultantTurn`, each attempt as it begins): `kind`, `step`, `queued_ms`, `since` = `job_created` (the Workflows instance's `event.timestamp`), `previous_step` or `previous_attempt`. After a Workflow replay, the first measurement is taken from when the cached step returned, which is the resume time. Shop imports are not covered.

Tests: `tests/test_UAT_FC_BUG-385_job_container_diagnostics.test.ts`, five UATs: the four in the test plan, plus `job_step_queued` measured from creation and then from the previous step.
