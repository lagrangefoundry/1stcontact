## What is wrong

**A delegation is metered if and only if its enclosing turn wrote a `turn_spend`
row.** Where the turn wrote one, the worker is attributed correctly. Where the
turn wrote none, the worker's spend is discarded — and the delegation becomes
invisible to every reader of the meter.

Measured on the dev environment 2026-09-24 (Lagrange Foundry,
`biz_5b101742d436573a04a2512fb7ecdbb5`):

- **Eight worker sessions have ever existed.** Four appear in a
  `turn_spend.attributed` list. **Four do not.**
- The split is not random. All four attributed workers ran inside a turn that
  wrote a spend row (4 of 4). All four lost workers ran inside a turn that wrote
  no row at all (0 of 4).

## Why it happens

`turnSpendRecord` in `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/spend-core.ts`:

```
const usage = countersOf(spend.usage)
if (!measured(usage)) return null
```

The **caller's own** usage gates the whole row, and `attributed` is a field of
that row, so it is discarded with it. The rule *"a turn that measured nothing
writes no record"* is right about the caller and wrong about the second party:
a turn that died before its terminal meta arrived — an error, an abandoned
stream — may still have caused a worker to run to completion on a real bill.

REQ-295's accounting criterion holds for every **delegation** exit path
(`reported`, `stopped`, `silent`, `failed`) and does not hold for the
**caller's**. That is the gap; the delegation surface is not at fault.

Today's instance: the 11:49 local turn ended
`D1_ERROR: string or blob too big: SQLITE_TOOBIG` (the last casualty of REQ-309,
already fixed), so its worker — 70 tool calls, 27 site writes, zero failures — is
unmetered.

## Why it matters more than the money

The absolute loss is small: comparable workers priced at **$0.14–$0.57** each.
The damage is to the instrument. REQ-293's split-by-model report is the
designated answer to *"did delegation move any work"* — the one number EPIC-20
intends to steer by — and on the record to date it under-counts delegated spend
by half. Neither of the two motivation levers now under consideration can be
evaluated with it until this is fixed.

## What it should do instead

Write the row when **either** the caller's usage is measured **or** `attributed`
is non-empty.

This deliberately widens *nothing, never zero*, and the widening is the decision
in this ticket rather than an accident of it. `turn_spend` gains rows whose four
counters are absent but whose `attributed` is not. Such a row is not a turn
claimed to be free — it says **"this turn caused measured spend elsewhere, and
none of its own was observed"**, which is a fact and not a zero. A reader that
sums the four counters is unaffected; a reader of `attributed` stops losing half
its subject.

Consequences to settle while implementing:

- `cost_micros` for such a row is the caller's own cost and is therefore **null**,
  not `0` — the same rule, one column over.
- `requests` is `0` legitimately: none were observed.
- The four counter columns are `NOT NULL` today. Whether they take `0` — a
  measured absence the row's own shape already disclaims — or the schema widens
  to nullable, is the implementer's call; the first is cheaper and the row is
  already unambiguous because `cost_micros` is null.
- `outcome` carries the turn's real end (`error`, `aborted`), which is what makes
  the row legible rather than mysterious.

## How it will be known to work

A turn that delegates and then fails before its own terminal meta arrives leaves
a `turn_spend` row whose `attributed` names the worker, whose `cost_micros` is
null, and which REQ-293's split counts on the delegated side.
