## What this epic is for

The **beta test experience**: getting the most out of the first dozen or so real people to use the
builder. The operator will sit with cohort-zero testers ([[DOC-71]] §2) through the first stages so
they don't fall off at the start. However a session is run, every tester should leave behind
**detailed, reviewable evidence** of where the experience worked and where it didn't.

This epic owns three things:
- the in-product feedback channel;
- the measurement of how long testers spend on things;
- the operator's review of each tester's session, transcripts included.

It doesn't own the fixes those reviews produce. Those go to the epic for the surface concerned
(EPIC-19 for the builder experience, EPIC-20 for cost, and so on).

## The experiences in scope

### 1. A Feedback button in the chat area

- **Always available:** a **Feedback** control in the builder's chat area that a tester can press
  at any moment, including mid-build.
- **It records where they are:** the conversation and the turn (the last message on screen), the
  current stage and phase of the site plan, the page and mode the preview shows, whether a build
  is running, and the time.
- **It asks quickly:** a multi-select list of common problems, then a free-text box at the bottom.
  Starting options (to be refined):
  - Not sure what to do next
  - The consultant's message is confusing
  - This is taking too long
  - The site doesn't look the way I wanted
  - Something looks broken
  - I was asked something I can't answer
  - I like this
- **It lands where the operator reviews people:** each submission appears on the tester's own
  **contact page** in the 1st Contact business's **Contacts**, as an entry in their history, with
  the recorded context. The operator can open the exact point in the conversation it was sent from.
- Submitting never interrupts the conversation or the build. It's acknowledged inline.

### 2. Time and progress data

The raw signals largely exist: the turn ledger (`turn_log`), the junction's turn and progress
records, surface-shown activity events, and plan stage changes. The work is turning them into a
per-tester timeline the operator can read:
- **how long** in each stage;
- **session length**: when a tester sends feedback or stops, had they just sat down or been
  working for hours? A session is a run of tester activity ended by an idle gap (exact gap to be
  set in the child ticket). Each feedback entry records time into the current session and total
  time on the site so far;
- **idle gaps and abandon points**;
- **how often** they used each surface.

- **reply time**: for each tester message, the time until the first visible reply text in that
  panel. This is the one wait the tester actually experiences; how busy the talker, worker and
  delegates are behind it doesn't matter to it.

**Not measured:** "waiting vs active" (dropped 2026-10-27). With a talker, a worker and its
delegates running concurrently, waiting on the system isn't a state the tester is in, and system
activity timing belongs to EPIC-19/EPIC-20 instrumentation. "Reading vs walked away" isn't
distinguished: the idle gap that ends a session covers walk-aways. No browser activity signals
(visibility, focus, typing) are collected. When a wait hurt, the tester says so with the
"This is taking too long" feedback option.

**Cost is not part of this epic** (dropped 2026-10-27). Spend stays in EPIC-20's metering.

This shows on the tester's contact page alongside their feedback.

### 3. Session review with transcripts: out of scope for now

Reading a tester's transcript, tool activity, feedback and timeline as one record (for example
with Claude) is deferred. Nothing here should make it harder later.

## Constraints

- **Production-ready, not dev-only.** Testers use `app.1stcontact.io`.
- **Feedback is operator-facing.** The tester sees only their own submission's acknowledgement.
- **Nothing here changes the consultant's behaviour.** Feedback isn't fed to the consultant
  mid-session unless a later ticket decides it should.

## Decisions (2026-10-27)

- **Everyone is a tester** for now. No beta-tester flag.
- **Testers are contacts.** A tester has to be a contact in the 1st Contact business to be invited
  and to sign in, so the contact record exists before their first login. Feedback and timeline
  attach to that contact. Checked in code: the invite starts from the contact
  (`POST /api/people/invite` → `invitePeople`), the session's subject is the contact's `users.id`,
  and for a signed-in request `admit()` returns that row as `admission.user`. Existing code already
  writes contact history with `contactEventInsert({ contactId: admission.user.id, … })`, so
  feedback entries can use the same path.
- **Sign-in:** 1st Contact's own magic link (REQ-475). Cloudflare Access stays on operator paths.
- **Consent** is part of the T&C the tester accepts. No per-review prompt.
- **Feedback button** goes in **every** chat panel and records which assistant it was sent from.
- **Feedback captures a screenshot of the preview** along with the page/mode reference.
