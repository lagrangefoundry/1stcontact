## Notes for the Editor

**The two violations are one drift with one root cause, and they share an
alternative resolution.** CAP-85 draws a careful line: this capability owns
*that the bytes and operations are reachable over this origin*; the editing and
write-path capabilities own *what they mean*. STORY-99 was written against
REQ-115's origin and never grew the two seams REQ-117 added to that same origin
in the very same bundle. So the capability promises a division of labour its only
story does not implement, and this capability's evidence does not cover its own
stated scope.

Note what this is **not**: it is not missing evidence for the *system*. Both
behaviours are proven, under the editing capability, by `test_UAT_AC1006_…` and
`test_UAT_AC999_…`. The defect is one of ownership, so **narrowing CAP-85 is a
legitimate and cheaper resolution than authoring ACs**, and if ACs are authored
they must assert *reachability and semantics-freeness over this origin* only —
duplicating AC-1006/AC-999's content here would trade a coverage gap for
duplicate evidence across capabilities. Pick one direction deliberately; do not
do both.

**Findings 3 and 4 are the same repair twice, and they are cheap.** Both are an
existing, real-entry-point UAT whose probe set stops short of one clause its AC
names — a gesture in AC-973, a tree in AC-964. Neither needs a new test file.
Doing them in one pass over
`tests/reconciliation-builder-workspace-{chrome,origin}.test.ts` is sensible.

**Finding 5 must wait on the ac level, and the ac level is currently red.**
REPORT-2101 (`report-5a4cf7a4`, ac level, 2026-08-16T09:43, FAIL, 3 violations)
is unrepaired, and its finding 1 is AC-966. This is why AC-966 is recorded here
as `pass` with a warning rather than as a coverage gap: the *behaviour* is
active, covered and covered twice; only the AC's wording lags REQ-119.

**Zero deprecations, and that is a checked result rather than an omission.** The
one hypothesis that would have produced them — REQ-145 retiring the Node origin
and the verbatim Worker front — is disconfirmed: REQ-145 and its dependency
REQ-147 are both `draft`, so STORY-99's "deliberate and temporary" clause and the
REQ-119 AC-1 deviation are current. Likewise AC-1030, whose provenance is thin
(BUG-32's body does not enumerate it, though it was authored inside BUG-32's
window and serves its stated anti-silent-green goal), is **not** `needs_review`:
it is in current code, it is an active AC, and nine other criteria depend on its
anchoring for their evidence to exist at all.

**Environment caveat, declared.** Ten of these ACs have mount-gated halves that
skip when the shared component store is absent (`tests/support/webui-installed.ts`).
This session's Bash permissions do not reach outside the repository, so the
presence of the out-of-band install could **not** be confirmed from here — unlike
REPORT-2102, which verified it. That does not change any verdict in this report:
coverage is a property of the tests, and the designed safeguard for the silent
green is AC-961/AC-963 asserting `WEBUI_INSTALLED` as an *outcome*
(`origin:462`) rather than gating on it, which is present and correct. But a
green run of these suites on a machine without the install would leave the
mount-gated halves unexecuted, and that is what those two unconditional
assertions exist to make loud.

**Not a finding, recorded so it is not mistaken for one.** AC-1036's confinement
probes overlap AC-978's rendered-channels tree in the same shape (noted as
warning 3 of REPORT-2102 and warning 5 of REPORT-2101). That is an *exclusivity*
concern for the ac level, not a coverage gap — both ACs are covered — so it is
deliberately absent from the table above.
