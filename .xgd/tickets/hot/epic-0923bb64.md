## Why this epic exists

A single hour of design work in the builder chat cost **$20** (2026-09-08, site-1stcontact). Claude was costing more than minimum wage to draw one hero segment, and the session felt slow as well as expensive. This epic holds what we learned, what was fixed, and what is still owed.

The whole diagnosis, the arithmetic behind every number below, and the design argument for delegation are in the chat transcript on **CHAT-47** (`chat-5011da11`) — 2026-09-09 to 2026-09-10. Read that for the reasoning; this body is the standing summary.

## What was actually wrong

Not the model. The request shape.

Every request is one prefix — `[tools][system][messages]` — and Anthropic's cache matches it from the start. Two consequences govern everything:

- a change invalidates everything **after** it, never before it;

- therefore anything that changes often must sit as **late** in the prefix as possible.

A tool-using turn is not one request. After every tool result the backend calls the API again carrying the whole conversation. The measured 2026-09-08 session was **15 exchanges but 117 round trips** — one turn alone ran 41 tool calls.

`cache_control` was applied to the **system assembly only**. The message history — including every screenshot — was re-sent uncached on all 117 round trips. Cost was quadratic in turn count. Measured breakdown of the ~70k of accumulated context: screenshots 33%, knowledge search 16%, `get_l1` 14%, `set_l1` 11% — but screenshots were only ~2% of tokens _billed_, because the expense was never their size, it was being re-sent 117 times.

Two aggravating facts found alongside it:

- **No token telemetry.** `usage` on the `message_start` frame was read and discarded, so spend could only be modelled, never observed.

- `max_tokens`** hardcoded at 4096.** A large `set_l1` payload hit the ceiling, the stream ended with no `content_block_stop`, and the tool was dispatched with an **empty input object** — silently. Observed twelve consecutive times in one session (2026-09-09, Lagrange Foundry site), 34% of all tool calls in that session failing, and the model retrying blind each time.

## The fix, and one thing we got wrong first

Caching the message history was the change with the best ratio, and it landed. But the first version only helped **within** a turn, not across turns — because the per-turn reminder (which carries "the site moved under you") sat at the end of `system`, and `system` precedes `messages`. Every turn the reminder changed, it invalidated the entire history behind it. 1stcontact's reminders change on nearly every turn by design.

That gap was ours: the original acceptance criterion said "within a turn" and never mentioned the second _turn_. The follow-up moved the volatile and reminder tiers **past** the history, appended to the final user message at request-build time and never written back into stored history. The principle was generalised, not reversed: stable first, volatile last — applied to the whole request rather than to one segment of it, because the request is the unit the cache matches on. Recorded as **DOC-22 Amendment X**.

## What is live in 1stcontact today

- `claude-opus-5`**, **`max_tokens`** 64000**, set in `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/backends.json` (BUG-67). The model is now this repository's decision, not a framework release — that seam is the part of BUG-49 we asked for.

- Message-history caching and per-request token telemetry are in the installed shared store and active on every session.

- The truncation failure that produced twelve empty `set_l1` calls is fixed on both halves: the ceiling is roomy, and an unfinished tool block is refused rather than dispatched.

Note the consequence of the Opus move: **the front face is now the expensive model.** Opus 5 is $5/$25 against Sonnet's $3/$15, and thinking is on by default. That is the right trade for judgement — what the thing is, what it means, which direction to take — and the wrong one for the mechanical majority of the work, where the payload is large and the decisions are small. Which is what the remaining work is about.

## What is still owed

1. **Re-measure, from telemetry rather than a model.** Every number above for the current configuration is an estimate; REQ-143/145 mean real `cache_read_input_tokens` and per-request spend are now recorded. Confirm cross-turn reads are non-zero and scale with history, and establish a fresh per-session baseline on Opus 5. The CHAT-47 figures predate the Opus move and are no longer comparable.

2. **Effort levels — the cheap lever, still not taken.** The backend sends no `output_config` at all. Effort does not touch the cache prefix, so it is a per-turn dial with no architectural cost: low for mechanical construction turns, high for strategy. Not implemented anywhere in the framework yet. Worth doing **before** delegation, and this epic should not be used to skip it.

3. **Adopt the delegation surface.** REQ-148 shipped and `delegation_surface.json` is installed in the shared store, but 1stcontact grants nothing from it — `instances.json` declares one role, `consultant`, and no worker. Adoption means: a builder role with a narrower grant, a backend configured for that role's workers, `DelegateWork` granted to the consultant and `ReportDelegatedWork` to the builder.

The shape the framework enforces, and which we should not fight:

- the brief is **prose** (intent cannot be enumerated), but authority comes from the **role's declared grant** — no phrasing of a goal widens it;

- the worker's conversation never enters the caller's, so the construction transcript stops being billed at the consultant's rate;

- **no nesting**, and worker spend **rolls up** into the caller's ledger, so this cannot make cost invisible again;

- `accept` checks are the load-bearing part. If Opus has to screenshot and re-inspect everything the worker did, the tokens have been moved to the more expensive side rather than saved.

The likely split, from the existing capability groups: builder keeps ReadSite, AuthorPages, ManageComponents, MeasureDrawings, DrawImages, SeeSite; consultant keeps ManagePages, WriteConfig and ManagePalette — brand colours, site structure and config are the decisions worth Opus.

1. **Choose the worker's model from measurement, not from estimate.** On the 2026-09-08 workload the bulk of spend was construction (21 screenshots, 21 `write_image`, 18 `set_l1`, 15 `get_l1`). Haiku 4.5 at $1/$5 would do that roughly 3× cheaper than Sonnet does today, with the strategic layer on Opus small enough that its rate barely matters. That is an argument for trying, not a sizing — item 1 supplies the sizing.

## Explicitly not the approach

**Per-turn model switching within one conversation** — Opus for strategy turns, Sonnet for construction turns, same session. Prompt caches are keyed per model. Alternating A/B/A/B roughly halves the hit rate and pays the writes twice, undoing most of what REQ-143/144 bought. Delegation exists precisely because each side then keeps its own clean cache lineage.

Charging the operator's turns at a typing rate rather than at the wall-clock gap — the session was multitasked, and the gaps are not work. On 09-20: 1.58 h of measured machine time plus 1.03 h of typing at 40 wpm = **2.60 h engaged**. Whole build: 3.18 h machine + 2.18 h typing = 5.36 h.

### What this changes about the path forward

1. **Persist the turn's **`usage`** at the host.** One write beside the audit flush in `ai.ts`. Nothing else here can be confirmed without it.

2. **Dial the window down to 10** — REQ-126's own stated experiment, never run, the largest single configuration lever at ~29%.

3. **Defer the tool definitions.** ~13k tokens of schema on every round trip for a grant of ~60 operations, most unused in any turn.

4. **Reconsider the 1h TTL.** Chosen for sessions paced by a person thinking; with a prefix invalidated by the window and by image ageing far more often than by an idle gap, the doubled write is paid many more times than the long lifetime is used.

5. **Make the window trim and image ageing cache-preserving** (framework). Trim at a breakpoint boundary, or in blocks, rather than one exchange per turn.

6. Effort levels and delegation are unchanged and still correct. Delegation is the largest single lever — over half — and it stacks with everything above.

## Metrics first: what to measure, before optimising anything

Three passes of the analysis above landed on $252, $55 and $107 for the same build. The first two were wrong by 4.5× and 2.4×, in opposite directions, and both errors were about numbers the API already reports and this repository already throws away. **That error band is wider than the margin of the business** — at $20/engaged-hour a $50/month plan sells two and a half hours; at $4 it sells twelve. Nothing downstream of this section is worth doing until the measurement exists.

Two instruments are needed, and they answer different questions.

### Instrument 1 — per-turn spend, from the API's own counters

**What the API reports.** Four counters per request: `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_read_input_tokens`, `cache_creation_input_tokens`. They partition the input side, and they are the only way to know what a turn cost, because a cached token and a fresh one differ by 20× in price and are indistinguishable from anything this repository currently records.

**What upstream already does.** REQ-143 captures `usage` off `message_start` and `message_delta`; `turnUsage` folds the turn's requests into `{usage, requests}`; `turnSpend` puts it on the terminal `done` event; REQ-148 §6 adds `sessionUsage` and `attributed`, so a delegated worker's spend rolls up into its caller. **All of it is built. None of it is written down here.**

**Where it hooks.** `streamPrompt` in `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/host-core.ts` already reads the terminal event — `if (event.kind === DONE) outcome = turnOutcome(event.meta)` — and already has a `finally` that calls `closePending(deps, sessionId, outcome)`. The spend record is the same shape of change in the same two places: fold `turnSpend(event.meta)` beside `outcome`, and write it in the `finally`.

**Through a port, not a table.** `deps.recordTurnSpend(record)`, following `LedgerDeps`: the Worker implements it against D1, the `1c` CLI appends to its own file or declines, and neither learns the other's storage. That is the same argument `ledger-core.ts` makes for being a third surface.

**What a record holds.** Tenant, session, turn id, start and end, role, model, outcome, request count, the four raw counters, `attributed` spend from any delegated worker, and a **cost in micros computed at write time**. Both, not either: the raw counters so a past period can be re-priced when rates change or when a model swaps, and the settled cost so a bill does not depend on a price table that has since moved. The price table is four rates per model — input, output, cache read, cache write — and it belongs beside `backends.json`, as this repository's configuration, for the reason BUG-49 gives.

**The first thing it must confirm** is that `cache_read_input_tokens` is non-zero and grows with the conversation. Every number in the section above assumes the caching is working; nobody has ever seen it work.

### Instrument 2 — engaged time, the unit the customer can understand

Token cost is not sellable. Consulting hours are. The meter has to be something a client would accept as fair without understanding any of the above.

**The definition, chosen from the data rather than assumed:** a turn's _engaged time_ is the wall clock from the client's message to the last thing the turn did, plus the gap until the next turn **capped at five minutes**. The clock pauses after five minutes of silence and resumes when they come back. This is how every time tracker works, it is measurable server-side with no assumption about the client, and it cannot be gamed by typing slowly.

## The metrics plan — two REQs, and what they are not

### What is actually missing, precisely

Narrower than "no telemetry". Tracing it end to end:

- `ClaudeAPIBackend` captures `usage` per request and puts `{usage, requests}` on its terminal event (REQ-143).

- `SessionManager` folds it and **already writes it into the junction log** on the `turn_end` record — `record.usage`, `record.requests`, `record.cost_usd` — and `manager.usage(sessionId)` reads it back, including a delegated worker's `attributed` spend (REQ-148 §6).

- 1stcontact's junction is `memoryJunctions()` (REQ-103's Cloudflare packaging). **It dies with the isolate.** The `ArchiveSyncer` persists the transcript to the chat ticket and carries no `usage` with it — confirmed: neither the `chat_transcript` nor the `tool_transcript` comment on CHAT-3 contains a single token count.

- Nothing in this repository calls `manager.usage()`, and `streamPrompt` reads the terminal event for `outcome` and discards the rest of `meta`.

So the per-turn numbers exist, in memory, for the life of an isolate, and are then dropped. **Nothing needs to be built upstream.** This is entirely a 1stcontact gap, and it is a write and a read.

### Provider portability is a design constraint, not a later migration

Open-weights inference is roughly 7× cheaper and not yet at the level this product needs for judgement — but the gap is closing faster than we will reach scale, so **nothing built here may assume Anthropic.** Three consequences, all cheap now and expensive later:

- **The counter vocabulary is already neutral.** `usage.js` uses the Anthropic names deliberately — _"the four questions worth asking of any cached endpoint"_ — and the ChatGPT adapter already reports into them, with `cache_creation_input_tokens` pinned at zero because that provider caches automatically and never bills a write. So a record holding those four counters is portable by construction. Do not add a fifth.

- **The price table is keyed by **`(backend, model)`**, never by model alone**, and it is configuration beside `backends.json` — the seam BUG-49 built.

- **Store raw counters _and_ settled cost.** Raw so a period can be re-priced against another provider; settled so a bill does not move when the table does.

One honest limit to record now: **token counts are not portable across tokenizers.** Re-pricing a stored month against an open-weights model gives an estimate, not an answer. A true comparison needs the same workload replayed through the other backend. That replay harness is real work and is **explicitly not** in these two tickets — but the records below are what make it possible, so they must keep enough per-turn shape to drive one.

### REQ-292 — the turn spend record

**Behaviour.** Every turn that reports spend leaves one durable record, per tenant, readable after the isolate that produced it is gone.

- `streamPrompt` in `host-core.ts` already reads the terminal event for `outcome`; it folds `turnSpend(event.meta)` beside it and writes the record in the `finally` that already calls `closePending`.

- The write goes through a **port** — `deps.recordTurnSpend(record)` — following `LedgerDeps`: the Worker implements it against D1, the `1c` CLI implements it against a file or declines, and neither learns the other's storage.

- The record holds: tenant, session, turn id, `started_at` / `ended_at`, role, backend, model, outcome, request count, the four raw counters, `attributed` spend from any delegated worker, and **cost in micros settled at write time** from the price table.

- A turn that measured nothing writes no record — the framework's own "nothing, never zero" rule, because a spend of zero and an unmeasured turn are different facts and a meter must not confuse them.

**Acceptance must include the thing nobody has seen.** `cache_read_input_tokens` non-zero, and growing with the conversation, across two consecutive turns. Every figure in this epic assumes the caching works; that assumption has never been tested against the wire.

**Not in scope:** any enforcement, any UI, engaged time.

#### Where the record goes: a new D1 table

`turn_spend`**, in D1, by migration **`0019_turn_spend.sql` — the next number after `0018_activity_log.sql`.

The handle is not currently reachable from the AI host: `WorkerAiEnv` declares `SITES: R2Bucket` and the API key and nothing else. So the D1 binding is **assembled by **`router.ts`** and passed in as a parameter**, and `null` is an ordinary state — the same shape `fidelity` and `images` already have on `workerHost`, and for the same stated reason. A deployment with no meter still opens the session and still takes the turn.

The write itself lands in a path already held open: `streamTurn` wraps the turn's `finally` in `ctx.waitUntil` (BUG-46), so the record survives the response closing without a new lifecycle mechanism and without delaying the last frame the client sees.

The four alternatives, and why not:

- `log_records`**.** Tempting — it exists, it already has `business`, `duration_ms`, `outcome` and a JSON `data` column, and it needs no migration. Disqualified: `pruneRecords()` DELETEs it on a band and `log_floor` records how far that has reached. **A meter you bill from must not be deletable by an ops job.** Nothing has been pruned yet — 6,165 rows, floor at 0 — but the mechanism exists and is meant to run.

- **R2, beside the audit** (`spend/<tenant>/<session>/<turn>.json`). Durable, and the flush path already exists. Disqualified on the read: a month's report becomes a prefix listing and thousands of GETs. The audit gets away with that because nothing reads it routinely; a meter is read on every dashboard load and every billing run.

- `counters` `(tenant_id, type, value)`. An aggregate only — it cannot say which turns, cannot split by model or role, and cannot be re-priced, which is the whole of the portability argument. It is the right _second_ structure: a cap check must not scan a month of rows. **Rows for truth, counters for speed** — but the rollup arrives with the cap, which is not this ticket.

- **The chat ticket, via the ledger surface.** Wrong on principle, by `ledger-core.ts`'s own argument: the ledger is _the engagement's_ record and spend is not part of the engagement. It would also put the client's bill in a document the consultant can read and the knowledge base indexes.

**Retention is keep, not prune.** The raw counters are the asset that answers the open-weights question a year from now; deleting them forecloses it. Rows roll into a period aggregate when they age out of usefulness — named here so it is a decision rather than an omission, and built when there is enough data for it to matter.

### REQ-293 — reading it back, in hours and in dollars

**Behaviour.** A tenant's spend for a period, in both units, from the records REQ (1) writes.

- **Engaged time is computed at read, not stored.** A turn's share is its own duration plus the gap to the _next_ turn capped at five minutes — and the next turn does not exist when the first one ends. Storing it would mean a retroactive update on every turn; deriving it from two timestamps costs nothing and cannot drift.

- Five minutes is chosen from measurement, not taste: the median inter-turn gap on 09-20 was 4.2 minutes, so two minutes stops the clock during ordinary thinking and ten bills multitasking as consulting.

- The report answers, per tenant per period: engaged hours, settled cost, cost per engaged hour, and the same three split by role and by model — the split is what makes a delegation experiment readable, and what makes a second provider comparable.

**Not in scope:** the cap. Hours are the right meter and the wrong cap — cost per engaged hour varied twenty-fold in one evening — so enforcement needs a dollar ceiling underneath the hours, and neither the plan nor the ceiling is decided. It is a separate ticket once a week of real records exists to set it from.

### Sequence, and what gates what

1. **REQ-292**, alone, then **one week of ordinary sessions**.

2. Re-read the Measurement section above against records rather than against a model. The lever ranking there is a prediction, and the predictions in this epic have been wrong by 4.5× and 2.4× in opposite directions.

3. **REQ-293** can be built in parallel with that week — it only needs the record's shape, not its contents.

4. Only then the levers, and only in the order measurement puts them.

No BUG is filed yet. If REQ-292 shows `cache_read_input_tokens` at zero across turns, that is one, and it outranks everything else in this epic.

## Four open questions, checked against the installed code

### Principal and delegate spend stay separate — yes, by construction

`manager.usage(sessionId)` returns `{usage, turns, attributed?}`. `usage` folds **the caller's own turns only**; `attributed` is a separate list, one entry per delegation, each `{session, role, backend, usage, requests?, cost_usd?}`. The worker's session id, role and backend all travel with its spend, so REQ-293's split by model and by role works on delegated work without a second mechanism.

**One footgun to pin in acceptance:** the caller's true total is `usage + sum(attributed)`, and a reader taking `usage` alone under-reports every delegating turn. That is the shape most likely to be got wrong, and it fails silently and in the flattering direction. REQ-292 and REQ-293 must each state which of the two they mean, everywhere they report a number.

### Exposing cost to the principal — the mechanism already exists, unwired

Two halves, and the first is already done: the `delegate` result returns the worker's `usage` to the caller, so a consultant is told what each delegation cost the moment it completes.

The second half — what the session has spent _in total_ — has a precedent to copy rather than a mechanism to invent. REQ-169 built `budgetProvider` (`session.budget`), which renders occupancy as `used / window / percent / remaining` and **pushes it into the seed every turn**, on the REQ-131 argument that a fact the model must not miss is delivered rather than left to a tool call it may skip. A spend or hours gauge is the same provider shape with different numbers.

Three schemes, cheapest first:

1. **A gauge in the seed.** One line per turn: hours used, hours remaining, percent. Costs a few tokens per turn, no new surface, no tool. The model sees it whether or not it thinks to look.

2. **A tool the model may call.** Cheaper per turn, and worse: the turns it would skip the call on are the long ones, which are exactly the turns where the number matters. REQ-283 made this mistake once already with a write-only ledger and corrected it by delivering the record in the seed.

3. **Nothing to the model, enforcement at the host.** Simplest and most brittle: the session is cut off mid-thought with no chance to land the work tidily.

**Scheme 1, with a caveat worth stating now:** the gauge changes every turn, so it must ride in the per-turn tail past the message history (REQ-144), never in the cached prefix. A budget line written into `system` would invalidate the whole prefix on every turn — the exact defect REQ-144 exists to have fixed.

### Does the context ever fill up? No, we do not ensure that

Checked, and the honest answer is that three of the four things needed are missing:

- **A single exchange is deliberately unbounded.** `recentExchanges` keeps _at least one whole exchange, whatever its size_ — cutting inside one would orphan a `tool_result` and the API rejects that. So the 40-exchange window bounds growth **across** turns and declines to bound a turn. The measured Lagrange Foundry session had a turn with 55 tool calls; nothing would have stopped it at 200.

- **Nothing compacts on the API path.** `_compactionDue` returns false unless the backend declares the `compaction` capability, and only the Claude Code CLI adapter does. The API backends hold no conversation of their own to compact.

- `contextWindow`** is not configured.** `backends.json` names `model` and `max_tokens` and nothing else, so the manager reads the window as 0 and cannot compute fullness even if asked.

- **The gauge is not wired.** `session.budget` is not among this project's priming entries, so neither the model nor the host can see it coming.

What _does_ exist, and is genuinely the right shape:

- **A summary maintained as we go** — the ledger's standing note and its decision entries (REQ-283), delivered back into the seed every turn through the `memory.trigger` reminder. `record_decision` and `set_standing_note` were called 57 times in the measured session, so it is in use, not merely present.

- **Chunk-based search over past conversation** — chat tickets are indexed in the knowledge base and `KnowledgeChunkSearch` was called 25 times in that session.

- **A pointer to the session's own tool transcript** — `TOOL_TRANSCRIPT_NOTE_PROVIDER` exists upstream and is **not wired here**, so the model cannot address by turn id the one record that holds what its tools actually returned.

So the design the question describes is largely built. The gaps are narrow and nameable: configure `contextWindow`, wire the occupancy gauge, wire the tool-transcript pointer, and decide what should happen to a single turn that runs long. None is filed yet.

### Caps — discussed, not built

Deferred deliberately. Recording the shape while it is fresh:

- **Meter in hours, cap in dollars.** Cost per engaged hour varied twentyfold in one evening, so an hours-only cap lets one client burn a month in an afternoon while another talks for the same hours and costs nothing.

- **The check must not scan a month of rows.** `counters` `(tenant_id, type, value)` is the right hot-path structure beside `turn_spend` — rows for truth, counters for speed.

- **The AI should see the limit, by scheme 1 above**, so it can land the work tidily rather than be cut off mid-thought — and so it has a reason to delegate that it can actually perceive, which is the incentive REQ-295's additive design currently lacks.

- **Decide what happens at the limit**: degrade, refuse, or charge overage. Not answerable before a week of REQ-292 records exists to set a number from.

REQ-296 is urgent and sequences **ahead of** REQ-295: a `claude-haiku-4-5` worker has a 200k window against Opus's 1M, and the measured build already reached ~300k resident. Opening workers before the guard exists is opening sessions that overflow.

REQ-297 renders REQ-293 and computes nothing itself. Its gate is `ownsPlatformBusiness`, **not** `platform_operator` — `identity.ts` forbids gating any route on the latter in terms, and a console that appeared "because you are an admin" would be the failure mode DOC-40 §2.1 rule 1 names.

## Benchmarking a cheaper model — design notes

Open-weights inference is roughly 7× cheaper and improving faster than we will
reach scale, so the question is not whether to try one but how to know what it
costs us in quality. The proposal — fixed materials, a test business, a run per
model, judged on the site, the conversation and the cost — is the right shape.
Three changes and one addition.

### Canned prompts would measure the wrong thing

A consulting session is adaptive: turn N+1 depends on what the assistant said in
turn N. A fixed prompt list ignores that. A model that asks a good clarifying
question gets punished — its question goes unanswered and the next canned prompt
barrels past it — while a model that charges ahead without asking scores well.
That biases systematically **against consultative behaviour**, which is the
product.

Fix the **information**, not the text. A package carries a client script: the
business facts, the preferences, the things this client cares about and the
things they will not budge on, plus a persona. It is revealed **on demand** —
when asked, answer from the script; never volunteer beyond it; when the script
is silent, say so the way that client would.

Played either by a person for a small number of gold runs, or by a **client
simulator** on a cheap model for volume. The simulator's tokens are harness
cost and must be excluded from the measured spend, or a chatty assistant looks
expensive twice.

### Start with the benchmark that needs no judge

Before any of that: **tool discipline**. Can the model drive a 60-operation
surface without malformed arguments, schema violations, loops, or giving up
after an error?

We already have the baseline and it cost nothing: the audit ledger records
**110 failed tool calls out of 1,100** across the measured period — 10% on
`claude-opus-5`, and 25% on one session. A weaker model's failure rate against
the same surface is the best early predictor there is, it needs no judge, no
rubric and no client simulator, and the ledger records it for free.

If a candidate cannot keep JSON valid against this surface, nothing downstream
matters and we have found out for the price of one session.

### "Quality of the website" has to be split, or it cannot be scored

**Mechanically, for free** — and this is where a weaker model's regressions will
actually show: does it build, does it publish, are there dangling references,
does the capture succeed, is every page non-empty, is the palette used
consistently, and what do the fidelity ladder's semantic deltas say (reachable
only through `1c gate --size`, not from `capture.json`).

**By judgement, expensively**: is the copy on-brand, is the hierarchy sensible,
would a client accept this.

The rubric is not ours to invent — DOC-46, DOC-35 and DOC-49 already state what
good looks like in this product's own words. The judge scores against those.

### Judging discipline

- A judge from a **different model family** than anything being judged.
- **Blind, with randomised order** — a judge told which model produced which site
  is scoring the label.
- The judge model **pinned for the life of the benchmark**, or scores drift
  between runs for reasons that have nothing to do with the thing being tested.

### The matrix, not the binary

The useful result is not "all Opus against all open-weights". It is **consultant
model × worker model**. An open-weights *worker* under an Opus consultant is a
far safer first step than an open-weights consultant, and it captures most of
the saving, because construction was the bulk of the spend. Two by two is the
minimum worth running.

### Runs, not a run

One session per model tells us almost nothing — these systems are not
deterministic and two live conversations diverge by the second turn. N runs per
package per configuration, and **cost reported with its variance** rather than
as a single figure. The Opus baseline runs are the expensive part of the
exercise; the candidates are cheap by definition.

### What it is also for

A benchmark run once is a report; run on every change it is an asset. The same
harness makes our **own prompt changes falsifiable** — today, editing the
priming prose has no way to be shown better or worse, which is an uncomfortable
thing to say about the file that decides what the product is.

### Two practical notes

- **Isolation has a precedent.** `gutter.ts` already runs marked synthetic
  traffic against a horizon, joined to nothing, torn down after. A benchmark
  tenant wants exactly that property and should follow it rather than invent a
  second one.
- **It depends on REQ-292.** Comparing models on cost before the meter lands
  means comparing them with the instrument that was wrong by 4.5× and 2.4×.

### Sequence

1. The tool-discipline probe. Cheap, no judge, no harness, highest signal.
2. The packages and the client script, on the Opus baseline, to establish what
   the scores of a *good* run look like.
3. The matrix, once REQ-292 is reporting cost.

## Delegation: verified in place, 2026-09-24

Traced end to end against the source in `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/`, not
against REQ-295's claims. It is wired, and **the switch ships on** —
`delegation.json` carries `"enabled": true`. That reverses the default the
ticket was filed with, and the document states both reasons: REQ-296's guard
discharged the overflow objection outright, and the wait-for-a-baseline
objection was overruled on the grounds that a week spent measuring what
construction costs is a week of paying it. The switch remains the rollback.

### What is actually there

| Piece | Where |
|---|---|
| The switch, validated at start-up | `delegation.json` + `delegation.ts` |
| Worker model — `claude-haiku-4-5`, 32000 ceiling | `backends.json` -> `claude_builder` |
| Builder's narrowed grant | `instances.json` -> `builder` |
| Consultant's grant, unchanged and additive | `instances.json` -> `consultant` |
| Runtime, worker toolbox, adapter closure | `host-core.ts` ~1253-1355 |
| Builder's own role prose | `priming.json` -> `builder_priming` |
| Consultant's method prose | `priming.json` -> `templates/delegation-method` |
| Context guard on the worker's smaller window | `budget-core.ts` (REQ-296) |
| UATs | `tests/test_UAT_FC_REQ-295_delegation{,_config}.test.ts` |

Three properties worth recording because they are the ones that could have been
got wrong and were not:

- **The consultant lost nothing.** The delegation surface composes as an
  `extraSurfaces` entry beside `l1` and `fidelity`; it removes no group. Option
  (a) as filed — delegating is a decision per piece of work, not a capability
  the consultant traded away. The narrower design stays in reserve.
- **Off means never composed**, not composed-and-refusing. With the switch off
  no worker role is registered, no second backend is built, the manual never
  mentions `delegate`, and `delegationMethod()` returns `null` so the method
  entry and its separator drop out. The prompt is then byte-for-byte what this
  repository sent before delegation existed.
- **The worker gets its own L1 instance and camera**, never the consultant's,
  because a Toolbox binds each surface to the grant it was built with — sharing
  one would re-bind the consultant's L1 surface to the worker's narrower grant
  and silently take capabilities off the conversation. It composes the L1
  surface, the camera and the manual and deliberately nothing else: no corpus,
  no ledger, no catalogue — those are the engagement's record, and a worker
  writing to them would be a second author on it.

### The guidance the primary actually gets

`templates/delegation-method`, rendered into the consultant's priming as the
`delegation` entry — third, and **before the cache boundary**, so it is in the
cached prefix and costs nothing per turn. It tells the consultant four things:

1. **What to hand over** — work where the payload is large and the decisions are
   small. Keep judgement. The test given is operational rather than abstract:
   *if you would have to explain the reasoning twice to hand it over, do it
   yourself.*
2. **How to write the brief** — for someone who cannot see the conversation,
   because they cannot; and name the constraints you would have applied
   silently, since they are not obvious to anyone who was not here.
3. **What to put in `accept`** — whatever you would have checked afterwards, and
   specific enough that "passed" means something: not "the page looks right" but
   "every section has a heading".
4. **What to do with the result** — believe a passed check and move on; look
   again only where one failed or came back unanswered. Stated with the reason,
   which is the load-bearing part: re-inspecting checked work does not save the
   tokens, it moves them to the more expensive side, which is the whole point of
   handing over.

The builder's half is primed independently (`builder_priming`) and carries the
matching obligation: answer every check in the words it was given, from what it
actually looked at, and never claim a check it skipped — *"the one failure that
costs more than doing nothing at all: the consultant stops looking precisely
where they should have looked."*

### The gap this leaves, unchanged from when option (a) was chosen

**Nothing gives the consultant a per-turn reason to delegate.** The method prose
sits in the cached prefix and is read once; there is no reminder in the per-turn
tail, and no cost signal it can perceive. The `delegate` result returns the
worker's `usage`, so it learns what a delegation cost *after* choosing to make
one — never what its own turn is costing, which is the number that would create
the incentive. The model does not see the bill.

So the entire lever currently rests on that prose being persuasive, and it could
deliver 0%. That was accepted knowingly when option (a) was chosen over the
narrowed grant, on the grounds that it is *measurable*: REQ-293's split-by-model
report says what fraction of spend actually moved. Two things follow, and
neither is filed:

- **Read REQ-293's split as the first question after a week of real sessions.**
  A low delegated share means the prose is the lever and the narrowed grant
  (option (b)) is the fallback — held in reserve in REQ-295's body.
- **A per-turn budget gauge is the cheaper intervention**, and REQ-296 already
  built the machinery: `registerBudgetProvider` is wired and the occupancy
  figure exists. Surfacing spend alongside it would give the consultant a reason
  to delegate that it can *perceive* rather than merely have been told once.
  That is the same mechanism a usage cap would need, so the two should be
  designed together rather than separately.

### Defect found: half of all delegations are invisible to the meter

**Four of the eight worker sessions appear in no `turn_spend.attributed` list at
all.** The rule is exact, and it is not about delegation:

> A delegation is metered **if and only if its enclosing turn wrote a spend
> row.** Where the turn wrote one, the delegation is attributed correctly — 4 of
> 4. Where the turn wrote none, the delegation is lost — 0 of 4.

The mechanism is `turnSpendRecord` in `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/spend-core.ts`:
`if (!measured(usage)) return null`. The **caller's own** usage gates the whole
row, and `attributed` is a field of that row, so it goes down with it. A turn
that dies before its terminal meta arrives — an error, an abandoned stream —
therefore discards the record of a worker that ran to completion and cost real
money. REQ-295's accounting criterion holds for every *delegation* exit path
(`reported`, `stopped`, `silent`, `failed`) and does not hold for the *caller's*.

Today's instance: the 18:49 turn ended
`D1_ERROR: string or blob too big: SQLITE_TOOBIG`, so its 70-call Haiku worker is
unmetered. Comparable workers on 09-22 priced at $0.14–$0.57 each, so the
absolute loss is small — the damage is to the instrument, not the bill.

**Why it matters more than its size.** REQ-293's split-by-model report is the
designated answer to "did delegation move any work", and it under-counts
delegated spend by half on the record to date. The one number this epic intends
to steer by is measured by an instrument that drops half its subject.

Not filed. The fix shape is small and has a decision in it: write the row
whenever *either* the caller's usage or `attributed` is present, which means
`turn_spend` would gain rows whose four counters are absent but whose
`attributed` is not — a deliberate widening of *nothing, never zero*, since such
a row says "this turn caused measured spend elsewhere and none of its own was
observed", which is a fact rather than a zero.

### Not a defect: the `SQLITE_TOOBIG` that killed that turn

Already diagnosed and already fixed as **REQ-309** — the `tool_transcript`
comment reached 2,162,212 bytes and every archive write after it failed. The
evidence confirms the fix landed between the two sittings: the frozen segment
stops at `2026-09-22T23:00:09`, and a fresh segment was created at
`2026-09-25T02:06:52`, after which all six turns recorded normally. The 18:49
turn was the last casualty, not a new occurrence.

### What this section does not establish

Production. All of the above is the local `wrangler dev` store, so the turn-level
figures carry the warm-isolate caveat that has dogged every estimate in this

construction.

### Options, ranked (operator asked; none filed)

1. **Take the bulk construction tools off the consultant** — option (b), already
   held in reserve in REQ-295's body. The only option whose yield does not depend
   on the model's disposition on the day, which is what the 7-vs-1 variance
   argues for. Carve out by **size rather than judgement**: the consultant keeps
   single-element writes — the delegating turn legitimately made two — and
   anything touching N+ elements must go through a worker. That boundary is
   exactly the one the observed data already draws.
2. **A per-turn reminder in the tail** — the operator's option (1), relocated.
   The current method prose is already imperative and specific; what it lacks is
   *position*. One line in the reminder slot every turn is a different mechanism
   from the same words in a cached prefix that is 186k deep by the end of a
   sitting. Cheap, and it is the missing half of the design as shipped. Note the
   hazard in "unless there is a reason not to": delegating has real local costs
   to the model (write a brief, define checks, trust an unseen report) where
   `set_l1` is certain, so an escape hatch is where the 88% will go — and it
   would go there invisibly.
3. **A comparative cost signal** — the operator's option (2), reframed. Right
   direction, and REQ-296's `registerBudgetProvider` is already wired. But
   *"here is your spend, minimise it"* is gamed by doing less work: the cheapest
   turn skips the screenshot, does not re-read the page and stops verifying —
   precisely the behaviours the method prose spends paragraphs preventing. Expose
   the **ratio, not the total**: not "this turn has cost $4.24" but "a
   construction call of yours costs ~5× a worker's on rate card alone, and yours
   carries a 186k prefix where the worker carries a fresh one". That motivates
   *relocating* work rather than *skipping* it.

**Sequencing.** Fix the metering defect first. Neither lever can be evaluated
with an instrument that loses half its subject, and REQ-293's split is the
designated evaluator.
