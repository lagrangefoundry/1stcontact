## Why this epic exists

A single hour of design work in the builder chat cost **$20** (2026-09-08, site-1stcontact). Claude was costing more than minimum wage to draw one hero segment, and the session felt slow as well as expensive. This epic holds what we learned, what was fixed, and what is still owed.

The whole diagnosis, the arithmetic behind every number below, and the design argument for delegation are in the chat transcript on **CHAT-47** (`chat-5011da11`) — 2026-09-09 to 2026-09-10. Read that for the reasoning; this body is the standing summary.

## What was actually wrong

Not the model. The request shape.

Every request is one prefix — `[tools][system][messages]` — and Anthropic's cache matches it from the start. Two consequences govern everything:

- a change invalidates everything **after** it, never before it;

- therefore anything that changes often must sit as **late** in the prefix as possible.

A tool-using turn is not one request. After every tool result the backend calls the API again carrying the whole conversation. The measured 2026-09-08 session was **15 exchanges but 117 round trips** — one turn alone ran 41 tool calls.

`cache_control` was applied to the **system assembly only**. The message history — including every screenshot — was re-sent uncached on all 117 round trips. Cost was quadratic in turn count. Measured breakdown of the ~70k of accumulated context: screenshots 33%, knowledge search 16%, `get_l1` 14%, `set_l1` 11% — but screenshots were only ~2% of tokens _billed_, because the expense was never their size, it was being re-sent 117 times.

Two aggravating facts found alongside it:

- **No token telemetry.** `usage` on the `message_start` frame was read and discarded, so spend could only be modelled, never observed.

- `max_tokens`** hardcoded at 4096.** A large `set_l1` payload hit the ceiling, the stream ended with no `content_block_stop`, and the tool was dispatched with an **empty input object** — silently. Observed twelve consecutive times in one session (2026-09-09, Lagrange Foundry site), 34% of all tool calls in that session failing, and the model retrying blind each time.

## The fix, and one thing we got wrong first

Caching the message history was the change with the best ratio, and it landed. But the first version only helped **within** a turn, not across turns — because the per-turn reminder (which carries "the site moved under you") sat at the end of `system`, and `system` precedes `messages`. Every turn the reminder changed, it invalidated the entire history behind it. 1stcontact's reminders change on nearly every turn by design.

That gap was ours: the original acceptance criterion said "within a turn" and never mentioned the second _turn_. The follow-up moved the volatile and reminder tiers **past** the history, appended to the final user message at request-build time and never written back into stored history. The principle was generalised, not reversed: stable first, volatile last — applied to the whole request rather than to one segment of it, because the request is the unit the cache matches on. Recorded as **DOC-22 Amendment X**.

## What is live in 1stcontact today

- `claude-opus-5`**, **`max_tokens`** 64000**, set in `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/backends.json` (BUG-67). The model is now this repository's decision, not a framework release — that seam is the part of BUG-49 we asked for.

- Message-history caching and per-request token telemetry are in the installed shared store and active on every session.

- The truncation failure that produced twelve empty `set_l1` calls is fixed on both halves: the ceiling is roomy, and an unfinished tool block is refused rather than dispatched.

Note the consequence of the Opus move: **the front face is now the expensive model.** Opus 5 is $5/$25 against Sonnet's $3/$15, and thinking is on by default. That is the right trade for judgement — what the thing is, what it means, which direction to take — and the wrong one for the mechanical majority of the work, where the payload is large and the decisions are small. Which is what the remaining work is about.

## What is still owed

1. **Re-measure, from telemetry rather than a model.** Every number above for the current configuration is an estimate; REQ-143/145 mean real `cache_read_input_tokens` and per-request spend are now recorded. Confirm cross-turn reads are non-zero and scale with history, and establish a fresh per-session baseline on Opus 5. The CHAT-47 figures predate the Opus move and are no longer comparable.

2. **Effort levels — the cheap lever, still not taken.** The backend sends no `output_config` at all. Effort does not touch the cache prefix, so it is a per-turn dial with no architectural cost: low for mechanical construction turns, high for strategy. Not implemented anywhere in the framework yet. Worth doing **before** delegation, and this epic should not be used to skip it.

3. **Adopt the delegation surface.** REQ-148 shipped and `delegation_surface.json` is installed in the shared store, but 1stcontact grants nothing from it — `instances.json` declares one role, `consultant`, and no worker. Adoption means: a builder role with a narrower grant, a backend configured for that role's workers, `DelegateWork` granted to the consultant and `ReportDelegatedWork` to the builder.

The shape the framework enforces, and which we should not fight:

- the brief is **prose** (intent cannot be enumerated), but authority comes from the **role's declared grant** — no phrasing of a goal widens it;

- the worker's conversation never enters the caller's, so the construction transcript stops being billed at the consultant's rate;

- **no nesting**, and worker spend **rolls up** into the caller's ledger, so this cannot make cost invisible again;

- `accept` checks are the load-bearing part. If Opus has to screenshot and re-inspect everything the worker did, the tokens have been moved to the more expensive side rather than saved.

The likely split, from the existing capability groups: builder keeps ReadSite, AuthorPages, ManageComponents, MeasureDrawings, DrawImages, SeeSite; consultant keeps ManagePages, WriteConfig and ManagePalette — brand colours, site structure and config are the decisions worth Opus.

4. **Choose the worker's model from measurement, not from estimate.** On the 2026-09-08 workload the bulk of spend was construction (21 screenshots, 21 `write_image`, 18 `set_l1`, 15 `get_l1`). Haiku 4.5 at $1/$5 would do that roughly 3× cheaper than Sonnet does today, with the strategic layer on Opus small enough that its rate barely matters. That is an argument for trying, not a sizing — item 1 supplies the sizing.

## Explicitly not the approach

**Per-turn model switching within one conversation** — Opus for strategy turns, Sonnet for construction turns, same session. Prompt caches are keyed per model. Alternating A/B/A/B roughly halves the hit rate and pays the writes twice, undoing most of what REQ-143/144 bought. Delegation exists precisely because each side then keeps its own clean cache lineage.

## Measurement: the Lagrange Foundry build (2026-09-09 → 2026-09-21)

Reconstructed 2026-09-21 from the session's own records — the `tool_transcript`
and `chat_transcript` comments on **CHAT-3** (`chat-50932534`, in the
control-app's ticket store, not the xgd one) and the 768 R2 audit objects under
`audit/biz_5b10…/`. Audit and transcript align call-for-call, 0 mismatches over
758 calls, so counts, order and true result sizes are **measured**.

**The dollar figures are calibrated, not derived.** Two observations from the
operator's account pin them: **$166.50 of API spend across 2026-09-01 → 09-21**,
and **at least $75 on 09-20 alone** (seven $10–11 top-ups). A first pass
modelled $252 for the build and a second $55; neither survived contact with
those numbers. What the calibration fixes is the one thing the records cannot
show — how long the Worker isolate holding `messages[]` survived between turns —
and the answer is **about an hour**, which is what a long-running local
`wrangler dev` process gives you rather than the seconds a production isolate
gets.

**This is why item 1 matters more than anything else in this epic.** REQ-143/145
capture `usage` and put `{usage, requests}` on the turn's terminal `done` event.
**1stcontact persists none of it.** `apps/control-app/src/ai.ts` flushes an
audit record per *tool call* and nothing per *request*;
`cache_read_input_tokens` appears nowhere in this repository or in either
transcript. Two rounds of this analysis were wrong by 4.5× and 2.4× in opposite
directions, and both errors were about an unobservable the API already reports.
One write beside the audit flush ends the guessing.

Screenshots are the cheapest thing in the session. The 34.5 MB is base64 on the
audit's scale; an image bills by dimension, ~460 tokens each. The intuition that
pictures are what costs money is wrong here.

The shape is now clear: **cost is dominated by long tool-using turns replaying
their own accumulated results.** A turn with 55 tool calls is 56 requests, and
the 56th carries everything the first 55 produced. That is the same quadratic
the 09-08 diagnosis found, surviving the caching fix because a cache read still
costs 10% and 10% of a very large number 56 times is not small.

### The levers, measured

Each line includes the ones above it. They are not additive — each makes the
tokens the next would have saved cheaper.

| | 09-20 | $/hour | whole build |
|---|---|---|---|
| As it ran | **$75** | **$29** | $107 |
| Window 40 → 10 exchanges | $53 | $20 | $81 |
| + tool definitions deferred (~13k → ~2k tokens/request) | $50 | $19 | $74 |
| + cache TTL 1h → 5min (write 1.25× not 2×) — **config total** | **$39** | **$15** | $61 |
| + a window trim that preserves the cache prefix (framework code) | $30 | $11 | $50 |
| + low effort on construction turns | $25 | $10 | $42 |
| + construction delegated to Haiku 4.5 — **all in** | **$12** | **$4** | $19 |

The first three are settings. They roughly **halve** the cost and need no new
code in either repository.

Two notes on the table. **Window 20 is worse than window 40** (~$111): a smaller
window slides more often, and every slide drops the oldest exchange off the
*front* of `messages[]`, which invalidates the byte-prefix the cache matches on.
Only at 10 does the smaller payload outrun the extra invalidation. And the
fourth line is the same effect attacked directly — `boundDialogue`'s front-drop
and `ageImages`' in-place redaction each rewrite the prefix, and at the observed
isolate lifetime that is worth ~$9 on 09-20.

### Cost per hour

Charging the operator's turns at a typing rate rather than at the wall-clock gap
— the session was multitasked, and the gaps are not work. On 09-20: 1.58 h of
measured machine time plus 1.03 h of typing at 40 wpm = **2.60 h engaged**.
Whole build: 3.18 h machine + 2.18 h typing = 5.36 h.

**$29/hour as it ran; $15/hour on settings alone; $4/hour with delegation.**
Against the $20/hour measured on 2026-09-08, the caching work already landed —
09-20 was a far heavier session per hour than that one — and the remaining
headroom is large and mostly cheap.

### What this changes about the path forward

1. **Persist the turn's `usage` at the host.** One write beside the audit flush
   in `ai.ts`. Nothing else here can be confirmed without it.
2. **Dial the window down to 10** — REQ-126's own stated experiment, never run,
   the largest single configuration lever at ~29%.
3. **Defer the tool definitions.** ~13k tokens of schema on every round trip for
   a grant of ~60 operations, most unused in any turn.
4. **Reconsider the 1h TTL.** Chosen for sessions paced by a person thinking;
   with a prefix invalidated by the window and by image ageing far more often
   than by an idle gap, the doubled write is paid many more times than the long
   lifetime is used.
5. **Make the window trim and image ageing cache-preserving** (framework). Trim
   at a breakpoint boundary, or in blocks, rather than one exchange per turn.
6. Effort levels and delegation are unchanged and still correct. Delegation is
   the largest single lever — over half — and it stacks with everything above.
