## Why (Ariel's AR Welding run, 2026-10-21; operator)

The talker (REQ-416) is where conversation strategy belongs — it isn't running
builds; it's talking to the client. But its standing job is "discovery" with a
list of story areas still missing, so with nothing else to steer it, it keeps
asking for more story. In this run, seven consecutive questions drilled into the
Saturday kids' classes (free or paid, what they take home, at what age they move
up from glue, how many per class, the three regulars, safety gear, who teaches)
— one asked twice — while the positioning, the headline and the reactions on the
story board stayed open.

> It feels a little random right now, like it is just trying to get the user to
> talk rather than helping gather material that is likely to make it to the site.
> The agent should do more than keep the client talking … keeping them occupied
> until the build is finished is good, but it would be even better if it didn't
> feel like we were burning tokens and killing time. I think it would be helpful
> if the purpose of the question were made clear (it would also help keep the
> talk on point). (operator)

Small talk and rapport stay valuable (the best material often comes sideways);
the change is that talk serves the site.

## What changes

1. **Every question says what it's for, in the client's terms.** e.g. "For the
   classes page: how many kids do you take on a Saturday?", "So the top of your
   page answers what a contractor cares about: what do they say when they
   recommend you?". If the talker can't name where the answer goes (a section, a
   page, a decision), it doesn't ask. The client can then say "not needed" and the
   conversation moves on.
2. **The talker's facts are decisions and sections, not story areas.** Its
   per-exchange notes list the open decisions and the sections being written, with
   what each still needs (from the plan: positioning card, headline, sections on
   the pages, functions), plus the due list (reaction task, positioning card,
   critique) and readiness per area (REQ-425). This replaces REQ-416's "story
   areas still missing" line.
3. **Enough is enough.** After two or three questions on one topic, the talker
   checks the value: "I've got what the classes section needs — want to add more,
   or shall we look at the headline?" When a decision or section has enough, it
   changes gear: run a focus task (REQ-424), draft the positioning card, propose
   headline options — all plan writes the talker may already make.
4. **Priority follows value.** The talker's first move (including BUG-240's cued
   opening at a build start) is the most valuable open decision for the site's
   purpose — usually positioning and proof before side topics.
5. **One warm follow-up is fine.** When the client clearly cares about something,
   one follow-up for rapport, then back to the open decision — with the purpose
   stated.
6. **No repeated questions.** The talker checks the conversation and the plan
   before asking; a question already asked and unanswered is re-pointed to, not
   re-asked.
7. **The talker updates readiness** as it reads the client (REQ-425), so the pace
   adjusts mid-stage.

## Test plan
- Talker priming carries the purpose-stated question rule, enough-is-enough,
  priority-by-value and no-repeat rules.
- The talker's per-exchange facts list open decisions/sections with what each
  needs, and the due list; the "story areas missing" line is gone.
- A scripted talker turn on a topic already asked three times proposes moving on
  (scripted model asserting the facts it was given name the decision).
