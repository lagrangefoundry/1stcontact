## Why (Ariel's AR Welding run, 2026-10-21; operator)

The talker (REQ-416) is where conversation strategy belongs — it isn't running
builds; it's talking to the client. But its standing job is "discovery" with a
list of story areas still missing, so with nothing else to steer it, it keeps
asking for more story. In this run, seven consecutive questions drilled into the
Saturday kids' classes (free or paid, what they take home, at what age they move
up from glue, how many per class, the three regulars, safety gear, who teaches)
— one asked twice — while the positioning, the headline and the reactions on the
story board stayed open.

> It feels a little random right now, like it is just trying to get the user to
> talk rather than helping gather material that is likely to make it to the site.
> The agent should do more than keep the client talking … keeping them occupied
> until the build is finished is good, but it would be even better if it didn't
> feel like we were burning tokens and killing time. I think it would be helpful
> if the purpose of the question were made clear (it would also help keep the
> talk on point). (operator)

Small talk and rapport stay valuable (the best material often comes sideways);
the change is that talk serves the site.

## What changes

1. **Every question says what it's for, in the client's terms.** e.g. "For the
   classes page: how many kids do you take on a Saturday?", "So the top of your
   page answers what a contractor cares about: what do they say when they
   recommend you?". If the talker can't name where the answer goes (a section, a
   page, a decision), it doesn't ask. The client can then say "not needed" and the
   conversation moves on.
2. **The talker's facts are decisions and sections, not story areas.** Its
   per-exchange notes list the open decisions and the sections being written, with
   what each still needs (from the plan: positioning card, headline, sections on
   the pages, functions), plus the due list (reaction task, positioning card,
   critique) and readiness per area (REQ-425). This replaces REQ-416's "story
   areas still missing" line.
3. **Enough is enough.** After two or three questions on one topic, the talker
   checks the value: "I've got what the classes section needs — want to add more,
   or shall we look at the headline?" When a decision or section has enough, it
   changes gear: run a focus task (REQ-424), draft the positioning card, propose
   headline options — all plan writes the talker may already make.
4. **Priority follows value.** The talker's first move (including BUG-240's cued
   opening at a build start) is the most valuable open decision for the site's
   purpose — usually positioning and proof before side topics.
5. **One warm follow-up is fine.** When the client clearly cares about something,
   one follow-up for rapport, then back to the open decision — with the purpose
   stated.
6. **No repeated questions.** The talker checks the conversation and the plan
   before asking; a question already asked and unanswered is re-pointed to, not
   re-asked.
7. **The talker updates readiness** as it reads the client (REQ-425), so the pace
   adjusts mid-stage.

## Correction and the real ask (operator, 2026-10-21)

> In fairness the AI is trying to gather material to populate the kids classes page that I asked for — but I would prefer a more strategic and collaborative approach rather than a constant stream of questions.

The questions had a purpose (the classes page the client asked for); the problem is the **mode**: interrogation, one fact at a time, with nothing visible being built. The collaborative pattern instead:

- **Draft first, with gaps marked.** For a page or section, the talker shows what it plans and drafts it from what it already knows, with the gaps marked — e.g. "For the classes page I'd cover: what the class is, ages, price, what kids make, safety, who teaches, how to sign up. Here's a first version from what you've told me; the [?] bits are what I don't know yet." The client fills in, corrects or says "skip" in one go.
- **Facts go to the panel in one batch.** Short factual gaps (price, ages, class size, what to bring) become a small panel focus task (REQ-424) instead of a chat drip; the chat keeps the conversation for what needs it (the story, the why).
- **Each exchange visibly moves the site forward**: the client sees the draft section improve, not just answers going in.
- This applies alongside the purpose rule (item 1): the draft states the purpose by itself.

Test: priming carries the draft-with-gaps pattern and "facts to the panel as a batch"; a scripted talker asked to fill a new section with five unknown facts adds them as panel asks and replies with a draft, not five chat questions.


- Priming half landed as an untracked prompt change so it can be tried now: "Working on a page together" (draft with [?] gaps, facts to the panel as one batch, purpose-stated questions, stop after two or three on a topic). The rest of this ticket (talker facts as open decisions, no-repeat, priority by value, the scripted test) is still to build.


## Implementation scope (free-coded, 2026-10-21)

Built against the plan as it stands today. REQ-425 (positioning card, readiness) is still draft, so the positioning-card and readiness lines (item 2's "readiness per area", item 7) are REQ-425's to add to the talker's notes when it lands; nothing here depends on it.

- **The talker's per-exchange notes become an agenda** (`talkerDiscovery` in `roles.ts`, agenda lines from a new `talkerAgenda` in `plan-core.ts`). REQ-416's "Still missing from the story board: …" line, its "every part of their story has a note" fallback and the "when the story is covered, point them at the panel" line are gone (and `missingStoryAreas` with them). Instead the talker is told:
  - **Open decisions, most valuable first, each with what it still needs**: the plan's decisions not yet settled (open, defaulted, or proposed and awaiting the client), only those talk can settle (settled by asking or talking, not by showing looks), ranked concept before detail and messaging/purpose before functionality, structure and imagery; at most five. What each needs is said in the client-facing sense: "a straight answer", "talking it through", "their yes on what was proposed", "a real choice (it was only defaulted)".
  - **What is being built or comes next** (the plan's tasks doing / next): the sections to draft with [?] gaps.
  - **Due**: what "Getting to know you" still needs from the story board (reactions, a heart, a headline offered), due checks, and what is waiting for a focus task (open panel questions, new notes with no reaction).
  - **Already asked, waiting on the panel** (the open asks' prompts): point to these, never ask them again.
- **The talker's job text is rewritten** (`talker-discovery` template) from "discovery … ask one question at a time and follow each answer up" to the conversation-strategist rules: move the site forward; every question says what it is for in the client's terms, and a question that can't name where its answer goes isn't asked; draft first with [?] gaps and put short facts on the panel as one batch (set_ask, then start_focus); after two or three questions on one topic check the value and change gear (focus task, offer_headlines, propose a decision); one warm follow-up when the client clearly cares, then back to the open decision; check the conversation and the panel before asking and re-point to a question already asked instead of re-asking; the first move is the most valuable open decision.
- **BUG-240's cued opening** (`talker-cue`): "ask one question about their story" becomes "move the most valuable open decision in your notes forward, saying what it is for".
- The consultant's priming section "Working on a page together" (commit 9077897094, prompt-only) already carries draft-with-gaps / facts-to-the-panel for both the consultant and the talker.
