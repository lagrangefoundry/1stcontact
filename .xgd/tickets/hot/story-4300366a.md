## Story

**As a** site operator working in the builder,
**I want** one popup that shows every colour my site has, how much each one is used, and lets me
choose one or fix one on the spot,
**so that** picking a colour and correcting a nearly-right colour are the same gesture, and I never
have to leave the page I am looking at to do either.

## Description

The site's palette gets a face: **one surface, opened from two kinds of place and identical in
both.**

- **To manage** — a **Colors** control in the builder's toolbar. Nobody is waiting for a value; the
  operator is here to change the palette. It is offered in both the viewing and the editing channel,
  because a palette is a property of the site rather than of one rendering of it.
- **To pick** — a colour field somewhere that needs a value. The surface additionally resolves to a
  selection and hands it back to whoever opened it.

Nothing else differs between the two. Picking and editing live together deliberately: *"this colour
is nearly right"* is then a one-gesture fix rather than a hunt for a different screen. It is not a
third channel or a workspace mode — a mode cannot be opened by a dialog that is waiting for an
answer, and further openers (an inline swatch, a right-click) are further callers of the same
component rather than further copies of it.

**What it shows.** One swatch per entry, labelled with the entry's name and how many places
reference it, an entry nothing references reported at zero rather than omitted. The count is the
single most useful fact on the surface: *"primary, used 45 times"* is what makes an edit
predictable, and it is the sentence both the removal rule and the rename confirmation are stated in.
A site whose palette is empty is a legitimate state — two of the stored sites have no palette at all
because their colours are still literals — so it reads as *"no colours yet, add one"* with the add
control present, never as a fault.

**Position within a colour's family.** Selecting an entry reveals a continuous light↔dark control
over the entry's whole family, with a live preview of the colour at the current position. It is
continuous rather than a handful of stops, which matches "a linear spectrum" and is the simpler
control; the cost is accepted and recorded — two elements cannot reliably be matched to the same
position by eye, so *"make these two the same"* is a request to the assistant rather than a slider
skill. **The preview is the renderer's own arithmetic, not a second opinion about it**: the colour
under the control is the colour the page will paint. In manage mode the control writes nothing — a
position belongs to a *use*, not to an entry — but it is still shown, because an operator choosing a
colour is choosing its whole family and this is the only place to see what its ends look like.

**What a pick resolves to.** A palette *reference* — the entry's name, plus the position when the
position is not the colour itself — and never a typed colour. The opener writes it into whatever it
owns; this surface never touches a page, which is the boundary with the segment editor. A position
at the colour itself is omitted rather than sent as zero: the two resolve identically, but an absent
position is the reference a literal converts to byte-for-byte. Cancelling resolves to nothing and
changes no state, and every route out of the dialog — the close control, Escape, a click outside —
answers the opener exactly once.

**Typing a colour lives here and nowhere else.** This is the one surface with free colour entry, via
a native colour control paired with a text field that mirrors it: the native control cannot express
every form the site accepts and a text field cannot be dragged, so each mirrors the other and either
may be submitted. From a segment an operator can only pick a colour the site already has, which is
what bounds the incoherence risk.

**Where the rules are: not here.** Every edit is posted to the same guarded write path the command
line and the assistant use, and the surface redraws from the census that write answers with rather
than from its own guess at what changed. A removal control greyed out on an entry in use is an
*explanation* of a rule, never the rule itself; a refusal is shown in the store's own words and
hint, because those say which rule refused and what to do instead. And because a colour change
repaints the page, the page displayed beside the popup is refreshed after a write — a write that
left a stale page on screen would read as a write that did nothing.

### Out of scope

- **The rules themselves and the surface they are enforced at** — the census, the four writes and
  their refusals belong to the palette management story; this story owns only what an operator sees
  and gestures at, and the fact that the surface is not the authority.
- **Writing a picked reference into a page** — that is the colour field's job, in the segment
  editing story that opens this surface to pick.
- **Transparency.** Not offered here: it is an independent axis of a *use* and belongs to a
  different conversation.
- **Deriving a palette from a site's existing literals** — that already exists as its own command
  and is not rebuilt here.

## Technical Context

- **Depends on the palette read/write surface** (plan item 1, `STORY-113`, CAP-98): the census this
  surface labels its swatches with, the closed set of operations it posts, and the server-side
  guards it renders the refusals of are all that story's. This story is that story's browser face.
- **The toolbar contract is `STORY-99`'s** (CAP-85): the workspace re-derives its controls from what
  is displayed, and the Colors control is one more registered action rather than a branch in the
  toolbar.
- **One arithmetic, two consumers.** The continuous control resolves a colour once per frame of a
  drag, so a round trip per frame is not an option; the light↔dark maths therefore lives in a module
  with no runtime imports, which the builder origin serves to the browser type-stripped, exactly as
  it already serves the edit bridge. That is what makes "the preview is the renderer's own
  arithmetic" affordable rather than aspirational — a second implementation in the browser would be
  free to drift, and the drift would show as a control previewing colours the page never produces.
- **The dialog shell is shared with the segment editor.** Backdrop, Escape, close and the
  shell-rooted host were hand-rolled by the segment dialog and are now one module both wear, so the
  two dialogs cannot diverge on how a dialog behaves. A dialog must be hosted inside the workspace
  shell: the theme tokens and the app font are declared there, and a dialog outside it resolves
  neither.
- **A recorded quirk, not repaired here.** The shared shell keeps attachment separate from
  construction, and that ordering is load-bearing: attaching at construction makes the segment
  editor's click-to-edit focus real, whereupon the browser moves focus away and the control reverts
  to a display cell before the operator has typed. That is landed segment-editor behaviour pinned by
  its own criteria; the repair is an upstream seam being asked for separately.
- **Divergence from intent — none of substance.** Three of the intent's decisions were revised
  during implementation and the ticket body records them as decisions rather than omissions: the
  original re-render-on-write criterion was withdrawn (both draft-side channels render at request
  time, so there is nothing to keep in step) and replaced by refreshing the displayed page; the
  position control is shown in manage mode as a family preview that writes nothing; and rename moved
  into scope after its cost was measured. The story documents the revised intent.
- **Evidence gap worth flagging.** The refresh of the displayed page after a write is wired at the
  workspace, where the popup reports a landed write, but the current free-coded evidence drives the
  popup with its own callback and does not assert the refresh end-to-end. The criterion below states
  the behaviour; a test author should expect to reach for the workspace, not the popup alone.
