## Cluster 2 Resolution

**Boundary**: Ownership of the post-write re-render and refresh of the displayed page
**Stories resolved**: 3 (all confirmed in place — no reassignment, no merge, no ticket writes)

### Verdict

The overlap is **apparent, not real**. The seam decomposes into three distinct
acts, each owned by exactly one capability and each carrying its own separate,
non-duplicative evidence. Nothing here is two implementations of one behaviour.

| Act | Owner | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The **definition changes**, whole-or-nothing, validated | CAP-86 / STORY-100 | AC-982, AC-983, AC-986 |
| The **origin produces fresh bytes on request** — draft-side channels rendered at request time, nothing materialised, every response non-cacheable | CAP-85 / STORY-99 | AC-1031, AC-1032, AC-1033, AC-977 |
| The **displayed page is replaced and the gesture re-binds** on it | CAP-87 / STORY-101 | AC-998 |

The operator's single moment — "I hit Save and my page changed" — is narrated by
all three stories, which is what made this look like a triple claim. But no two
of them perform the same work: the write path never re-renders for the browser,
the origin never writes, and the gesture never renders at all (it re-requests).

over disjoint subject matter (write contract, origin, gesture) with no duplicated
criterion between them.

### Why the survey saw a conflict — residual stale prose, recorded not absorbed

The contradiction the survey detected is real, but it lives in **body prose**,
not in story ownership, and therefore is out of scope for the three actions this
step may take (reassign / merge / confirm). Recording it so it is not lost:

1. **AC-992's body is stale against its own passing test.** Its criterion text
   still says a save "re-renders **both** the editable rendering and the plain
   draft rendering **before reporting success**", and its Verification says to
   "assert both the editable and plain rendered outputs **on disk** reflect it".
   That is the retired materialise-before-reply step, and it directly
   contradicts AC-1031 ("serving a channel materialises nothing... there is
   still no rendered artifact on disk"). The AC's **title** is already correct
   ("leaves both renderings current"), and its test
   (`test_UAT_AC992_the_origin_is_the_same_surface_faulting_and_re_rendering_alike`)
   already carries the correction inline: "REQ-119 moved WHERE that is observed,
   not whether it holds... there is no artifact to inspect and the honest
   observable is the origin itself." Only the criterion body was left behind.
   This stale sentence is the single largest source of the apparent
   "write path owns re-render" claim.

2. **STORY-100's description carries the same residue** in one sentence: an
   image edit is "named, read, applied, validated, refused and **re-rendered**
   by the identical operations a copy edit is". Elsewhere the same body already
   documents the change in full ("that step is gone and there is no artifact
   left to inspect... Only the observable moved, to the origin"). Not edited
   here: this step may not modify story content outside a merge.

3. **A fourth capability echoes the stale framing.** capability-7e4714b7 (AI
   Site Assistant, active — not in this cluster's capability set) says in its
   out-of-scope that "validation, atomicity and **re-render** are owned by the
   structured edit capability". Same wording residue, same fix.

Each is a body-text correction inside a single capability with no story
movement implied. The ownership itself is settled and correct as it stands.
