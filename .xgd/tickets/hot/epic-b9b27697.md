## Scope

Everything font-related, in three areas:

1. **Platform font library** — free fonts worth having, built into the platform and
   reusable across every tenant.
2. **Tenant font uploads** — a customer brings their own font as an asset.
3. **Font takedown** — enforce removal of a font that turns out to be unlicensed,
   across every site serving it.

Areas 1 and 3 are one decision, not two (see "The storage fork" below).

## What already exists (REQ-101, free_and_reconciled)

- `fonts/registry.yaml` — the project-level provenance index. Family, foundry,
  source, download date, licence terms, outstanding actions, file list.
- `licence.redistribute_in_product` — the load-bearing flag. `true` / `false` /
  `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, where unresolved is treated as "not permitted" by every gate,
  so an unanswered licence cannot leak into product distribution by default.
- `1c fonts check` (`tools/generate/src/cli/fonts.ts`) — joins every site's
  `l1.resources.fonts` *and* every font file on disk against the registry. Four
  violation kinds: `unregistered-family`, `unregistered-file`, `unprovenanced-file`,
  `redistribution-not-permitted` (the last fires only for `config.distribution: "product"`).
- `l1FontFaceSchema` — binds a `fontFamily` handle to its served `.woff2`. Structured
  only; the renderer is the sole `@font-face { src: url(…) }` sink.
- Area 2 plumbing is largely present already: `material.ts` classifies
  `woff/woff2/ttf/otf/ttc` as `kind: 'font'`, and the Library → **Use on site** path
  (`promoteToSiteAsset`) promotes non-image material to site assets.

## Area 1 — platform font library

### Mirror the whole corpus. Curate nothing.

**The assistant is the chooser.** There is no curated font menu, no category taxonomy,
no pairing hints, no house shortlist. A hand-written list of "good" families is a taste
judgment frozen at authoring time by the same model that would make the per-site call
anyway — so it adds nothing and rots. The full corpus is the menu.

What the platform supplies instead is only the two things the model cannot supply itself:

1. **Licence status**, which is a *fact* the model cannot verify and which, got wrong,
   ships an unlicensed font to a customer site.
2. **Served bytes**, because a family the model names that is not on the origin falls
   back silently to a system stack. This is an *acquisition* question, not a choice
   question, and the answer is to mirror everything rather than to narrow the choice.

### Ingest

Mirror the whole OFL 1.1 / Apache 2.0 corpus (essentially all of Google Fonts, ~1,700+
families) eagerly into a platform R2 prefix. A few hundred MB is free, and eager
mirroring removes cold-start latency on first use of a family, removes a runtime
dependency on Google's servers at authoring time, and avoids leaking which tenant is
building what.

**The registry becomes generated, not hand-written.** Derived from upstream metadata:
one entry per family, `redistribute_in_product: true` where the licence field reads OFL
or Apache 2.0, excluded from the mirror otherwise. No human writes taste into it, and
no family needs an individual legal decision. The licence fact is queryable rather than
asserted — the `google/fonts` repo carries a per-family `METADATA.pb` with an explicit
licence field and the licence text in the family directory.

Net effect: the model picks any family it likes, and if it resolves it is both licensed
and served, because those are the only two things in the mirror.

### The only metadata carried alongside

Which **weights and variable axes actually ship** per family. This is not curation — it
is the same class of fact as "the file exists". Referencing weight 300 of a family that
only ships 400/700 is a silent fallback, identical in effect to a missing file. Category,
pairing hints and metric class are deliberately **not** carried: that is taste, and taste
is the model's.

### Excluded from the mirror

- **Fontshare / ITF FFL** (Satoshi) — free and commercial-use, but the grant is
  non-transferrable and terminable: it licenses *us*, not everyone we hand a site to.
- **Adobe Fonts** — forbids self-hosting outright.
- **Ubuntu Font Licence** families — redistributable, but modification terms differ;
  clear individually rather than in bulk.
- **Icon fonts** — a different problem. Font Awesome Brands carries third-party
  trademark constraints independent of the font licence; `eicons` has no standalone
  upstream distribution at all.

### OFL obligations that must travel

- Copyright notice and licence text ship with the distribution — concretely, a served
  `LICENSES.txt` alongside the files.
- The font files must not be sold standalone.
- **Reserved Font Name**: a *modified* version must not carry the reserved name, and
  subsetting is arguably modification. Practice is settled (Google's own subsets do this
  universally), but the zero-risk path is mirroring unmodified upstream release woff2 —
  variable where available — and unicode-range splitting only if byte cost bites.

### Side win

Five current `REVIEW_REQUIRED` entries close as a consequence. Cinzel, Lato, Oswald,
Raleway and Karla are all OFL families on disk as hash-named capture mirrors with
unverified subset/delivery provenance. Re-point them at canonical mirror copies and
their open actions resolve.

## The storage fork (binds area 1 to area 3)

Font files live per-site under `draft/assets/` today, deliberately, so a site stays
self-contained and portable. A platform mirror collides with that invariant:

| | Copy-on-select (per-site) | Shared platform origin |
|---|---|---|
| Portability | preserved | site depends on platform origin |
| Bytes | duplicated per tenant | one copy |
| **Takedown** | **N-tenant sweep + N rebuilds** | **one registry flip + purge** |

**Proposed:** platform fonts are shared-served from a platform R2 prefix; tenant-uploaded
fonts stay per-site. The provenance classes already differ — we control the platform copy,
we do not control what a customer uploaded — so the storage tier should differ too.
Takedown of a platform font becomes a flag flip; takedown of an uploaded font is a
per-tenant delete, which it was going to be either way.

A full-corpus mirror sharpens this: N per-tenant copies of a shared corpus is clearly
the wrong shape.

Consequence: the registry grows a tier distinction (platform vs. site), and `1c fonts
check` learns to resolve a platform-origin `src`.

**This decision is open and should be settled before area 1 is built** — it is unpleasant
to reverse once tenants hold assets.

## Area 2 — tenant font uploads (sketch, not yet scoped)

Plumbing largely exists. What is missing is the licence question: a customer uploading a
commercial font is asserting a licence we cannot verify. Needs an attestation step at
upload, recorded on the material row, and a registry entry in the site tier so
`1c fonts check` can still account for the bytes.

## Area 3 — takedown (sketch, not yet scoped)

Enforce removal of a font across every site serving it. Shape depends entirely on the
storage fork above. Needs: a revocation state distinct from `redistribute_in_product:
false` (which is "never cleared" rather than "was cleared, now withdrawn"), a sweep that
finds every referencing site, a fallback-family substitution so a revoked font degrades
rather than breaks the page, and an audit record of what was removed and when.

## Decision log

**2026-09-22 — curated font menu dropped.** An earlier draft of area 1 proposed ingesting
the corpus as inventory but shipping a curated 40–60 family *menu* on top, carrying
category and pairing metadata. Operator rejected this: the assistant is the chooser, and
the model that would write the curation is the same model that makes the per-site usage
call, so the artifact adds no judgment and freezes taste that should be live. Superseded
by "Mirror the whole corpus. Curate nothing." The residual platform responsibility is
narrowed to licence clearance and byte availability — both facts, neither a constraint on
choice.
