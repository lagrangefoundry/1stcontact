## Scope

Everything font-related, in three areas:

1. **Platform font library** — free fonts worth having, built into the platform and
   reusable across every tenant.
2. **Tenant font uploads** — a customer brings their own font as an asset.
3. **Font takedown** — enforce removal of a font that turns out to be unlicensed,
   across every site serving it.

Areas 1 and 3 share one decision, settled 2026-09-22 — see "Storage tiers" below.

## What already exists (REQ-101, free_and_reconciled)

- `fonts/registry.yaml` — the project-level provenance index. Family, foundry,
  source, download date, licence terms, outstanding actions, file list.
- `licence.redistribute_in_product` — the load-bearing flag. `true` / `false` /
  `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, where unresolved is treated as "not permitted" by every gate,
  so an unanswered licence cannot leak into product distribution by default.
- `1c fonts check` (`tools/generate/src/cli/fonts.ts`) — joins every site's
  `l1.resources.fonts` *and* every font file on disk against the registry. Four
  violation kinds: `unregistered-family`, `unregistered-file`, `unprovenanced-file`,
  `redistribution-not-permitted` (the last fires only for `config.distribution: "product"`).
- `l1FontFaceSchema` — binds a `fontFamily` handle to its served `.woff2`. Structured
  only; the renderer is the sole `@font-face { src: url(…) }` sink.
- Area 2 plumbing is largely present already: `material.ts` classifies
  `woff/woff2/ttf/otf/ttc` as `kind: 'font'`, and the Library → **Use on site** path
  (`promoteToSiteAsset`) promotes non-image material to site assets.

## The gap: the production assistant cannot obtain a font at all

Not "does not know where to find one" — has no hands. Established by reading the tool
surface:

- `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/instances.json` defines two production instances,
  `consultant` and `builder`. Both carry `ReadSite` (which holds `list_assets` and
  `get_asset`) and `AuthorPages`. **Neither carries `ManageAssets`**, the group holding
  `add_asset` / `remove_asset`.
- `add_asset` would not help if granted: `toolbox-core.ts:262` notes it "reads a file off
  the operator's disk", so it is structurally a dev-machine tool, not merely role-gated.
- `l1-surface.json:1242` states the consequence to the assistant directly: *"You cannot
  go and get a file — not from a URL, not from a machine, not from anywhere… so when you
  need a photograph, a logo or a font, ASK FOR IT HERE."*
- `REF-l1.md:604`: *"A painted font family must resolve to a face the page serves or name
  a generic every browser has, or it silently paints the browser default rather than
  anything chosen."*

So in production the assistant can see fonts already on a site and can paint a
`fontFamily`, but its only route to a *new* font is asking the customer to go download
one and drop it in the conversation. Otherwise it paints a family name that silently
resolves to the browser default.

## Area 1 — platform font library

### Full corpus for the assistant; a curated list only for the human dropdown

**Operator direction (2026-09-22): ship everything for the AI; for the human dropdown in
the editor, a curated list of under 100.**

Two front doors onto one mirror. The curated list is a **UI affordance, not a gate**: a
`<select>` of 1,946 families is unusable for a person. It never narrows what the assistant
may choose.

### The catalogue lives in a ticket

[[DOC-56]], a doc ticket of kind `system_kb` — the same kind as every other system-KB
member. Chunking is heading-anchored, so its 92 group headings give the ~92-chunk
granularity the bundle can afford, and the xgd artificer can inspect and reference it with
`xgd ticket get` like anything else.

An earlier draft of this epic argued the opposite — that the catalogue should be a
ticketless `REF-*` projection, citing `kb-projection.ts`. That rule is itself the defect;
see [[REQ-310]].

### The list is documented, because the model does not know it

An earlier draft of this epic argued the assistant needed no list — "the model already
knows what Playfair Display is", so ship a resolver and no catalogue. **Operator
challenged this and the claim did not survive testing.** A random sample of 40 non-Noto
families produced roughly one third the model could describe and two thirds it could not
(`ancizarserif`, `baumans`, `beiruti`, `borel`, `drsugiyama`, `keaniaone`, `novaoval`,
`pliant`, `vampiroone`, the whole `playwrite*` set). The corpus-size estimate in that same
draft — "~1,700+" — was also wrong against an actual 1,946. Twenty families post-date the
training cutoff and are invisible to recall entirely.

The consequence is a product risk, not a pedantic one: **a corpus the model cannot
enumerate is a corpus it will not use.** Left to recall it reaches for the same forty
faces and every site looks the same, while 1,900 licensed families sit unused.

So the assistant gets a **query path**, not only a resolver — see below.

### Category is an index, not taste — earlier position corrected

An earlier draft cut category, pairing hints and metric class together as "taste, and
taste is the model's". That was wrong about category: **you cannot query a corpus you
cannot describe**, so classification is the index that makes the corpus reachable at all.
Category, weights, axes and subsets are carried. Pairing hints and house shortlists remain
out — those are taste.

### Ingest

Mirror the redistributable corpus (OFL + Apache, 1,941 families) eagerly into a platform
R2 prefix. A few hundred MB is free, and eager mirroring removes cold-start latency,
removes a runtime dependency on Google's servers at authoring time, and avoids leaking
which tenant is building what.

**The registry becomes generated, not hand-written** — one entry per mirrored family,
`redistribute_in_product: true` from the licence field. No human writes taste into it and
no family needs an individual legal decision, with the five UFL families the exception
that must be cleared one at a time.

**The ingest must read each family's own `METADATA.pb` for licence.** Joining the live
family list to licence directories by normalised slug is *not* reliable: six `Edu *`
families were renamed upstream (`Edu NSW ACT Cursive` → `edunswactfoundation`) and failed
the join. `fonts/catalogue.json` marks those `licence_source: prefix-inference`.

### The assistant's surface: the KB carries the list, `use_font` binds it

**No new search tool.** The system knowledge base already is the search technology, and
the catalogue is simply another corpus document in it: authored markdown under
`kb/system/`, embedded by `1c kb build` with `@cf/baai/bge-small-en-v1.5` into
heading-anchored chunks, bundle-resident and byte-identical for every tenant
(`apps/control-app/src/system-knowledge.ts`). An assistant asking the KB for "geometric
sans serif" reaches the corpus through the path it already uses for every other design
question. A bespoke `search_fonts` would have been a second retrieval stack to keep
correct.

So the assistant surface is **one** new operation:

- **`use_font("Playfair Display", weights: [400, 700])`** — binds the face into the page's
  `resources.fonts` and returns the handle to paint. **Refuses legibly** when the family is
  not in the mirror, so the model picks another rather than silently painting a generic.
  **Reports which weights and axes actually ship**, so asking for weight 300 of a family
  that ships 400/700 is a stated correction rather than a silent fallback.

### Chunk granularity is a bundle-size decision

Embeddings are float32 at **1,536 bytes per chunk**; the whole system corpus today is 357
chunks / 548KB of `embeddings.bin` plus 222KB of metadata. A chunk per family would add
~2.9MB of embeddings and ~1.2MB of metadata to a **bundle-resident** index, which is
likely bundle-breaking.

Group families under headings instead — roughly 20 per chunk, ~100 chunks, ~200KB. This is
cheaper *and* better suited to the task: a query for "geometric sans" should return a
slate of candidates to choose among, not a single family. `system-knowledge.ts` records
that the index could move to R2 if per-family granularity is ever wanted — the
`IndexSource` port exists for exactly that swap.

### Knowledge correction

`l1-surface.json:1242` and `REF-l1.md:604` both currently teach the assistant that a font
is something it must be given. Once the tools exist these are wrong, and left in place
they will stop it reaching for them. The "or a font" clause comes out of 1242.

### Excluded from the mirror

- **Delisted / sandboxed families** — the 110-directory gap between the repo and the live
  list.
- **Fontshare / ITF FFL** (Satoshi) — free and commercial-use, but the grant is
  non-transferrable and terminable: it licenses *us*, not everyone we hand a site to.
- **Adobe Fonts** — forbids self-hosting outright.
- **UFL 1.0** (5 families) — redistributable, but modification terms differ; clear
  individually rather than in bulk.
- **Icon fonts** — a different problem. Font Awesome Brands carries third-party trademark
  constraints independent of the font licence; `eicons` has no standalone upstream at all.

### OFL obligations that must travel

- Copyright notice and licence text ship with the distribution — concretely, a served
  `LICENSES.txt` alongside the files.
- The font files must not be sold standalone.
- **Reserved Font Name**: a *modified* version must not carry the reserved name, and
  subsetting is arguably modification. Practice is settled (Google's own subsets do this
  universally), but the zero-risk path is mirroring unmodified upstream release woff2 —
  variable where available — and unicode-range splitting only if byte cost bites.

### Side win

Five current `REVIEW_REQUIRED` entries close as a consequence. Cinzel, Lato, Oswald,
Raleway and Karla are all OFL families on disk as hash-named capture mirrors with
unverified subset/delivery provenance. Re-point them at canonical mirror copies and their
open actions resolve.

## Storage tiers — SETTLED 2026-09-22

**Operator decision: platform fonts are platform-wide and shared. Tenant fonts are
per-site, uploaded by the tenant, and the tenant's licence responsibility.**

Rationale as given: fonts are small, but copying them around gets fiddly, and fiddly in
the way that rots. The earlier copy-on-select option is closed.

| | Platform tier | Tenant tier |
|---|---|---|
| Source | the mirror (OFL / Apache only) | uploaded by the client as material |
| Storage | one shared platform R2 prefix | per-site `draft/assets/` |
| Licence | cleared by construction | **attested by the tenant at upload** |
| `src` | platform origin URL | site-relative asset |
| Takedown | registry flip + purge | per-tenant delete |

**The responsibility split, and its one limit.** The tenant uploads, the tenant attests
they hold the licence, and liability sits with them. But the platform still serves the
bytes, so an attestation shifts *liability* without removing the *obligation to act* on a
valid takedown notice. Areas 2 and 3 are therefore a pair: the attestation is the record
of who claimed what, the takedown is the mechanism for when a claim proves false.

**This collapses most of area 3.** The platform tier is cleared by construction, so the
"sweep every tenant" problem does not arise for it — takedown there is a narrow residual
case (a family withdrawn upstream, or an OFL licence dispute) handled by a registry flip
and a cache purge. The real takedown surface is the tenant-upload tier, which is per-site,
where deletion was always a per-tenant action.

Consequence for the build: the registry grows a tier distinction (platform vs. site), and
`1c fonts check` learns to resolve a platform-origin `src`. **`use_font` is unblocked** —
it writes a platform origin URL.

## Area 2 — tenant font uploads (sketch, not yet scoped)

Plumbing largely exists. What is missing is the licence question: a customer uploading a
commercial font is asserting a licence we cannot verify. Needs an attestation step at
upload, recorded on the material row, and a registry entry in the site tier so
`1c fonts check` can still account for the bytes.

## Area 3 — takedown (sketch, not yet scoped)

Mostly collapsed by the tier decision. The platform tier is cleared by construction, so
its residual case is narrow — a family withdrawn upstream or an OFL licence dispute —
handled by a registry flip plus a cache purge. **The real surface is the tenant-upload
tier**, which is per-site.

Still needed: a revocation state distinct from `redistribute_in_product: false` (which
means "never cleared" rather than "was cleared, now withdrawn"), a sweep that finds every
site referencing a revoked family, a fallback-family substitution so a revoked font
degrades rather than breaks the page, and an audit record of what was removed and when.

## Decision log

**2026-09-22 — curated font menu dropped as a constraint on the assistant.** An earlier
draft proposed the assistant choose from a curated 40–60 family menu. Operator rejected:
the assistant is the chooser, and the model that would write the curation is the model
that makes the per-site call, so the artifact adds no judgment and freezes taste that
should be live.

**2026-09-22 — curated list reinstated, for humans only.** Operator direction: ship
everything for the AI; curate under 100 for the human dropdown in the editor. A UI
affordance for a person facing a `<select>`, not a gate on the assistant.

**2026-09-22 — the real gap identified.** The production assistant has no font acquisition
path whatsoever, which is why area 1's central deliverable is tooling rather than a list.

**2026-09-22 — "the model knows the corpus" tested and refuted; catalogue documented.**
Operator challenged the claim that the assistant needed no list. Sampling showed roughly
two thirds of non-Noto families unknown to recall, the corpus-size estimate wrong (1,946
vs. "~1,700+"), and 20 families post-dating the training cutoff. `fonts/catalogue.json`
and `fonts/CATALOGUE.md` now document the list. Two corrections follow: the assistant
needs **`search_fonts`** alongside `use_font`, and **category is an index rather than
taste** and is carried after all.

**2026-09-22 — `search_fonts` dropped; the KB is the search technology.** Operator:
"We already have search technology, we don't have to invent a new one — this should just
go into the knowledge base." Correct: `kb/system/` is authored markdown embedded by
`1c kb build` and queried through the existing knowledge toolbox, so the catalogue is a
corpus document rather than a new tool. The assistant surface narrows back to `use_font`
alone. Chunk granularity becomes a bundle-size decision — see that section.

**2026-09-22 — storage tiers settled: platform-wide and shared.** Operator: "I was
assuming this would be platform wide and shared — fonts may be small but copying them
around is just going to get fiddly; per-tenant fonts are user-uploaded and their
responsibility to meet the licence requirements for." Copy-on-select is closed. Platform
fonts are shared-served and cleared by construction; tenant fonts are per-site and
tenant-attested. `use_font` is unblocked, and most of area 3 collapses onto the
tenant-upload tier.

**2026-09-22 — the KB document is a projection, and the ticket is its policy counterpart.**
Operator asked for a ticket backing `kb/system/REF-fonts.md` for consistency. `REF-*` files
are deliberately ticketless projections; the corpus convention pairs a projection with an
authored doc (`REF-l1` with `DOC-23`). So `REF-fonts` stays projected and a font-policy DOC
ticket (item B2) becomes the ticket-backed artifact.

**2026-09-22 — the projection convention is the defect, not the exception.** This epic
cited `REF-*` projections as settled policy justifying a ticketless catalogue. Operator:
"Unfortunately it is the rule that is broken — this is something we need to clean up. The
system kb should be doc tickets of kind `system_kb`." The catalogue is [[DOC-56]], a doc
ticket; the projection cleanup is [[REQ-310]]. Populating a KB from documents rather than
tickets is retired for 1st Contact (it remains live in `xgd`, where the change has not
propagated).

**2026-09-22 — priority raised.** Operator: this is the next priority; it blocks the
Lagrange Foundry site and the beta.
