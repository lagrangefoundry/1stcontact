## Story
**As a** visitor to a business's published site, **I want** to submit one of its public forms (a waitlist, a beta application, a "send me the papers" request) without signing in, **so that** my address and what I wrote reach that business's contact list, and any asset the form promised arrives in my inbox. **As the** business and the platform, **we want** that endpoint to be useless for mailing strangers, probing who is already a contact, flooding a contact list, or burning the sending domain's reputation. **As an** operator building a form in the builder, **I want** the form in the draft preview to submit for real, **so that** I see exactly what a visitor will see when they press the button, and the test lead is marked as coming from the preview.

## Description
Before REQ-223 every public form in the product posted to an endpoint that had never existed. This story covers the capability that now exists: an anonymous visitor writing into one tenant's contact database through a form on that tenant's published site. BUG-78 extends it to the builder's draft preview, which was the only place an operator could press the button and the one place it could not work.

In scope:
- **The endpoint.** A same-origin `POST /api/lead` on the published site: the bare path for the apex site and `/site/<siteKey>/api/lead` for a keyed site. It accepts a JSON submission (the upgraded form) and a native form POST (no JavaScript).
- **The preview endpoint (BUG-78).** The control app answers `POST /preview/<slug>/draft/api/lead`: the preview channel's own root plus the same `api/lead` suffix. So one root-relative `action` is correct in both places and the renderer never learns which channel it is in. It is the same handler as the published endpoint, not a second one: same body and field limits, submit shapes, honeypot, acknowledgement and refusals. It writes into the tenant in process, without a service binding. Only the `draft` channel accepts a submission; a lead POST to any other preview channel, or for a slug the store does not know, answers `404`.
- **Which tenant.** The tenant is taken from the request URL and never from the body. On the published site it is the site path; in the preview it is the slug, resolved to a site key through the operator's own store.
- **What is written.** One contact at the `lead` stage per (tenant, address). Each submission adds one append-only provenance event: channel, site, page, form instance, submit label, the other answers, and every declared consent checkbox with its wording and answer. Authored values are read from the definition the submitter was served: the live published revision for the published site, the draft for the preview.
- **Controls.** Body and field limits, a rate limit keyed on site and source IP, a honeypot, and server-side Turnstile verification. Any missing configuration fails closed. The preview skips the rate limit and Turnstile, because Cloudflare Access has already identified the submitter. Only the server can set that bypass; nothing in a request can obtain it.
- **The acknowledgement.** Every outcome that depends on who submitted answers one frozen acknowledgement, byte for byte. Malformed or unverified submissions are refused instead.
- **Asset delivery.** A form that declares an asset sends it at most once, ever, per address, through the platform's existing recorded-mail path. An address with a bounced or complained message is never sent to.
- **The internal seam.** The published site hands the write to the control app over a service binding to a named RPC entrypoint, and no URL reaches it.
- **Form surface.** `contact-form` gains a `checkbox` field type and a per-mount instance handle. The Turnstile sitekey and widget are stamped onto served pages at serve time by the published site only. The draft preview posts for real. The edit render still cannot post.

Out of scope (REQ-223 §9): notifying the business owner, AI spam detection and reputation checks, the whitepaper PDFs themselves, fixing production sign-in, and a builder UI for a form's `action`. A public re-send path is deliberately excluded (§5). The `complained` message status and webhook mapping, and the amendment of the published-site server's read-only rule, are covered by plan item 8 (STORY-169 / STORY-95 upgrades), not here. Shared D1 persistence between the two local dev Workers and production configuration (`APEX_SITE_KEY`, a real Turnstile secret, the Access bypass for sign-in) are BUG-78 operator actions, not code.

## Technical Context
- **Architecture.** DOC-5 rules 22–23: public forms need no login, and spam is handled by Turnstile, rate limiting and honeypot. AI spam detection and reputation checks are named out of scope, so their absence is a decision. Rule 11: this is the narrow dynamic endpoint the public site is allowed. Rule 34: lead capture is one of the two named exceptions to "a published site never depends on compute".
- **Reuse, unchanged.** Person creation goes through the one definition of how a person enters a tenant (CAP-114 `addContact`: AC-1743, AC-1744, AC-2336), so it is idempotent on (tenant, email) and lands at `pipeline_stage = 'lead'`. The submission is recorded as a contact event (append-only: AC-2258, AC-2263, AC-2264) under a new dotted kind with its label, plus a separate asset-sent kind. The path is resolved by the existing route grammar (AC-2244, AC-2333). Delivery uses the existing mail port, templates (an `asset` template) and message record, so a delivered asset appears in the contact's history beside invites.
- **Ownership.** Every existing add-contact path (STORY-146/165) is owner-only behind Access (AC-2172). This story is the first anonymous write, which is why it is a feature and not an upgrade.
- **Preview route (BUG-78, upgrade).** `apps/control-app/src/router.ts` handles the route inside its existing `/preview/<slug>/<channel>/…` match. It resolves the site key with `store.siteKey(slug)`, the same lookup the `published` redirect uses, and calls `handleLead` imported from `apps/public-site/src/lead.ts`. This is the first import from one app into another. It is deliberate, to avoid a second copy of the validation surface, and moving it to `packages/` would be a one-import refactor. `LEAD_INTAKE` is supplied as an in-process wrapper around `captureLead` that sets `channel: 'draft'` after spreading the submission, so the channel is a server fact and never comes from the payload. `handleLead` takes an optional `identified` context flag. When it is set, the rate-limit and Turnstile blocks are skipped. Shape, size, intake presence, honeypot and acknowledgement still apply. The control app passes the flag at its one call site. The public site never passes it. `formDefinitionOf` takes a `channel` (default `published`): `draft` reads `store.readPages(slug)` and `published` reads the live revision exactly as before. `LeadSubmission.channel` (`'published' | 'draft'`) defaults to `published`.
- **Related.** STORY-169 (message status vocabulary, email webhook) and STORY-95 (published-site serving, AC-912) are upgraded by plan item 8 to add `complained` and the single POST exception. STORY-85 owns the behavior-module framework that `contact-form` lives in.
- **Per-site rate cap tension** (REQ-223 §6 asks for this to be recorded). The limiter is keyed on (site, source IP) at 20 submissions per 60 seconds (`apps/public-site/wrangler.toml:85-101`). A per-site cap is itself a denial-of-service lever against that customer: anyone who fills the bucket from an address refuses that address's real enquiries until the window rolls. It is therefore deliberately generous, a backstop rather than the main defence. Turnstile is the main defence, and zone-level WAF rate rules are complementary configuration, not code.
- **Policy tension to flag (not a code issue).** Security Policy §5 says a published site ships no third-party script except Stripe.js on the checkout page. REQ-223 §6 explicitly and deliberately adds Cloudflare's Turnstile script from `challenges.cloudflare.com` on pages carrying a form. The operator's request is the intent and the code follows it, so the Security Policy document needs a second named exception. That is a documentation follow-up, not a defect for regression to fix.

## Reconciliation Decisions
- **The preview submits for real** (settled at level 1 by BUG-78's own body, "Decision on finding 1", 2026-09-11; body, code and plan agree). The operator's words: "The draft preview answers the lead endpoint itself, and a submission from it writes a real contact into the real tenant … test rows in the CRM are wanted, not avoided." The alternative, a preview that declines to submit, was rejected. Formalized as the preview-endpoint AC.
- **Controls skipped in the preview, never by request** (BUG-78, 2026-09-11). Intent: "both controls exist to answer a question Access has already answered … If the preview ever moves out from behind Access, both controls come back", and "IT IS SET IN CODE, AT ONE CALL SITE, AND NO REQUEST CAN CARRY IT". The code matches. A body field named `identified` posted to the public endpoint is stored as an ordinary answer and challenged as before. Formalized as the bypass AC, including its negative half.
- **The channel is recorded on every submission, including published ones** (BUG-78, 2026-09-11). The plan item says the lead "records its channel (preview)". Intent and code are more precise: the recorded value is `draft` for a preview submission and `published` otherwise, and it is always present "so a reader can tell a live lead from one written before the field existed". The AC follows the intent. It is provenance on the event, not a second class of contact.
- **Definition read from the served snapshot** (BUG-78, 2026-09-11): "read the definition from the snapshot the submitter was actually served". AC-2505 is generalized from "live published definition" to that rule, and the published behavior is unchanged.
- **A lead POST to a non-draft preview channel or an unknown slug answers 404** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-09). Intent says only that "the edit channel refuses". The code answers `404 Not found` for the `edit` channel, for the `published` channel and for a slug the store does not resolve. These answers do not need to look like the acknowledgement, because the route sits behind Access and the caller is an identified operator, not an anonymous prober. Formalized in AC-2515 and the preview-endpoint AC. Note that the router's doc comment says `published` "falls through to the redirect", but the code returns `404` before reaching it. Only the comment is inaccurate. It is recorded here as a documentation nit, not a behavior to fix.
- **Unknown site and submissions with no address** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). REQ-223 §2/§8.2 lists five indistinguishable outcomes and is silent on a site key that names no site, or a submission carrying no email-shaped value. The code writes nothing in both cases and still answers the frozen acknowledgement. This follows the same rule (the response must not reveal anything about tenants or addresses), so it is formalized as part of the frozen-acknowledgement AC and the provenance/no-write ACs.
- **Two classes of response** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). Intent says identity-dependent outcomes are indistinguishable, and that a missing or invalid Turnstile token and oversized bodies are *refused*. The code draws the line exactly there: refusals (415 wrong content type, 413/400 limits, 400 missing token, 403 failed verification, 503 unconfigured or unwritable) are visibly different from the acknowledgement, because a malformed request tells the caller nothing about anybody. Formalized as separate refusal ACs.
- **Unwritable deployment fails closed** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). Intent says absent Turnstile configuration fails closed and is silent on a missing service binding, a missing or erroring rate limiter, or the write throwing. The code answers 503 in each case rather than thanking a visitor whose address was dropped (the DOC-47 failure). Formalized as its own AC.
- **"Refused before the body is parsed"** (§8.10, decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08; updated at reconciliation review after report-0fca9951). Taken literally, a field count cannot be checked before parsing. The code refuses a declared over-size body (`content-length` over 8 KiB) with `413` without reading it. A body with no declared length (chunked), or one that lies about its length, is read as a byte stream with a running count and cut off with `413` the moment the count passes 8 KiB, so it is never read whole. Field count (24) and per-field length (4000 bytes) are checked immediately after parsing, before any rate-limit, verification or write. The AC states this ordering, not an impossible one.
- **Bounded body read — RESOLVED** (originally recorded as "DIVERGENCE: an undeclared large body is read whole" at reconciliation review, 2026-11-08; resolved under code_frozen override report-0fca9951). REQ-223 §6 says a large body "must be rejected rather than read". `readFields` (`apps/public-site/src/lead.ts`) reads through `request.body.getReader()`, adds each chunk's byte length to a running total, and as soon as the total passes `MAX_BODY_BYTES` cancels the stream and returns `413`, so at most one chunk beyond the cap is ever pulled. The bytes are decoded once with `TextDecoder`, so the limit is measured on bytes. The declared-length check runs first. AC-2509 states the bounded read, and its UAT sends a never-ending stream and asserts `413`, cancellation, and no more than cap + one chunk pulled. No code issue remains.
- **DIVERGENCE: `multipart/form-data` is accepted but parsed as urlencoded** (recorded at reconciliation review, 2026-11-08). `shapeOf` accepts `multipart/form-data` as the form shape, but the body is parsed with `URLSearchParams`. A real multipart submission therefore loses every field, including its Turnstile token, and is refused `400` as missing a token. The published `contact-form` posts urlencoded (and JSON when upgraded), so no form the platform renders is affected; a hand-written `enctype="multipart/form-data"` form would be. No AC asserts multipart acceptance. **Code issue flagged** (code frozen): either parse multipart with `request.formData()` or refuse it with `415` like any other unsupported type.
- **Suppression is per tenant** (recorded at reconciliation review, 2026-11-08). REQ-223 §5 says an address that bounced or complained is "never mailed again by this endpoint", and the sending domain is shared across the platform. The code checks only the submitting tenant's own contact for that address (`apps/control-app/src/lead.ts`); a complaint recorded under tenant A does not stop tenant B's form mailing the same address. This is a narrowing of §5, recorded as a divergence and stated in AC-2512. The cost is deliverability: a complaint is the event that damages the shared domain, and one tenant's complainer can still be mailed by another tenant's form. **Code issue flagged** (code frozen): consult a platform-wide suppression list before any asset send.
- **Control order** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). Intent names the controls but not their order. The code runs shape, then size, then rate, then honeypot, then Turnstile, so a honeypot hit never spends a siteverify call. Formalized as: a filled honeypot is acknowledged without contacting the verifier.
- **Authored values come from the served definition** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08; generalized 2026-11-09 for BUG-78). §7 requires storing "the wording the visitor was shown". The code reads consent wording, submit label, page and asset from the definition the submitter was served, by form-instance handle, never from the request. For the published site that is the live published revision, and for the preview it is the draft. So a caller cannot assert a wording or an asset. Every declared checkbox is recorded, ticked or not (an unticked box submits nothing), and the address is not repeated in the answers bag. Reserved wire fields (honeypot, Turnstile token, instance handle) are not stored. Formalized in the provenance AC.
- **A body field naming a tenant is stored as an answer** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). §8.5 says such a field "is ignored" for routing. The code ignores it for routing and keeps it as one more thing the visitor typed (§4: extra fields must not be dropped). Formalized that way.
- **Half-declared asset** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). Intent is silent. A form with an asset key but no URL, or a URL but no key, is treated as promising nothing, because at-most-once needs a key to remember and delivery needs a URL. Formalized in the asset-delivery AC.
- **Apex endpoint with no apex site** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). Intent is silent. A deployment with no apex site answers a bare `POST /api/lead` with the ordinary 405, the same as any other path.
- **Unconfigured sitekey serves the published page unchanged** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-11-08). The refusal happens loudly at the endpoint (fail closed), not by altering the page.
- **Capability placement** (plan open assumption). Placed in CAP-114 (capability-e5939ceb) because the substance is the identity write. The checkbox and Turnstile form-surface ACs are folded in as the consent and anti-spam half of the same capability per REQ-223 §7. They could move to STORY-85 without other change.
