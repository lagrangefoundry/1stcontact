## Symptom

`bin/copy-site --from <env>:<business> --to <env>:<business>` (REQ-654) names each end's business by name alone, but business names are only unique within an account. Two different customers can each have a business with the same name.

The tool resolves a name like this (`resolveBusiness` in `tools/generate/src/cli/copy.ts`):

1. It looks in the businesses the caller's identity (the service token's mapped user) is a member of.
2. Only if nothing matches there does it look in the platform directory (`/api/admin/sites`).
3. It refuses when more than one business in the list it ended up reading matches.

So a match in the operator's own account silently wins over another account's business with the same name, and the two are never compared. Copying a prepared site to a customer's business with the same name as the operator's own test or starter business would overwrite the operator's business, or read the wrong source. Nothing in the output names the account, so the mistake is not visible before the write.

## Fix

**Each end names its business by account email and business name:**

    bin/copy-site --from dev:operator@example.com:"Client Shop Test 1" \
                  --to production:client@example.com:"Client Shop" --handoff

- The spec is `<env>:<email>:<business name>`. The email identifies the account: the account that person belongs to, looked up on that side through the platform directory. The business name is matched (trimmed, case-insensitive, as today) only among that account's businesses.
- **A bare name with no email is refused** before anything is read or written, with a message giving the `<env>:<email>:<name>` form.
- **A business id (`<env>:biz_…`) still works on its own**, since an id is unambiguous.
- **Refusals name what went wrong:**
  - no account on that side for that email;
  - the email belongs to more than one account and the name matches a business in more than one of them (list the accounts);
  - no business of that name in that account (list what the account has).
  In every case nothing is read from the other side and nothing is written.
- **The output names the owner account on both ends**, in the dry-run and the real run (the owner's email and account name next to the business name and id), so the operator can check the target before the write.
- The same resolution is used everywhere `copy.ts` resolves a business by name: `copy-site`, its `--handoff` and `--backup` forms, and `copy-to-cloud` / `copy-from-cloud` / `copy-repro-to-dev` where they take a business name. They keep one resolver, not two.
- The platform directory (`/api/admin/sites`, `apps/control-app/src/directory.ts`) gains what the lookup needs: the account's member (or owner) email(s). It is an operator path behind Access, so this widens nothing public.
- `bin/copy-site --help`, the `1c` usage text and its examples are updated to the new form.

## Why free-coded

A small change to one resolver and one directory field, and the current behaviour can overwrite the wrong customer's site.

## What landed

- `tools/generate/src/cli/copy.ts`: `parseBusinessSpec` (`<email>:<name>` or `biz_…`; a bare name is refused) and one `resolveBusiness` that every caller uses. `copy-site` (including `--handoff`, `--backup` and `--dry-run`), `copy-to-cloud`/`copy-from-cloud` and `copy-repro-to-dev` all go through it. `parseEnd` checks the business spec, so a bare `copy-site` end is refused before any request. `BusinessRef` carries `owner` (account id, account name, emails, and the email it was named by), and `describeBusiness` formats it for output.
  - `email:name` is looked up in the platform directory only. The email picks the account(s) whose active people hold it, and the name is matched (trimmed, case-insensitive) among those accounts' businesses. The caller's memberships play no part.
  - An id is looked up in the directory first, which gives its owner. If the directory has no entry for it, or refuses the caller, `/api/businesses` is used as before.
  - If the directory can't be read when an email is given, the command refuses and says to use a `biz_…` id or the operator's credential, followed by the underlying refusal (which includes the Access advice).
- `apps/control-app/src/directory.ts`: `PlatformSite.ownerEmails`. This is every email of an active person on the owning account, primary first, and empty for the platform business. It comes from one grouped query, not a query per site (DOC-5 rule 35).
- Output: `copy-site` (real run, plain dry run, handoff and handoff dry run, backup), `copy-to-cloud`/`copy-from-cloud` and `copy-repro-to-dev` print each end as `'<name>' (<id>) — owner <email>, account <account name>`. The plain `copy-site --dry-run` now resolves the destination as well, so it can name both owners.
- Help and usage text: `bin/copy-site`, `bin/copy-to-cloud`, `bin/copy-from-cloud`, `bin/copy-repro-to-dev`, the `1c` usage for all four, and the examples in `apps/control-app/ACCESS.md`.
