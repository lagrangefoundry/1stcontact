# A held control and a working one are the same greyed button

## What the operator experienced

The operator came back to a console sitting on Iteration 3 and read the page
bottom-up: the stale-reference warning ([[BUG-120]] behaviour 4) telling them
the loaded bundle is at capture schema 1 against an extractor at 4, and
underneath it **[run again] and [recapture], both greyed, both showing a
spinning cursor.**

They reported it as a hang — *"both buttons are greyed out and beachball"* —
and stopped, because that is what a spinner means. Nothing was running. The
loop was **held**: Iteration 3 filed REQ-271 and no `implemented.json` sits
beside it, so `heldBy()` holds and `page.ts` renders both continuations inert
([[REQ-272]] part 1, behaviour 3). The press the operator was waiting to be
allowed to make was one they were already allowed to make — on a different
button, the release, which was on the page the whole time.

## Two defects, one experience

### 1. `cursor: progress` is the console's word for both "wait" and "your turn"

`page.ts:326`:

```css
button[disabled] { cursor: progress; opacity: .5 }
```

One rule, and the console disables buttons for two unrelated reasons:

- **a round is in flight** — `state.running`. The machine is working; *wait* is
  the correct instruction, and a progress cursor is the correct word for it.
- **the loop is held** — `state.held`. Nothing is working. The console is
  waiting on **the operator**, who must press [the implementation has landed]
  before either continuation will move.

These are opposites — one says *do nothing*, the other says *do something* —
and the page renders them identically, in the cursor idiom that means the first.
An operator who reads the cursor stops reading, which is exactly what happened:
the ⏸ sentence explaining the hold, and the button that lifts it, were one
element above the buttons being stared at.

The console already knows which reason applies for each control; only the
stylesheet cannot tell.

### 2. The hold sentence names one of the two controls it holds

`console.ts:heldView()`:

> Iteration 3 filed REQ-271. **[run again]** is held until that implementation
> lands — the next iteration exists to measure it, so running one before it
> lands measures nothing new.

[recapture] is held too, and always has been: it carries `data-held="1"`, the
poller keys on that, and [[BUG-120]] rendered the two as one group precisely
because they are the same kind of act. The sentence predates that grouping and
was never updated.

The wording is worst in exactly the state this operator was in. With a bundle
behind `CAPTURE_SCHEMA`, [recapture] is the button that can move the numbers and
[run again] provably cannot — so the page warns about a stale reference, offers
two controls, and then explains the hold in terms of the one that is *not* the
answer. Someone reading only the ⏸ line can reasonably conclude [recapture] is
held for some other reason, or is broken.

## Why this is not cosmetic

The hold exists to stop a round being spent measuring an implementation that has
not landed — its cost is the reason [[REQ-272]] added it. This defect inverts
that saving: the operator who cannot tell held from busy does not press a wrong
button, they press **nothing**, and the loop stalls until they come back and ask
why. In the reported case the implementation the hold was waiting for (REQ-271)
had already landed; the only thing missing was the operator's press, and the
page was actively discouraging it.

## Behaviour wanted

1. **Inert-because-busy and inert-because-held do not look the same.** A control
   disabled while a round runs keeps the progress idiom. A control disabled by
   the hold reads as unavailable, not as working — `not-allowed` rather than
   `progress`. The distinction survives the poller, which re-computes
   `disabled` every second between reloads and must re-compute the reason with
   it, or the cursor goes stale the moment a round ends. And the press that IS
   available stays available: [the implementation has landed] is the operator's
   way out of the hold, so it is not greyed alongside the controls it releases —
   a release that looked inert with the rest would leave the reader exactly as
   stuck as the cursor did.
2. **The page carries the reason a control is inert**, in the markup rather than
   in the stylesheet's guesswork, so the two states are distinguishable by a
   test reading the page and not only by a human reading a cursor.
3. **The hold names both controls it holds.** [run again] and [recapture] are
   held together; the sentence says so, in the same breath, so that an operator
   looking at a stale reference is not told the hold is about the button that
   cannot help them.
4. **No change to what is held, when, or how it lifts.** [[REQ-272]]'s hold
   predicate, the `implemented.json` release record, the 409 refusal on a press
   that lands in the gap, and [[BUG-120]]'s grouping are all unchanged. This
   ticket changes what the page SAYS about a state it already computes
   correctly.
5. **No tooltips.** [[BUG-120]] removed `title=` from the continuation group on
   the grounds that a fact deciding whether a $7 round measures anything cannot
   live in a hover; the reason a control is inert is that same class of fact and
   stays in visible text and in the cursor idiom.

## Acceptance

- With a round in flight, the inert controls declare themselves busy; with the
  loop held and nothing running, they declare themselves held — and a test reads
  both states from the markup.
- The distinction is still correct after a poll tick with no page reload.
- The hold sentence names [run again] and [recapture] together.
- [[REQ-272]]'s and [[BUG-120]]'s existing UATs pass untouched: the hold still
  gates both continuations and not [reproduce], `data-held="1"` still marks
  them, the release still writes `implemented.json`, and the continuation group
  still carries no `title=`.

## How it was done

### The reason is an attribute; the cursor is a rule keyed on it

`page.ts` gained one helper, `inertAttrs(reason)`, and every site that used to
emit a bare `disabled` now goes through it:

```
button                     reasons it can be inert
[reproduce]                running
[open <stored site>]       running
[run again], [recapture]   running, then held
[the implementation …]     running only — the hold never greys its own release
```

`running` wins when both apply: it is the one that is true about the machine,
and the hold has not begun to be the operator's problem until the round that
would have filed it has finished.

The stylesheet stopped guessing:

```css
button[disabled] { cursor: not-allowed; opacity: .5 }
button[disabled][data-inert="running"] { cursor: progress }
```

`not-allowed` is the default because it is the honest answer for every other way
a control can be inert; `progress` is now reachable only through the state that
is actually working.

**Attribute order is load-bearing.** `disabled` is emitted before `data-inert`,
so the `data-held="1" disabled` pair [[REQ-272]] and [[BUG-120]] assert as
contiguous text stays contiguous. This ticket adds a fact to the markup; it does
not get to invalidate the evidence already standing on it.

### The poller re-computes the reason with the state

The poll script already re-computed `disabled` every second — keying on
`state.running` alone would re-enable a held control a second after the round
that held it finished, which is the press the hold exists to stop. The reason
needed the same treatment for the same reason, one field over: a reason computed
only at render time is a lie one second after a round ends, when the button that
was busy becomes the button that is held and goes on claiming the machine is
working. That is the exact moment the reported operator arrived at.

### The hold sentence

> Iteration 3 filed REQ-271. **Both continuations — [run again] and [recapture]
> — are held** until that implementation lands: the next iteration exists to
> measure it, so running one before it lands measures nothing new.

The clause [[REQ-272]]'s UATs match — *held until that implementation lands* —
is unchanged, as is the ticket id and the release label the 409 refusal quotes.

## Test plan

`tests/test_UAT_FC_BUG-130_inert_reason.test.ts`, five UATs. The console, its
HTTP surface, the page it serves and the poll script that page ships are real;
substituted are the headless browser, the billed model and the commands the
console shells out to.

1. `..._a_round_in_flight_and_a_held_loop_are_different_states_in_the_markup` —
   both states in one test, because a page that learned a single word passes
   half of it either way. A round is held open mid-flight and the page read;
   then released, and the page read again. Also asserts the release control is
   neither disabled nor carrying a reason while the loop is held.
2. `..._the_stylesheet_says_wait_only_for_the_state_that_is_working` — the
   defect itself, in the one place it lived: exactly one `cursor: progress` rule
   in the served CSS, and it is the one behind `[data-inert="running"]`.
3. `..._the_consoles_own_poll_script_re_computes_the_reason_with_the_state` —
   the script is extracted from the served HTML rather than imported, and run
   against the console's own `/state` with a stale `running` reason planted on a
   held button. Asserts the tick corrects it rather than merely leaving it
   disabled.
4. `..._the_hold_sentence_names_run_again_and_recapture_together`.
5. `..._a_stale_reference_and_the_hold_do_not_point_at_different_buttons` — the
   state the report came from: a schema-1 bundle, both controls held, and the
   sentence naming both.

All five fail against the pre-fix source and pass after it. Regression scope:
every suite that imports the console — 19 files, 212 tests — including
[[REQ-272]]'s and [[BUG-120]]'s untouched.
