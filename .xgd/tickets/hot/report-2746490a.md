## Observations

### Judgment calls

- **Three intents were classified as upgrades to existing stories rather than
  new ones, on the strength of the stories' own deferrals.** STORY-117 says in
  as many words that corpus residency for a deployed worker "is not forced
  yet"; REQ-158 forces it. That is the definition of partial coverage, so the
  build half is item 3 rather than a new story. The same reasoning applies to
  REQ-164 against AC-1295.

- **REQ-165 is a feature, not a third upgrade to STORY-117.** STORY-117's
  subject is documents a human wrote and a human opted in. A projector that
  reads the live module registry, the L1 schemas and the declared surface, and
  writes documents nobody maintains, is a different capability sharing one
  directory. Its whole discipline — one source per fact, two namespaces two
  sweeps, membership read from the declaration, no internal cross-references —
  has no counterpart in STORY-117's criteria.

- **REQ-163 is split four ways and REQ-161 two, which is more items than either
  ticket has parts.** The split follows what each piece can be held to
  independently: the pipeline is the ordering and the seam; the describers are
  the four sub-pipelines and the six outcomes; the guard is a security
  boundary with its own module and its own load-bearing test; promotion is a
  gate that REQ-163 shipped unrouted and REQ-161 wired, so it belongs to
  neither ticket alone. Collapsing them would produce two stories of 5-6 points
  each with unrelated failure modes.

- **The promotion gate is filed once, across two intents.** REQ-163 implemented
  `promoteToSiteAsset` with its refusal and no caller; REQ-161 supplied the
  first caller and added free-name selection and the byte copy. One capability,
  one story, evidence from both commits.

### Explicit supersession (FRAGILE-INTENT 3.3, case 2)

- REQ-161 supersedes AC-959, AC-976 and AC-1064 — the ticket lists all three by
  number and states the restatement.

### Uncertainty worth recording

- **A granted automation service identity can pass the gate and still not reach
  the builder.** REQ-167's own test comment says so: a service token
  authenticates as a `common_name` and carries no email, and the email is what
  an account binds to. STORY-120's AC-1376 (the identity is accepted as an
  automation service identity) remains true *at the gate*, which is where that
  story's boundary is — but the operator's copy-a-site-up automation now needs
  an entitlement behind it or it is refused 403. This is declared, not
  accidental, and item 17 should state the boundary rather than quietly widen
  AC-1376. Whether the automation identity gets an entitlement row is a
  question for the identity work, not for this reconciliation.
- **REQ-159's known gap is intentional and should be documented, not fixed.**
  Above the floor the clustered path needs a describer the Worker does not have
  (`@lagrangefoundry/ai-knowledge/describe` is Node-only), so `describe` is a
  required injected seam and `DescriberNotConfiguredError` names it. Item 6's
  criteria should assert that the previous map stands rather than being
  replaced by a mechanical paragraph.
- **REQ-163's DNS open question stays open.** workerd cannot resolve a hostname
  before fetching it, so a name resolving to a private address defeats the
  literal-host check. Item 9's criteria should assert what the guard does check
  and must not claim completeness.
