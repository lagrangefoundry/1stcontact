# Motion on small-business websites: survey and taste guide

**Owner:** [[EPIC-26]]. **Feeds:** the finishing-touches stage guide ([[DOC-71]] §12.9), and REQ-461 / REQ-470 (the L1 `video` node).
**Method (2026-10-26):** 33 live sites across four kinds of business. Each home page's HTML was fetched and searched for video, animation libraries and reduced-motion handling. Hero clips were downloaded and measured with ffprobe (length, size, resolution, audio track). Frames were sampled and looked at, and loop seams were measured by comparing the first and last frames (PSNR). **Limit:** nobody watched these pages play in a browser. Placement, hover behaviour and how readable the text is come from markup and frames, and the site notes mark these as unverified.
**Evidence on disk:** clips, contact sheets and HTML are in `.xgd/tmp/motion-survey/{food,grooming,makers,studios}/`. This is git-ignored, and the clips are third-party material, so they must not be committed.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **The most common failure is file weight, not taste.** Most sites built with real care still serve a whole film as a background: Saint Frank 121 MB / 133 s, Hania Rani 116 MB, Birds 61 MB, Noma 56 MB .mov, Wwake ~61 MB across 11 clips, Mork-Ulnes ~84 MB. The good loops are **1–3 MB**: Kinloch 1.3 MB, Juvet 2.7 MB, Onyx 2.7 MB, 3L 3.4 MB, Mikkeller 72 KB. Muted backgrounds often still carry an audio track.
2. **Nobody gets seamless loops right.** Nearly every loop measured cuts hard back to its start. Only 3L (forward-then-back) and near-still subjects (Kinloch's water, Benchmark's trees) hide the seam. Our `loop` and ping-pong modes beat almost the whole field.
3. **The most tasteful pattern is near-still real footage.** Kinloch Lodge is a 2 s, 1.3 MB loop where only the loch shimmers. Noma's door and garden is a locked-off shot. These read as a living photograph, give text a calm background, and are what our `loop` mode makes from a client's own photo.
4. **Real footage has to look chosen.** 3L's black-and-white grade with one warm colour, and Waves Yoga's warm slow cuts, are real footage that has been shaped. Reposted social reels read as cheap even though they're authentic: Daydream Florals' mirror selfie, Temperance's screen recording, Gypsy Floral's slowed drone shot.
5. **Photoreal AI of something that isn't theirs breaks trust.** Throne Barbershop's hero is a Sora clip of a gilded barbershop that isn't Throne. It has garbled lettering in a framed picture and a mirror that doesn't reflect the room. A visitor is buying a particular chair and a particular barber. Our approach, animating the client's **own** photo and ending on that photo, is on the right side of this line. It only stays there if the motion is believable for that real object.
6. **Skilled sites fail by having too many good clips.** Onyx has 14 videos, Fogo Island Inn 9, and Wwake 11, including inside the navigation menu. Every clip is good, and together they flatten the page. "One signature effect" holds up, with one refinement (§3, test 3).
7. **Almost no site handles reduced motion for video.** Only Hepple Spirits fully respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Juvet does too, through Webflow's runtime. **No site with a loop longer than 5 s offers a pause control**, which WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires (§5). Handling both is cheap for us and puts us ahead of almost everyone surveyed.
8. **Illustrated motion avoids the uncanny problem entirely.** Mikkeller's 1.6 s drawn loops (72 KB) and Crust Bikes' cartoon desert are plainly art and plainly the brand. Drawn brand marks also work: Onyx's signature writes itself over the hero, and Fernweh has a Lottie logo.

## 2. Pattern catalogue (the consultant's vocabulary)

| Pattern | What it is | Best examples | When to offer it | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Living photo** (cinemagraph) | A still where one element moves: water, steam, leaves, flame | Kinloch Lodge (loch), 3L shave (steam), Benchmark (tree canopy), our Old Scratch fire | Default signature for almost anyone with one strong photo | The moving element needs room in the frame; keep the motion subtle |
| **Reveal, then rest** | One action that ends on the real photo and holds | Our Dina's pepper grinder | Food, products, the moment something is finished | Clean last frame, no unfinished action; account for the end state |
| **Reveal, then gentle loop** | The above, followed by a subtle ongoing loop | Dina's grinder then coffee steam (REQ-470) | When one image has both a moment and an ambient element | The loop must be quieter than the reveal |
| **Ping-pong place shot** | A slow camera move along the shopfront or room, forward then back | 3L shopfront | Businesses where the place is the brand (barbers, cafés, shops) | Wrong for anything that visibly runs backwards (fire, pouring, falling) |
| **Ambient band** | A shallow strip of motion with a call to action over it, not full-screen | Couverture & The Garbstore ("Visit our store") | Shops that want people to visit | Keep it a strip; text sits over the calmest part |
| **Craft clip** | Hands and tools in a tight crop, trimmed to the best 4–8 s | Benchmark (B&W hands turning and chiselling), Wwake (knotting a chain), Passenger (pour-over) | Makers, food, trades | Wide lifestyle footage weakens a "handmade" claim (Moots, Stinner) |
| **Scroll-scrubbed moment** | The visitor's scrolling plays one gesture forward | Hepple (lemon twist over a martini) | One premium moment, handled carefully | Pinned scrolling borders on scroll-jacking (NN/g); use at most one |
| **Drawn brand motion** | The logo or signature draws itself on; illustrated loops | Onyx signature, Fernweh Lottie, Mikkeller tiles, XGD SVG effect | Brands with a strong mark or illustration style | Plays once; small file size |
| **Hover-to-play** | Still until the pointer is over it | Mikkeller tiles (from markup, unverified); our Lagrange Foundry illustrations (REQ-470) | Grids of illustrations or products | On touch, play once when it comes into view |
| **Trailer loop + Play button** | A short silent loop, with the full film (with sound) one click away | Ólafur Arnalds | Musicians, venues, anyone with a real film | Never use the full film as the background |
| **Entrance reveals** (baseline) | Headings and images fade or rise in as they appear | Discovery Sea Kayaks (WOW.js), Stinner (AOS) | Everyone, quietly | Applied to every heading it feels templated (Gold Rush); 100–500 ms, ease-out |

## 3. The taste tests, revised by the survey

1. **The motion carries the story or the brand.** *Confirmed.* Lune's lasers show no croissant, Stinner's launch film shows no frame-building, and Gypsy Floral's drone shot sells the videographer. Crust Bikes adds one extension: for a shop with a strong look, **the brand's aesthetic can be the story** when the product isn't shown.
2. **It's clearly real or clearly art, never the uncanny middle; and real mustn't be dull.** *Confirmed and sharpened.*
   - Real must also look **chosen**: graded, cropped, trimmed. A reposted social clip isn't enough.
   - "Uncanny" includes camera gimmicks, not just AI. Hepple's tiny-planet drone shot is an example.
   - Photoreal AI is acceptable only when it animates **the client's own** subject faithfully (Throne fails this).
   - Footage that barely moves, at 15 MB each, is dull for its cost. Mork-Ulnes: a still image would cost about 1% as much.
3. **It respects the page.** *Refined:* "**one motion language**" rather than strictly "one signature effect". 3L runs two loops successfully because they share one grammar: black and white plus one warm colour. The baseline stays **one signature effect plus entrance reveals**. A second motion is allowed only in the same language and away from the first, never competing loops in view at once. Text contrast is best protected by **fading the clip into the page background** under the text (3L), better than a flat dark overlay (Throne's 40%). Saint Frank's 0.2→0.65 gradient is the fallback.
4. **New: it still works when the motion never plays.** On iOS Low Power Mode, with reduced motion on, or while a slow connection is still loading, the visitor sees only the poster. So the poster must be the real photo and tell the story on its own. Our end-on-the-original reveals already guarantee this.

## 4. Don'ts, with the site that shows each

- **Don't put a whole film in the background.** Saint Frank 133 s, Tim Wendelboe 54 s, Fogo 75 s. Nobody watches past the first few seconds; cut 4–8 s and link the film.
- **Don't ship heavy files:** over ~3 MB for the hero, or 1080p/60 fps sent to phones (Birds 61 MB).
- **Don't put an intro gate or splash video in front of hours and menu** (Lune).
- **Don't put motion in the navigation or on every section** (Wwake, Onyx, Fogo).
- **Don't use photoreal AI of a place or person that isn't the client's** (Throne).
- **Don't use reposted social reels or screen recordings as hero footage** (Temperance, Daydream).
- **Don't slow clips to half speed or filter the colour away** (Gypsy Floral 0.5×, Paper Crane's filter turns tattoos grey).
- **Don't run motion on a stale page.** Rye Wax's glitch logo loops above "SHOP CLOSED TEMPORARILY". Motion draws attention to neglect.
- **Don't scroll-jack or drag-scroll for its own sake** (NN/g; Ólafur Arnalds' drag gallery).
- **Don't use sound** (unchanged), and remove the audio track from the file, not just the playback.
- **Don't strobe or glitch past three flashes per second** (WCAG 2.3.1). Rye Wax measured safe; check any glitch effect.

## 5. Evidence beyond taste (cited)

- **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A):** motion that starts automatically, lasts more than 5 s and sits alongside other content needs a way to pause, stop or hide it. A mood loop doesn't count as "essential". Honouring reduced motion alone does **not** satisfy it. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html
- **SC 2.3.1 Three Flashes (A):** https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html. **SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA)** covers parallax and scroll reveals: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html
- **prefers-reduced-motion:** web.dev suggests a reduced version might stop autoplaying video, and recommends animating only under `no-preference`. https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion
- **LCP:** a hero `<video>` counts as LCP at whichever comes first, its poster or its first frame. Good LCP is ≤2.5 s at p75, so the poster needs to be small and high priority. https://web.dev/articles/lcp. Lazy-load videos below the fold: https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video
- **NN/g:** UI animation 100–500 ms, ease-out for entering, never linear (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/); scroll-jacking is risky (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/); parallax costs usability (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/parallax-usability/); don't auto-advance carousels (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding/). No NN/g or Baymard study on autoplay hero video was found.
- **Mobile autoplay:** iOS allows muted (or audio-less) `autoplay playsinline` and pauses it off-screen (https://webkit.org/blog/6784/new-video-policies-for-ios/). Chrome always allows muted autoplay (https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay). The claim that iOS Low Power Mode blocks autoplay is based on a WebKit bug report only (https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=216887), so treat it as likely but unconfirmed.

## 6. Implications for the product (for the operator to decide; nothing here is built)

- **WCAG 2.2.2 and loops.** An indefinite `loop` clip with no pause control fails Level A. Options for the L1 `video` node (REQ-461/REQ-470):
  - (a) the renderer adds a small, unobtrusive pause/play control to every `loop` clip;
  - (b) loops stop after ≤5 s of total motion, resting on the poster, with `restMs` as the pattern;
  - (c) both.
  `once` reveals of ≤5 s and hover-triggered clips already comply.
  **Operator decision (2026-10-26):** endless loops get a small pause button that appears on mouse-over (option a, hover-revealed).
- **Size budget becomes a hard check, not a hope:** 720p, H.264, no audio track, ≤~3 MB for the hero, a poster always present and small, lazy-loading below the fold. The processing step in REQ-220/REQ-461 should enforce this.
- **Reduced motion: poster only.** This is already in REQ-461 §C; the survey shows it's rare, so it is a real advantage.
- **Seamless loops are a selling point.** The survey found hard-cut loops almost everywhere, so a seamless loop of the client's own photo is something we can genuinely talk about.
- **Text over clips:** prefer fading the clip into the page background under the text, with a gradient overlay as the fallback.
