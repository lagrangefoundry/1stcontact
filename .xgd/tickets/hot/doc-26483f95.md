# The Contact: one entity, three axes, and the words for them

## Why this document exists

The product is called 1st Contact and the word *contact* had no settled meaning.
One population currently carries four names across four layers:

| Layer | Noun |
| --- | --- |
| the table | `users` |
| the model ([[DOC-42]] §4) | Contact / Member / Operator / Entitled |
| the code | `Person`, `people`, `peopleOf`, `PersonDetail` |
| the tab | Contacts |

Each was chosen well for its own surface and none was chosen against the others.
This document is the authority; [[DOC-40]] §2 and [[DOC-42]] §4 defer to it where
they disagree.

## 1. A user is not an entity

**User** means anyone who uses the site. They may or may not be a contact; they
may or may not be able to sign in. **There are no rows for users** and there is no
`users` table in the model this document describes.

[[DOC-40]] §2 currently says *"**User** — a person, identified by a verified email
address, scoped to a tenant"*, and that is the definition of a **Member** below.
The noun is retired at the model level and survives only in its ordinary English
sense.

**Falsifier:** a table, column or type named for users.

## 2. A contact is the entity

**Contact** — someone or something we hold at least an email address for, and
usually more. It is the row. Everything else in this document is a fact *about* a
contact.

**A contact may be a person or an organisation.** This is not a detail: it decides
what a name is. A person has a given name, a family name, perhaps a title and a
suffix ([[CHAT-38]]); an organisation has one name and none of that structure.
Modelling only people would make every organisation a person with an empty
surname.

This retires `Person` and `people` in the code. They assert something false about
half the population.

**Falsifier:** a required field that only makes sense for a human being.

## 3. Three axes, not one state machine

The mistake this document exists to prevent is treating these as stages of one
line. They are independent, and the combinations are all real:

| Axis | Fact | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Access** | **Member** | this contact can sign in |
| **Commercial** | **Customer** | this contact has paid us at least once |
| **Pipeline** | *(see §4)* | where they are in the relationship, and it will grow |

All four access/commercial combinations occur:

- member and customer — a paying operator of a business
- customer, **not** a member — someone who paid and never signed in, or whose
  sign-in was withdrawn
- member, **not** a customer — an invited colleague, a free account, us
- neither — most of the list

So *member* is not a later stage of *customer* and neither is a later stage of
*contact*. **Contact is the noun; member and customer are adjectives.** A contact
who is both is one row with two facts true, not a row that has advanced twice.

**Falsifier:** a single `status` column that has to hold both "can sign in" and
"has paid"; or code that infers one from the other.

### Why "Contact" cannot be the initial state

Because it is the entity's name. Using it as a value on one of the axes is a
category error — it makes *contact* mean both "every row in this table" and "a row
with nothing else true yet", and the second reading is what makes people write
queries that exclude customers from the contact list.

## 4. The pipeline axis is open

The initial state needs a name that is not *Contact*, and the set will grow.
Candidates, with what each commits to:

- **Lead** — the CRM standard. Commits to commercial intent, which is wrong for
  someone who filled in a contact form to ask a question.
- **Prospect** — the same, more strongly.
- **Enquiry** — names the event that created the row rather than the relationship.
  Accurate for form captures, odd for a contact typed in by hand.
- **Known** — neutral and true of every row, which may make it useless as a stage.

**Undecided.** It wants to be settled together with the second and third stages,
because a first stage named without knowing what follows it is named against
nothing.

## 5. Unary facts and binary relations are different things

The reason *Operator* feels unlike the others: it is not a fact about a contact at
all.

- **Contact, Member, Customer** are **unary** — true or false of the contact
  alone.
- **Operator** and **Entitled** are **binary** — true of a *pair*. Operator is
  (contact, business): this contact may run that business, held in `memberships`
  with a `role`. Entitled is (account, business): this account has been granted
  access to that thing ([[DOC-42]] §6, [[REQ-184]]).

So a contact is not "an operator". They are an operator **of a business**, and of
a different business they are not. The same person is an operator of one and a
customer of another.

Whether *Operator* survives as the word is open. What is settled is that it
belongs on a different axis from §3's, because it cannot be answered without
naming a business.

## 6. What this amends

- **[[DOC-40]] §2** — its **User** noun becomes **Member** (§1). Its **Account**,
  *"structurally a `users` row in the platform's own tenant"*, becomes a table of
  its own ([[REQ-194]]).
- **[[DOC-42]] §4** — its table of four relations stands, but it reads as one
  population in two states and §3 above replaces that with three axes. §9's
  *"contact and member are one population in two states"* is the sentence that
  most needs rewriting: one population, yes; two states, no.
- **[[REQ-188]]** — filed as a linear Contact → Invited → Member progression. Its
  *marker* reasoning survives (a member is someone who completed sign-up, not
  someone we invited), its framing does not. It needs revising against §3 and §4
  before it is built.
- **The schema** — `users` is renamed for the entity in §2, in [[REQ-190]]'s
  baseline. The code nouns follow.

## 7. Open

- The pipeline stage names (§4), first and following.
- Whether *Operator* is the right word (§5).
- Whether an organisation contact can hold person contacts — a contact *at* an
  organisation is an obvious want and a relation this document does not yet have.
- Where *Customer* is computed from. "Has paid at least once" is a fact about
  payment history, and there is no payments table yet; until there is, the axis
  exists in the vocabulary and has nothing to read.
