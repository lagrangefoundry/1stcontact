# Content Model, Rights & the Project Corpus

## 1. Purpose

Defines what material the system holds on a client's behalf, what rights attach
to each kind, where it is stored, and how the builder AI reaches it.

[[DOC-12]] settles the *site* — draft, revisions, renders. This document settles
everything **around** the site: what the client uploaded, what we downloaded on
their behalf, what we captured from the web, and how all of it becomes
searchable without becoming publishable.

Companion to [[DOC-12]] (site storage), [[DOC-13]] (capture), [[DOC-10]] (chat
and the system KB), [[DOC-36]] (data collection), [[DOC-37]] (deletion).

## 2. What this closes

- **[[DOC-13]] §10 — "IP/copyright deferred."** The park expires here. The risk
  was never storage; it is *leakage into output*, and that becomes live the
  moment reference material is visible to an AI that writes the client's site.
- **[[DOC-10]] §12 — "Attachments: a separate ticket type, or referenced by
  key?"** Answered in §7: neither. A first-class attachment record on the
  ticket, bytes in a blob store behind a port.
- **[[DOC-10]] §6.4 — "Operator-authored knowledge is not in scope here; when it
  lands it is a knowledge base in the tenant's own store."** It lands here, and
  it is *one* knowledge base, not one per kind of material (§8).

## 3. The inventory

Seven classes of material, which is the whole of what the system holds:

| # | Class | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Public rendered site | the bytes served at `/site/<slug>/` |
| 2 | Draft + revisions | the L1 definition, assets, metadata |
| 3a | The client's previous site | a capture of `oldbakery.com` |
| 3b | Reference sites | a capture of a competitor they admire |
| 3c | Downloaded background | an industry report, a news article |
| 4a | Uploaded assets | photos, logos, fonts |
| 4b | Uploaded background | positioning papers, brand guidelines |

## 4. The axes

The obvious two axes — *"is it public?"* and *"does the client own it?"* — are
both wrong, in instructive ways.

### 4.1 "Public" conflates a fact with a permission

A competitor's site is publicly viewable — on *their* domain. That says nothing
about whether **we** may serve it. The useful property is not where the material
can currently be seen but what we are permitted to do with it.

So **exposure is a consequence, never an input.** It is derived from rights and
never set independently:

| Rights | Exposure |
|---|---|
| owned / licensed | publishable |
| third-party | tenant-private, never served |

### 4.2 Ownership gives *two* independent bits, and they invert

The sharp finding. 3a and 3b are the **same technical artifact** — a [[DOC-13]]
capture bundle — and differ only in rights. But rights do not order them
consistently:

| | may copy/bytes enter a **published site**? | may it cross the **tenant boundary** into cross-client learning ([[DOC-36]] §8)? |
|---|---|---|
| **3a** their own previous site | **yes** | **no** — their business, and [[DOC-36]] §6 flags exactly this material as confidential |
| **3b** a third-party reference | **never** | **yes** — a public marketing page, structure only, feeding [[DOC-15]]'s coverage corpus |

The permissions run in opposite directions. A single `owned: bool` would be
wrong for half the cases, and a rule deriving one bit from the other would be
wrong for the same half. Therefore **both bits are stored explicitly**:

- `republishable` — may this material's bytes or verbatim copy enter a published site?
- `exportable` — may it leave the tenant as aggregate?

`rights` remains as the human-legible classification that a person set and can
audit; the two bits are what code reads.

### 4.3 The axes that survive

| Axis | Values | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| **Role** | site source / AI context / derived artifact | whether it is versioned, and where it is stored |
| **Modality** | structured / media / prose | how the AI reaches it — control surface, asset picker, or search |
| **Rights** | the two bits of §4.2 | what it may become |
| **Exposure** | derived (§4.1) | which serving path |

## 5. The classification, complete

| # | Role | Modality | `rights` | republish | export | Store | In the project KB? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | derived | rendered | — | — | — | R2 `out/` | no — derived, regenerable |
| 2 | site source | structured | owned | yes | no | D1 + R2 ([[DOC-12]] §7) | **yes — via a source adapter (§8.3)** |
| 3a | AI context | capture bundle | owned | yes | **no** | ReferenceStore ([[REQ-155]]) | yes |
| 3b | AI context | capture bundle | third_party | **no** | yes | ReferenceStore | yes — quarantined (§9) |
| 3c | AI context | prose | third_party | no | yes | ticket + attachment | yes |
| 4a | site source | media | owned/licensed | yes | no | ticket + attachment; bound by `site_assets` | yes — as its description |
| 4b | AI context | prose | mixed | partial | no | ticket + attachment | yes |

**The invariant this table exists to produce:** *capture-sourced assets may never
be promoted into a site's asset library unless the source bundle is
`republishable`.* Promoting a hero image out of a competitor's capture publishes
third-party copyright under the client's own domain. It is the most damaging
single action available in the system and it is currently one plausible tool call
away.

## 6. Everything is a ticket; the body is the text-shadow of the blob

One structural decision carries most of this document's simplicity.

**Every piece of project material is a ticket.** The heavy artifact — a PDF, a
JPEG, a font, a capture bundle — is *referenced* by the ticket. The ticket's
**body is the material's text shadow**: for a PDF, the extracted or summarised
text; for an image, a written description; for a font, what it is and when to
reach for it.

Three things follow, and each of them removes work:

1. **The modality axis disappears at the corpus layer.** The knowledge base
   indexes bodies, uniformly, and never learns that images exist. A photo is
   findable by *"the kitchen at dusk"* through the same path as a positioning
   paper. There is no second retrieval mechanism for media.
2. **The metadata requirements are already met.** The ticket store is a typed,
   versioned, multi-tenant document store with schema validation, linking,
   comments and tenancy bound into the handle. §4's axes are `fields`. No new
   store, no new schema language, no new tenancy model.
3. **The KB already knows how to read tickets.** `corpusPredicates()` renders a
   `CorpusSpec` to `type=X AND fields.y=true`. A KB over this material is a
   config entry, not an integration.

This is the same move [[DOC-10]] §8 made for chat and for the same reason: the
general-purpose component was already there.

## 7. Attachments

The one genuine gap in the substrate. The JS ticketing component's op surface is
`create / update / get / query / comment / comments / backlinks` — there is no
attachment op, and no blob concept. This is framework work, tracked as
**`lagrange-framework` REQ-104**, and this section is its
specification-by-consumer.

> Deliberately not written as a wikilink: this repository's own REQ-104 is an
> unrelated L1 layout ticket, and `[[REQ-104]]` would resolve to it. Cross-repo
> references are named by repository and id, in prose.

### 7.1 Bytes do not live in the ticket store

Established twice already in this codebase and not relitigated here:
`0001_site_store.sql` — *"Asset bytes are not [rows]: they live in R2 and this
schema holds only the pointer to them"* — and [[DOC-10]] §4.4. Attachments
follow: **metadata in the ticket store, bytes in a blob store.**

The component must not depend on R2. The blob store is a **fourth injected
seam** alongside the three the knowledge component already declares — a
`BlobStore` port (`put` / `get` / `head` / `delete`), implemented by R2 in the
Worker, by the filesystem for the file-backed store, in memory for tests. Same
shape as `IndexSource`, same shape as `SiteStore`.

### 7.2 How an attachment is referenced

**A first-class child record, by direct analogy to comments** — which the
component already homes as a sub-record keyed by parent uid and listed by an op
(`store.js` `type: 'comment'`). Attachments are that shape with bytes on the
side. Not a `fields.attachments` array, which would be unvalidated and would give
garbage collection no hook.

The blob address is **content-derived — `sha256` — within a tenant prefix**:

```
t/<tenant>/blob/<sha256>
```

Content addressing buys dedup (the same PDF uploaded twice is one blob),
permanent cacheability, and a pointer whose lifetime is independent of any one
ticket. It matches [[DOC-12]] §5.1's choice for preview snapshots.

**The tenant prefix is load-bearing, not tidiness.** A globally content-addressed
blob would be *shared* between two clients who upload the same file, which is
both an existence oracle across the tenant barrier ([[DOC-10]] §4.1) and a direct
contradiction of [[DOC-37]]: erasing client A must not delete B's bytes, but
leaving them means A's bytes survive A's erasure. Prefixing dedups within a
tenant and isolates across, which is exactly the strength each boundary wants.

### 7.3 Ordering and collection

- **Write blob first, then the record.** A crash then leaves an orphan blob,
  which a sweep collects, rather than a dangling pointer, which nothing can heal.
- **Collection is mark-and-sweep, lazily.** Delete-on-last-reference is racy
  across two stores with no shared transaction. A sweep over attachment records
  is honest and can run whenever.

### 7.4 What the component types, and what it does not

The attachment record carries **intrinsic** metadata only — `sha256`,
`content_type`, `size`, original `filename`, `created_at` — plus a `meta` map
validated by the host's TypePack.

`rights`, `republishable` and `exportable` are **1stcontact concepts and live in
`fields` on the ticket**, not on the attachment. They are properties of the
*material* and its classification, not of a byte range, and a general component
should not learn our copyright model.

**Text extraction is not the component's job.** Turning a PDF into a body is a
1stcontact ingestion step (§10). The component stores bytes and metadata.

## 8. Two knowledge bases

### 8.1 A KB is a view, not a folder

The temptation is one KB per kind of material — transcripts, brief, business
background, references, site. That is wrong, and the reason is structural rather
than a matter of taste:

- **Membership overlaps.** `search.js` filters `row.kbs` — a *list*. A document
  belongs to every KB whose predicate matches it. "Which KB does this live in?"
  is not a question the model asks.
- **The corpus is a predicate over fields.** Any view we might want later is one
  config line over data that never moves. There is no migration cost to
  deferring a split, so splitting early buys nothing and costs specificity.
- **Re-indexing is already incremental.** `corpusPredicates(spec, {since})`
  restricts to `updated_at >= cursor` and re-embeds idempotently, so "this
  material changes more often" was never a reason to separate corpora.
- **The default is all-KB co-ranked search**, with `kb` an optional scope. Two
  KBs that are always searched together are one KB with extra bookkeeping.

The kinds of material are therefore **ticket types and fields** (§9), and the
knowledge bases are two:

| KB | Corpus | Landscape | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `system` | the shipped design documents | authored | `shipped` — above tenancy ([[DOC-10]] §6.2.1) |
| `project` | `chat`, `material`, `reference`, `brief` + the site adapter | derived | the tenant's store |

The division is real because the two differ in everything that matters: `system`
is identical for every tenant, read-only, indexed at release; `project` is
tenant data, written continuously, and indexed incrementally.

### 8.2 Weight is max-over-KBs, so down-weighting requires exclusion

Worth recording because it is invisible until it bites. Ranked search computes
`kbWeight = Math.max(...inScope.map(name => scope.get(name).weight))`.

**Adding a document to a second KB can only ever raise its rank.** A "low-weight"
KB has no effect on a document that is also in a normal-weight one. Any future
down-weighting therefore requires the corpus predicates to be *disjoint* — which
is a design constraint on the split, not a knob discovered afterwards.

### 8.3 The site is in `project`, and does not become tickets

The site's own copy is the one member likely to pollute co-ranking: it is a
condensed, punchy restatement of the client's positioning material, which is
precisely the profile that wins cosine similarity. *"What do we say about
pricing"* risks returning the pricing page and crowding out the paper explaining
why.

That is an **empirical** question and is answered with measurement, following the
precedent [[DOC-10]] §6.3 set. Until there is evidence: site material sits in
`project` carrying `fields.origin=site`. If pollution is measured, the split is
`project` gaining `NOT fields.origin=site` plus a `site` KB at reduced weight —
disjoint, per §8.2, and one config change.

**The site does not become tickets.** A KB declaring a `source` reads from a
named store rather than the project store, so the site enters the corpus through
a **source adapter** presenting pages through the doc-reader contract,
projecting text on read. This avoids the two costs of materialising page
tickets: write amplification on every keystroke-settle, and a parallel copy of
the definition that can drift from it or be written back into it.

The projection is **text only** — ordered visible runs plus page metadata, no
geometry, no colour, no module config — and the KB's prompt names the L1 control
surface ([[DOC-30]]) as the authority. The awareness map then tells the AI: *this
is a searchable copy of the site's words; to read or change structure, use the
control surface.*

## 9. Types and fields

A type exists where the **shape** differs; everything else is a field.

| Type | Why a type | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `doc` | shipped, tenant-free, release-built index | exists |
| `chat` | transcript in a CAS comment, summary in body | exists ([[DOC-10]] §8) |
| `material` | one blob attachment, rights, AI-written body | **new** |
| `reference` | a capture *bundle*, re-extractable | **new** |
| `brief` | one per site, canonical decisions | **new** |

`material` covers 3c, 4a and 4b. An uploaded PDF, a fetched industry report and
an uploaded photo are one shape — *a blob with a text shadow and a rights
record* — separated by `kind`, not by type.

`reference` is separated from `material` because its attachment is a
multi-member bundle rather than one blob, and because it has a lifecycle of its
own: [[DOC-13]] §9's *"capture once, re-map forever"*, re-extraction, and gap
logging into the module backlog.

`brief` is a type but mostly **not a retrieval target**. It is small and always
relevant, so it belongs *inlined in the priming context*, not fetched by search.
Its presence in the `project` corpus is harmless and occasionally useful.

Fields on `material` and `reference`:

```
rights:        owned | licensed | third_party
republishable: bool          # §4.2 — may copy/bytes reach a published site
exportable:    bool          # §4.2 — may it leave the tenant as aggregate
origin:        uploaded | captured | fetched | site
kind:          document | image | font | capture
source_url:    string        # for captured and fetched material
```

## 10. Ingestion

Upload is not a write; it is a small pipeline, because the body has to exist
before the material is worth anything to the AI:

1. **Store the blob** (§7.3 ordering).
2. **Classify** — `kind` from the content type; `rights` per §11's open question.
3. **Shadow** — extract text from a document, describe an image, identify a font.
   This is the step that makes the material findable, and it is ours, not the
   ticketing component's.
4. **Create the ticket** with the shadow as body and §9's fields.
5. **Index incrementally** — the `since` cursor picks it up; no rebuild.

The awareness map is rebuilt on a slower cadence than the vector index. It
describes *territories*, and a new report does not move one.

## 11. Quarantine

Rights are enforced at the **write** path, not the read path.

Excluding third-party material from retrieval would defeat the purpose — the AI
is *supposed* to look at references; that is why we captured them. What must
not happen is third-party copy or bytes reaching a published site. So:

- Every search hit carries its ticket's `republishable` bit, and the prompt
  states the constraint: **form, never content**.
- The **detectable event** is a control-surface call whose text is verbatim from
  a non-republishable source. Text enters the site through one documented API
  ([[DOC-30]]), which makes an n-gram check against the quarantined corpus a
  real mechanical gate rather than an exhortation — the same *structural rather
  than disciplinary* character as tenant scoping bound into the store handle.
- **Asset promotion** is gated outright by §5's invariant.

v1 ships the prompt-level constraint and the asset gate. The n-gram check is a
follow-up, but the seam belongs in the design now.

## 12. Reference lifetime

*Do we even keep reference sites?* Yes — in two places, with different lifetimes,
and separating them is what makes the answer clean.

- **In the tenant: bounded.** The bundle is working material during the build.
  What is durable is the *distillate* — the [[DOC-9]] Brief entry recording
  "kept the emergency-callout framing from B, rejected A's pricing grid". Keep
  the Brief entry indefinitely; garbage-collect the bundle on a window.
- **Internally: permanent.** [[DOC-15]]'s coverage program wants captures as a
  corpus for the module backlog. That is not tenant data, is never served, and
  survives tenant deletion because it crossed the boundary as aggregate
  ([[DOC-36]] §8) — which is exactly what `exportable` licenses.

This also gives [[DOC-37]] a clean answer: a third-party capture registers as
*no-identity*, and the tenant's copy is erasable without destroying the internal
learning derived from it.

## 13. Open questions

- **Where the rights bits are set.** Capture and upload time is the only honest
  point, but it puts a legal question in front of a novice ([[DOC-35]] §5.2's
  register problem). The alternative is inferring from whether the URL matches
  their declared domain and asking only when it does not. Undecided.
- **Whether `reference` earns its own type**, or is `material` with
  `kind: capture` and a bundle-valued attachment (§9). Decided here in favour of
  separation; a close call, and cheap to reverse before either type ships.
- **Whether site copy pollutes co-ranking** (§8.3). Answered with measurement.
- **Blob size ceiling and upload transport** — direct-to-R2 presigned PUT versus
  through the Worker. Not settled here; it is the framework ticket's business.
