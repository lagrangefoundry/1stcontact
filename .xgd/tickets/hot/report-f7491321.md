### Why this overlap is acceptable

The two stories touch the same *sentence* ("the assistant can only act on one
site") from opposite sides, but they assert different propositions at different
observation points, and neither restates the other's evidence.

**The guarantee is located wholly in CAP-90.** It is produced by two facts the
conversation host owns: a turn is addressed to a conversation rather than a site
(AC-1053 — naming a site instead is refused as malformed before any turn
begins), and two sites are two independent conversations whose drafts and
transcripts do not cross (AC-1056). The binding is fixed once when the
conversation is opened; nothing above the host names a site.

**CAP-92 asserts nothing about site identity.** Reading all twelve ACs
(AC-1071…AC-1082) confirms this by exhaustion rather than by inference:

- AC-1073 closes and enumerates the write set — *which operations exist*.
- AC-1074 / AC-1075 gate the grant and the read/write effect — *which
  operations a consumer can reach*.
- AC-1079 records per call the surface, operation, effect, arguments, policy
  decision and outcome — site identity is not a checked scope in the record.
- AC-1081 governs how a place *within a page* is addressed and how long that
  address lasts — intra-page, not inter-site.

CAP-92 therefore constrains *what may be called*; it never constrains *what it
is called against*. That is exactly the division both capability bodies
declare, and both story bodies name the other explicitly out of scope.

### The one straddling AC, and why it stays put

AC-1058 ("The assistant is offered only granted site operations: no filesystem
operation, and no operation that names a site") is the sole criterion that
mentions both halves, and was the likely trigger for the survey flag. It belongs
in CAP-90:

- Its distinctive assertion is that **no offered operation accepts a site as a
  parameter**, so acting on the wrong site "is not a mistake available to it" —
  a property of the *primed toolset assembled for one open conversation*, which
  CAP-90's scope names verbatim ("Priming — what the assistant is told about who
  it is and what it can reach, assembled from the operations it was actually
  granted").
- Its verification observes what a running turn was handed, plus that the
  priming names the site under work — an observation only reachable inside a
  conversation.
- Its "exactly those its grant allows" clause *consumes* CAP-92's guarantee
  rather than duplicating it. The distinct CAP-92 proposition — an operation can
  be fully declared yet withheld, absent from the manual, and refused as an
  audited capability decision — is AC-1074, which asserts the declaration/grant
  separation and the audit record. No evidence is shared between the two.

### Design decision on record

STORY-103's Technical Context makes the boundary deliberate rather than
incidental. REQ-127 withdrew its own earlier clause making the site binding a
**declared scope predicate** — which would have placed the guarantee in CAP-92 —
on the stated argument that a predicate hands the model a site parameter it must
get right on every call, re-opening an error class that does not currently
exist. The binding was *located in the session* instead. Moving either story, or
migrating AC-1058 to CAP-92, would reverse a recorded and reasoned intent
decision and reintroduce the error class it was made to eliminate.
