## Notes for the Editor

**Finding 1 is the one that matters, and it is now two cycles old.** REPORT-1316
raised it on 2026-08-05 as a by-inspection observation and recommended the
`ac-edit`. Nothing changed: `render.ts` still has no enum guard, and AC-685 ¶1
still makes the unqualified claim. This cycle it is demonstrated — the emitter
really does emit `.l1-0 { border: 2px solid; } body { display: none } .pwn { … }`
from an unvalidated document. It is a **documentation defect, not a live
vulnerability**: `validateL1` rejects that document and Layer 1 is in the
production path (`packages/site-schema/src/schema.ts` embeds `l1DocumentSchema`
in the page schema, so `loadOrThrow` → `cmdRender` validates before render). The
cheap, policy-consistent repair is the `ac-edit`; the expensive one is hardening
ten enum sinks. Either is fine — but leaving the AC claiming a guarantee the code
does not provide is what should not survive another cycle, because that sentence
is the one a future reader will trust when deciding whether a new sink needs a
guard.

**Findings 2 and 3 are one repair.** Three `if (!HAVE_CHROMIUM) return` sites in
`reconciliation-nowrap-width-floor.test.ts` turn an unrun arm into a reported
pass. The fix is mechanical (`it.runIf`, as the substrate file already does) and
converts a silently-overstated green into an honest skip. Worth doing before the
engine situation in Info 11 is fixed, not after — otherwise the pattern stays and
the next environment regression is invisible again.

**Findings 5–8 are all blocked on decisions the ac level already made.** Finding
5 and 6 wait on REPORT-1670 Finding 5 (whether AC-930/AC-932 cede the retrofit to
STORY-97); Finding 7 waits on REPORT-1670 Finding 7 (deleting AC-686 ¶2); Finding
8 waits on REPORT-1670 Finding 4 (narrowing AC-716). **Doing any of them as test
edits now is wasted work** — in each case the correct repair is an `ac-edit`
upstream, after which the test is either already right or trivially trimmed.

**Finding 9 must be applied regardless of every other decision.** It is pure
prose, it has now survived three cycles, and one instance of it prints in the
test runner's own output on every run.

**What is genuinely clean, and it is most of the capability.** Seventy-two of
these ACs were assessed here for the first time and they hold up: real
`validateSite` + real `cmdRender` against an on-disk site (AC-849), the real
motion driver executed under a modelled IntersectionObserver with a genuine
scroll-jump scenario (AC-826), real JSDOM navigation and focus drives
(AC-839/843/845), the real Astro SSR container through a real workspace render
(AC-834), the real conformance harness with a throwing-core fixture (AC-703), and
the real census/retrofit commands over staged sites (AC-930/932). Assertion
density is high (median 12 per UAT, several above 100 per file) and the low-count
outliers are loop-driven tables, not thin tests. Internal mocking appears exactly
once in 97 UATs (Finding 4). AC-685 ¶2, the violation REPORT-1316 opened last
cycle, is now properly closed by a dedicated test carrying payloads in a gradient
stop, three colour axes, a background-image URL, a mask, a transform and two
font-face entries, asserting brace balance and absence rather than escaping.
