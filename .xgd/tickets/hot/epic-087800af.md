## Why

Operator, 2026-10-31: long work (a first draft takes about an hour) shouldn't need the client to sit and watch. "Notifications may be necessary — 'I have everything I need — I will take about an hour to build your first draft. I will send you an email when it's ready for you to see…'"

Operator, 2026-11-01: "We need a notifications system that allows different parts of the system to notify the owner and/or delegates when something happens" — e.g. (1) a long-running job finished, (2) a site form filled in by a site visitor, (3) a site failure or outage. **This epic provides the internal API those parts call; it does not build the triggers.** Channels: email now, push (when there is an app), maybe SMS.

**Pivot, operator 2026-11-04:** "owners are just level 1 users, notifications should go to level 1 and level 2 users." And: "The message needs to be editable/brandable by the site owner with AI support. This is not a launch requirement I guess, but I want to think ahead a little. I imagine this as a new tab 'Messages'." The epic is now **every message a business sends to its people, and when** — of which "notify the owner" is one case.

## The model: one business, writing to one person connected to it

(Level vocabulary per REQ-170: Alice is a 1st Contact customer — level 1; Bob is a customer of Alice's business — level 2.)

- "Your draft is ready" to Alice is a message the **1st Contact business** sends to its customer; it is edited in the 1st Contact business's Paperwork tab.
- "Thanks, we got your enquiry" to Bob is a message **Alice's business** sends to its customer; Alice edits it in her Paperwork tab.
- So there is no separate "owner notification" system and "buyer email" system. A message belongs to the business that sends it, and goes either to that business's own people (owner, delegates) or to its contacts. `templates.ts` already works this way (copy lives in each business's own ticket store; 1st Contact is just one business).

## The Paperwork tab

Three panes — **Items · Detail · AI chat** (the "Email assistant"). Items lists two kinds of thing:

**Templates** — the look. Generated and edited by the AI in **L1**, rendered to email through the existing L1 email target (`site-schema/src/l1/email.ts` allow-list, `framework/src/l1/email-render.ts`, from REQ-247). A template has a slot the message's content fills. A business may have **more than one**; it starts with **one default**. Restyling a template rebrands every message that uses it.

**Messages** — the words and the rules. Each message has:
- **Subject** and **email text as markdown**, user-editable; rendered through the safe sink (no raw HTML passes through), with placeholders from a closed list per message. Some placeholders are **required** and cannot be deleted (e.g. the sign-in link, the unsubscribe line).
- **SMS/push text** (plain, length-capped), user-editable, optional — empty means that channel isn't used.
- **Sender** — chosen from a dropdown of the business's senders (below).
- **Template** — which template it renders in (default: the business's default).
- **Trigger** — picked from a closed list of events the platform emits. System messages (sign-in, invite, lapsed, delegate) have editable copy but a fixed trigger.
- **Audience** — the business's own people (owner + delegates) or the contact the event is about.
- **On/off.**
- The **Detail pane shows a render of how it will look in the recipient's mailbox** — the message in its template, with sample data.
- **"Send to me" button** on every message's detail page (operator, 2026-11-04: "test what the email really looks like in an Inbox. Any fields should be filled with fake values"):
  - Sends the message **through the real path** — its actual sender, template and rendering — so the operator sees what a real inbox (and its spam filter) does with it.
  - Goes **only to the signed-in person's own verified address**; there is no recipient field (otherwise it is a way to mail anyone).
  - **Every placeholder is filled with fake values**, declared next to the placeholder list so the preview and the test mail show the same thing. They are plainly fake (e.g. "Sam Sample", `@example.com` addresses), and **never real customer data**.
  - Links point somewhere harmless; a test never mints a real sign-in token or unsubscribe record.
  - The subject is marked as a test, it is recorded as a test send rather than a real one, it does not count towards the business's hourly cap, and it has its own small rate limit.
- History comes free: replacing copy writes a new ticket and the newest wins (as templates already do), so a sent message still points at the copy that said it.

The styled HTML is **derived** (markdown + template), never stored per message — two stored versions of one email would drift.

**This replaces, not adds to,** today's three homes for message copy: `templates.ts` (invite/signin/lapsed/delegate — tickets, but no UI), `notifications.ts` (REQ-626's `job_done` copy, in code), and REQ-247's form messages (each a whole L1 page mixing words and styling — moves to message + template).

## Record of what was sent

Operator, 2026-11-04: "whenever we send emails to a contact we should record that and make it visible on their contact record. We need to store exactly the email that was sent not just a reference to the Message object that could change… perhaps message objects need to be versioned and we store a reference to the version that was sent?"

**Both, and the first half already exists (REQ-198 / REQ-267):** every send through `sendRecordedEmail` writes an `email` ticket holding the exact subject, from, to and HTML body that went out, plus delivery status; the People tab's contact detail lists them under "Messages" and shows the body in a sandboxed frame.

- **The stored copy is the evidence; the version reference is provenance.** A reference alone cannot reproduce a send: the event's values filled the placeholders, and the renderer itself changes over time. So the record keeps the full rendered email (and the SMS/push text, when those channels exist), and additionally points at the **message version** and the **template version** that produced it.
- **Messages and templates are versioned the way templates already are:** saving writes a new ticket and the newest for a key wins; old versions are never edited, so a record's references stay valid. Today the record carries `templateUid` only — it gains the message version's uid.
- **It is recorded in the sending business's store, on the recipient's contact.** Under this epic's model a notification to Alice is a 1st Contact business message, so it belongs on Alice's contact in the 1st Contact business (today REQ-626 records it in Alice's own business's store — to move when the Paperwork tab lands).
- **Test sends ("Send to me") are recorded as tests and appear on no contact record.**

## Senders (REQ-663)
Operator, 2026-11-07: "the emails will come from the domain the site is published on and the owner can configure a set of predefined send from emails. These can be used arbitrarily to be the sender for different emails. Sender configuration probably belongs in the Paperwork tab."
- A **Senders** list per business in the Paperwork tab; any sender can be used for any message, one is the default.
- The **domain part is fixed** — a domain the business's site is published on, once sending from it is verified (REQ-259); the **local part is free**.
- The UI **recommends against** local parts that tend to route to spam, and points out that replies go to that address — so it should be a real mailbox, or the sender carries a reply-to.
- A site only on its `<name>.1stc.site` address sends from the platform address under the business's name. Attaching a custom domain turns sending from it on automatically (the REQ-259 toggle goes). Operator, 2026-11-07: "OK", "Yes".
- REQ-660 does not change who mail is from; senders arrive with REQ-663.

## Email lists (later, same interface)

Operator: "We will (eventually) support email lists and sending messages to those — I think that will use this same interface, perhaps this is a different kind of message." Agreed: a **broadcast** is a message whose trigger is "sent by a person, now or scheduled" and whose audience is a list. Templates, senders, editor, preview and AI chat are shared. What differs: recorded consent per contact; an unsubscribe line and the business's postal address are mandatory (marketing-mail law); volume limits; and broadcasts should go out on a separate sending stream from transactional mail, so marketing complaints can't hurt delivery of sign-in and enquiry mail.

## Delivery layer (`notify()`, REQ-626 — landed)

- **One internal API, `notify()`**, that any part of the platform calls (consultant tool, host, form capture, monitoring). Callers say *what happened* (a closed `kind`), *where to go* (a link), and options; the system decides *who* and *how*. With the Paperwork tab, a `kind` becomes a trigger and its copy comes from the business's message for that trigger.
- **Recipients:** the business's owner and live delegates (REQ-475's members) at a verified address; anyone without one is skipped. Callers never supply addresses. (As landed, REQ-626 sends to the people the caller names by id, while they are owner or live delegate.)
- **The link is the caller's**; the system only checks it points into this platform's builder for that business (`/b/<businessId>/…`), so an AI-callable path can't mail arbitrary links.
- **Options:** *sign in automatically* (single-use magic link, expires after 24 hours — for immediate use) and *sender* (1st Contact or the business's sending domain). Job done uses both: auto sign-in on, sent as 1st Contact.
- **Channels behind one interface:** email first (through `sendRecordedEmail`, one recorded message per recipient); push and SMS plug in later without changing callers.
- **Guards:** at most one per dedupe key, a per-business hourly cap, an unsubscribe link that records a per-person opt-out. No private customer data in bodies (DOC-5 rule 26).

## Review of the shop's emails against this design (2026-11-07, after REQ-644)

REQ-644 added seven order messages. Triggers, audiences and senders fit the model; the copy, look and record do not yet.

**Fits:** owner/team mail goes through `notify()` (`new_order`, `cancellation_requested` — Alice's business writing to its own people); buyer mail is sent in the business's name (its verified domain, else the platform address under the business's name) with reply-to the owner; each buyer kind (`confirmation`, `shipped`, `cancelled`, `cancel_declined`, `return_refund`) maps one-to-one to a platform event, i.e. a trigger; test orders are marked `[Test]`; buyer data goes only to the buyer and the team.

**Deviates:**
- **A fourth home for copy.** Buyer wording is a `switch` in `order-emails.ts` (`copyFor`), and two more kinds were added to `NOTIFY_KINDS` — beside `templates.ts` and REQ-247's L1 pages.
- **No look.** Bodies are hand-assembled `<p>`/`<ul>` strings — no template, no branding.
- **Not on a contact.** Buyer mail is recorded with `contactId = order.id`; a buyer is not a contact, so nothing shows on the People tab. No message or template version is recorded (`templateKey: order:<kind>`, no `templateUid`).
- **On/off lives in Settings → Shop** ("Email the buyer when an order ships"), not on the message.

**New design point the shop surfaces:** markdown can't express an order's line items, totals or address. Messages need **block placeholders** — platform-rendered structured blocks (e.g. `{{order_summary}}`, `{{facts}}`, `{{shipping_address}}`) that sit in the markdown like a paragraph, each with fake sample values for preview and "Send to me".

## Assistant wiring (agreed with the operator, 2026-11-07)

Patterned on the Settings assistant (REQ-239): a role of its own, a tool declaration whose prose sits beside each operation (the manual is projected from it, never hand-written), and **one API, two callers** — the pane and the assistant both call the same routes; neither drives the other.

**Two roles, two conversations:** the **Email assistant** and the **Legal assistant**. Their grants differ (change vs view only), and a session told about a capability it was not granted offers it. Each mode's chat pane opens its own role; both reuse the host's per-business manager, budget, spend metering and interrupted-turn handling.

**1. Tools.**
- *Email* (surface `messages`). Reads: `list_messages`, `read_message` (all fields, placeholders, which are required, their fake values), `list_templates`, `read_template`, `list_triggers` (closed list, placeholders each offers), `list_senders`, `preview` (the rendered email with fake values, as a picture), `list_changes(since)`. Changes: `update_message` (subject, markdown, SMS text, sender, template, on/off), `create_template` / `update_template` (L1 email document, `validateL1` with the email allow-list), `set_default_template`, `revert_message` / `revert_template` (to an earlier version), `send_test` ("Send to me": only to the signed-in person, same rate limit — operator: "Yes - nice feature"). Declared refusals: a required placeholder removed, a placeholder the trigger doesn't offer, a sender not on the list, SMS text over length, raw HTML.
- *Legal* (surface `legal`, view only): `list_documents` (required set; current published version and any draft of each), `read_document(version)`, `list_versions`, `acceptance_summary` (counts only, never names), `list_changes(since)`. No write of any kind.
- **Neither reads sent mail or contacts** — those hold buyer and lead data (DOC-5 rule 26). Nothing sends to anyone but the signed-in person.

**2. Priming** (`email_priming`, `legal_priming` in `priming.json`, the settings shape): a written **role entry**, the **projected manual**, and the **business line** (read per turn).
- Email role: a business writes to its own people or its contacts; messages are words, templates are the look; HTML is derived, so a template change restyles every message using it; triggers are fixed (system messages can't change trigger); required placeholders stay; transactional only, not marketing; **changes are live on save** and reach the next real email, so offer a test send; it never sees customer data.
- Legal role: what the required sets are (level 1 / level 2); publishing a new version makes everyone accept again; the Privacy Notice is "I have read", not "I agree"; it explains what a document says, is not a lawyer and says so when asked for advice; the client edits in the pane, it cannot.

**3. Reminders** (per turn, short — substance lives in priming):
- **Changes the client made** — the `site.changes` pattern: the host counts pane edits to messages/templates (Legal: a draft edited or a version published) since the assistant's last turn and, if any, says "call `list_changes` with since: … before you touch anything, and never write over a change you have not read." The other direction: a `messages_changed` event (shape of `BUSINESS_CHANGED`) makes the pane refresh when the assistant saves.
- **What the client is looking at** — the message, template or document open in Detail, so "make this friendlier" resolves.
- **Must not forget** — Email: "After a change, say which messages it affects (a template affects all that use it) and offer a test send." "Say what a change does to real emails before making it." Legal: "You cannot change documents and do not give legal advice." "Before the client publishes, say that publishing makes everyone accept again." Plus the shared session-budget and interrupted-turn signals.

**Decisions:** edits are **live on save** (operator: "Keep it simple - live on save - as we discussed this gives messages a different lifecycle than the rest of the site") — every save is a version, revertable, and "Send to me" is the check; no draft/publish. The assistant may run "Send to me". The Legal assistant sees acceptance counts.

**Acceptance count (definition):** for each required document, among the people who must accept it — level 1: the business's members (owner and delegates); level 2: contacts who have signed in to the portal (a lead-form visitor never has to accept anything) — how many have accepted the **current** version, how many an **older** version only, and how many **none** (never, or withdrawn). From `user_acceptances` (`document_uid` names the version accepted), one grouped query per business. Used for "has everyone accepted the new Terms yet?" and "if you publish, these 14 people will be asked to accept again".
