## Symptom (operator, 2026-11-05, a first-draft run)

- **The consultant built the first draft without raising hero motion.** The operator had to bring it up ("adding motion to still images is one of our key differentiators; ideally a feature of your first draft"). No `signature_effect` decision was recorded at any point in the conversation.
- **The first clip was too subtle to notice.** It was a faint stir of leaves on a food photo; the operator didn't see anything move. Only after the operator proposed a reveal (the dish lifted into frame from one corner, ending on the original photo, then a faint loop) did the effect land. The operator called the result "excellent".
- The operator can't be in every conversation to suggest these. Reveals made this way across several test sites (an object brought into frame, an ingredient ground over a dish then steam rising, sparks that settle) are among the most impressive things the product does.

## Cause

1. **REQ-638's DOC-82 cutover (7fe13f6b52) removed BUG-306's first-build hold, and nothing replaced it.** None of the nine plan pieces names the effect. All that's left is one line in the first-draft playbook ("Offer it when it helps; no motion is a fine answer"), and the consultant passed over it, as it did before BUG-304 and BUG-306.
2. **The video tool's guidance says the whole clip should be subtle** (`videoGuidance()`: "the movement subtle — steam, a flicker, a slow drift"). The playbook's own shape is an eye-catcher that plays once, then a subtle loop. The model followed the tool and made the subtle part only.

## What

**1. The effect is an explicit conversation, inside "The look".** This follows DOC-82: a plan piece, not a build hold.
- The `look` piece's description covers the hero's movement, so agreeing the look includes it.
- The consultant raises the effect in its own words, either while settling the look or when it shows the first draft. It proposes something specific to *this* client's hero photo: what enters or moves, in what order, ending on their own photo, then the quiet loop. It offers two or three options and says which it picked. It doesn't wait for an answer before building.
- **The consultant chooses; the client can decline.** Unless the client has already said no to motion, the consultant picks the effect it thinks best for the hero photo and builds it into the first draft. It records the pick with `set_decision signature_effect` as **defaulted** (its choice, no client words needed). When the client is shown the draft, the consultant says in plain words: what moves, the other options it considered, and that the effect comes off whenever they like. It never asks them to approve it first.
- **If the client declines** ("no motion", "take it off"), the hero goes back to the still photo and the decision is **parked**, with their words. Nothing asks again. If they pick another option, it's **chosen**. Declining, or ignoring the question, is always fine: nothing waits on their answer.
- **No refusal and no hold (operator, 2026-11-05: "forcing a decision feels harsh").** The host doesn't refuse `set_stage look` or the first build over the effect. The safeguard is the default itself: the first-draft brief includes the consultant's pick unless the decision is parked. So a consultant that never raises the subject still ships an effect the client can see and remove, rather than none.
- Under `bc` the operator can answer for the client, as before.

**2. Examples to show the client.**
- The consultant can show the client a short **showcase** of effects made for other sites. Each item has the still, the clip, and one line saying what moves.
- It picks the two or three closest to this client's kind of photo, so the client sees what's possible rather than imagining it from a description.
- The showcase is platform material that the operator curates (add, remove, mark as shown). It isn't part of any one client's Library, and changing it needs no code change. Only items the operator has marked for showcase are ever shown; the operator is responsible for the owning business's agreement.
- No customer names or sites appear in code, tests or prompts. The items are runtime data.
- The client can play a showcase clip in the conversation.

**3. The reveal must be noticeable; only the loop is subtle.**
- **Video guidance rewritten:**
  - The reveal is the eye-catcher: a clear, nameable action that a visitor notices within the first second or two. Something enters the frame, is poured, falls, is lifted in or lights up, and the clip ends exactly on the original photo.
  - Only the loop that follows is subtle (steam, a flicker, a slow drift).
  - Reveal-then-loop is the default proposal; a loop alone is the exception.
- **The made clip's movement is measured and reported to the consultant** in the clip's result, for example how much of the picture changes over the reveal.
  - A reveal below the threshold comes back as information, not a refusal: "this moves too little for a visitor to notice (n% of the picture changes); make the action bolder".
  - The threshold is calibrated so that every showcase reveal passes and a faint-stir clip of the kind above is flagged.
  - The measurement runs wherever clips are already processed (architecture item 31: media processing in a job container, not a Worker).

## Done directly in the prompt (2026-11-05, operator: "make any prompt change directly — tickets only for code")

DOC-75 (the consultant's playbook) was edited without a code change:
- **First draft:** the signature effect is part of every first draft unless the client has said no to motion.
  - The consultant picks the effect and builds it with the draft, with no yes needed first, and records `signature_effect` as `defaulted` with its pick in `value`. The host already accepts `defaulted` for this decision.
  - It mentions the effect while discussing the look if that comes first.
  - When it shows the draft, it says what moves, one or two other ideas it considered, and that the effect comes off whenever the client likes.
  - If the client declines, the decision is parked with their words, the effect comes off, and it isn't raised again. If they prefer another idea, the decision is chosen and that one is made.
  - The playbook names this as the one exception to agreeing a clip before making it.
- **Making a clip:** the eye-catcher is a clear action that visitors see in the first second or two (something enters the frame, is poured, falls, is lifted in, or lights up). A faint stir is a loop, not an eye-catcher.
