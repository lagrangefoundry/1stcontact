## Symptom

Dev, 2026-11-03. A site consultant turn started at 17:38 UTC. A `bin/deploy --env dev` at 18:07:48 reloaded wrangler ("Reloading local server") and killed it mid-step; its last heartbeat was 18:07:44. Nearly four hours later:
- the builder still showed it running ("building"), with no notice that it had stopped;
- its `turn_log` row had no `ended_at`;
- the `ConsultantTurn` workflow instance was still on attempt 1 with its 17:38 start, and its 2-hour step timeout never fired or retried.

The host only recorded the loss (`turn_lost`, "junction: no live claim; last beat 18:07:44…") when the worker restarted around 22:04 and the panel reconnected. Until then, the client had no way to know the turn was dead short of asking someone to look at logs, and Stop's behaviour on such a turn was unverified.

## What we know

- **The kill itself is a dev artifact.** A local reload drops miniflare's in-flight workflow step and its timeout alarm. In production a deploy doesn't kill Workflows, and on prod Ariel's site a silent turn *was* closed by the watchdog ("watchdog: no sign of life for 5 min", 2026-11-03 15:13 turn).
- **The watchdog never runs in dev.** `sweepOpenTurns` (`apps/control-app/src/turn-log.ts`) runs from the `*/10 * * * *` cron in `wrangler.toml`. `wrangler dev` doesn't fire cron triggers, and nothing in `1c dev serve` / `bin/dev` invokes the scheduled handler, so in dev an open turn is never swept.
- **The client view didn't derive the loss either.** `sweepOpenTurns` says it only makes durable what every reader already derives via `turnState`, yet the panel kept showing the turn as running for hours. So the panel's read path doesn't apply the same silent-for-5-minutes rule, or doesn't re-read while it believes a turn is live. Not yet diagnosed. This part may apply to production too, in the window before the cron runs (up to ~15 minutes).

## Fix

- **A dev environment runs the same watchdog as production.** `bin/dev up` (or the dev worker itself) invokes the scheduled handler on the production cron's cadence, so dev turns are swept exactly as prod's are.
- **The client sees a dead turn as stopped without reloading.** While the panel shows a turn as running, it must notice when the host's `turnState` for that turn becomes `lost` or `restarted`, and show BUG-294's stopped notice with Carry on, within a minute or two of the 5-minute silence threshold. That holds in production as well as dev.
- **Stop works on a dead turn.** Pressing Stop on a turn whose runner is gone closes it at once and returns the panel to idle.

## Test plan

`test_UAT_FC_<ID>_*`:

## Diagnosis (2026-11-04)

- **Why the panel kept saying "building".** Since REQ-624 a consultant message is a job. The panel follows it with `followJob` (host-core), on the live prompt stream and on a rejoin (`tailSession`). `followJob` waited for the job's `job_end` and checked nothing else. The rejoin's BUG-294 lost-turn check (`buryLostTurn`) also stands aside while a job is running ("a job's death is its runner's to report"). So when the runner itself died (the dev reload killed the Workflow step, and miniflare never retried it), nothing ever wrote `job_end`, and the follower showed the turn running for as long as the connection lasted. The host's own judgement already said otherwise: `runningJob` treats a job as no longer running once it has written nothing for `JOB_SILENT_MS` (60 s) and its session has no live claim. That is why a reopen after the restart showed it lost.
- **Stop.** `stopTurn` only recorded the Stop in the inbox, for the runner to read. A dead runner never reads it, so Stop did nothing.
- **Dev cron.** `wrangler dev` never fires cron triggers. It always serves `/cdn-cgi/handler/scheduled?cron=<expr>`, though (miniflare's `unsafeTriggerHandlers`, on in wrangler dev), so `1c dev serve` can fire them itself.

## Design decisions

- **The dev cron runs inside `1c dev serve`** (the process `bin/dev up` starts for each served app), not as a separate service. It reads the crons from the frozen snapshot's own `wrangler.toml` (`[env.<env>.triggers]`, falling back to the top-level `[triggers]`, which wrangler inherits). At each UTC minute it fires every cron that matches, by requesting `/cdn-cgi/handler/scheduled?cron=<expr>` from the worker it started. The cadence is production's, read from the same config rather than restated. An app with no crons starts no ticker. A failed fire is logged and the next minute tries again.
- **A follower uses the host's existing liveness rule rather than a second one.** While following a job, `followJob` re-reads the junction about every 10 s (`LOST_CHECK_MS`). Once `runningJob` no longer names the job (silent past `JOB_SILENT_MS`, no live claim), it closes the job `lost`: the open turn is closed `lost` and a `job_end` is written carrying BUG-294's lost terminal event. The panel then shows the stopped notice with Carry on. The rejoin route already closes the ledger row on a lost end. The notice comes about a minute after the runner's last record, sooner than the 5-minute ledger threshold. It is the judgement a reopen already makes, so a live panel and a reloaded one now agree. This works the same in production.
- **Stop on a dead job closes it at once.** `stopTurn` still records the Stop. If the session's job is no longer running by `runningJob`, or a turn is open with no live claim and no job, it then closes the work `aborted` there and then: a `job_end` carrying an `aborted` done, the open turn released, and the ledger row closed `aborted`. The route answers `{ stopping: true, stopped: true }`, and the following panel reads the `job_end` and goes idle with no notice, because the client asked for the stop. A job whose runner is still alive is left to honour the Stop itself, as before.
- **Known limit.** In the first minute after a runner dies, before `runningJob` gives up on it, Stop is only recorded. The follower then closes the job `lost` when the minute is up.

## Also changed during the build

- **A job's silence is measured by its own records only.** `runningJob` used to take the newest record of *any* job on the session as the open job's last word, so an earlier job's records counted toward a later one. Now the count restarts at each `job_start`. That is what `JOB_SILENT_MS` already described ("none of its own records").
- **The live prompt route closes the ledger row when the job it followed ends `lost`.** The follower may have closed a job whose runner is gone, and that runner will never close the row. Closing it a second time, when the runner did end the job `lost` itself, is harmless.
