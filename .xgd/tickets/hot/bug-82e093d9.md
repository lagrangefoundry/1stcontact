## Symptom

Dina's Diner (chat `chat-e227e23b`, 2026-10-24). After capturing four sites and calling `add_comp` ×4 and `ask_comps`, the consultant announced the board as:

> I've put four diners on the comparison board: the other famous stop on 80 to Tahoe, the chain in Davis, an old Berkeley diner that's been kept going for forty years, and a Davis breakfast favourite. Look at them whenever you're ready, after the summary card, and tell me what you'd steal and what you'd never do. Open the board to see

The operator's verdict: "there is no clarity on WHY the user is being asked to do this or what the comparison board is." The client is asked to do a task with no reason given and no idea what the thing is. "After the summary card" is the consultant improvising directions to the board's place in Input, and it means nothing to the client.

## Root cause

The consultant followed its steering exactly. `COMP_ADDED_SAY` (`tools/generate/src/cli/ai/plan-core.ts`, returned in `add_comp`'s `say`) tells it to "tell your client in ONE line how many sites are on the comparison board … and what range they show, ending with this link exactly … Ask nothing about the sites until the client has looked." The `comps` section of `priming.json` gives a one-line example of the same shape. The why does exist, as the `ask_comps` note at the head of the board, but that is on the board, which the client has no reason yet to open.

## Fix

**Explicit supersession of BUG-245's "announced in ONE line" rule** (`tests/test_UAT_FC_BUG-245_one_voice_one_thing.workers.test.ts`, describe "comps are announced in one line…", the `toContain('ONE line')` assertion). The one-line rule is invalidated. Everything else BUG-245 established stays: one announcement for the whole board (never one per site), no pictures, addresses, site links or per-site descriptions in the conversation, the exact board link, and no question about individual sites until the client has looked.

The new announcement is **a short message of at most three sentences** that must:
1. **Say what the comparison board is:** a few real sites from the client's trade and area, gathered on the board in their Input, each one they can open and look around.
2. **Say why they are being asked:** what they like and dislike in these sites tells the consultant where their own site should sit, and the first draft starts from it.
3. **Say what to do:** on each card, write what they'd steal and what they'd never do and send it, or skip the card. End with the link exactly: `[Open the board to see](#show=comps)`.

It must not give directions to the board's position inside Input (such as "after the summary card"); the link takes them there.

Changes:
- `COMP_ADDED_SAY` in `plan-core.ts` is rewritten to require the three parts above, the at-most-three-sentence bound and the no-directions rule, keeping BUG-245's surviving rules.
- The `comps` priming section in `priming.json` (both copies) replaces its one-line example with a what/why/what-to-do example. Splice textually (see the priming.json escaping note); stay within the priming budget (BUG-129).
- `add_comp`'s description in `plan-surface.json`, which says "one line for the whole board", becomes "one short message for the whole board".

Illustrative target for Dina (not a template):

> Before I build your first draft, I've found four diners worth comparing yours with: the other famous I-80 stop, the Davis chain, a Berkeley diner kept going for forty years, and a Davis breakfast favourite. Seeing what you like and don't like in them tells me where your site should sit. On each one, tell me what you'd steal and what you'd never do, or skip it. [Open the board to see](#show=comps)
