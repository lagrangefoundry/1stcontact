## Symptom

Dina's Diner, review C1 (2026-10-24 01:24, first draft, with full pictures since
BUG-258). The home page's first body section opens "Hal's counter, since 1981",
then "Hal was a friend. I worked for him…" in the first person. Nothing on the
page has said who Dina is (her name first appears in the reviews), and "Hal's"
reads as a second business. Its pull-quote is a red filled box styled exactly
like the review cards.

The critic's verdict **praised** it: "Home tells the I-80 story with the client's
own headline, real prices and Hal's counter". Its finding C1.3 recommended
copying the red box ("the coffee line set as a pull-quote like Hal's story"). The
operator: "it feels like a bug".

It also missed the spacing all down the page, sections at five widths, Dina's face
cropped at the top of the frame, the hero photo and panel not matching in height,
and the logo size.

## Causes

1. **It reads with insider knowledge.** `criticBrief` (REQ-427) hands it the
   brief, the positioning card and **the hearted notes**, which include Hal's
   story. So it already knows who Hal is and that "I" is Dina. The rubric says
   "judge the site as a customer sees it", while giving it what no customer has.
2. **It probably cannot read the body text.** Its pictures are low-detail tiles
   (max about 768px wide). A 1280px page's 15–16px body text arrives at about 9px,
   so it reads headings and judges the rest from the notes it was given, not the
   page.
3. **The messaging lens rewards it.** "Is each claim backed by proof: their own
   photographs, names, reviews, numbers?" A named person with a history scores as
   proof. Nothing asks whether names and pronouns are introduced, whether a
   second brand is explained, or whether a quote and a review look different.
4. **The rubric hands off to checks that don't exist.** "Measured layout faults
   … are checked elsewhere; report them only when they change what a customer
   understands." Spacing scale, section widths, crops and column heights are not
   checked anywhere (REQ-429 does edges, overlap and a few others), so nobody
   judges them.
5. **At most three findings**, spent here on the band and slab, the nav and the
   Catering page.

## Fix

1. **A cold read first.** Before the informed review, the critic reads each page
   **knowing only what the page says**: no brief, no notes, no decisions. It
   answers as a first-time visitor:
   - who is this business, and who runs it?
   - who is speaking in each section?
   - what can I do here, and how?
   - what confuses me, or what is unexplained (a name, a pronoun, a second brand)?

   The informed review then compares the cold read with what the client asked
   for. Any gap is a finding, for example "a visitor cannot tell who 'I' is" or
   "'Hal's' reads as the business's name". This can be a separate short call or a
   first stage of the same one that is not given the brief; the child session
   chooses, and REQ-438's metering covers it.
2. **It is given the page's text.** Alongside the pictures, the critic gets each
   page's text in reading order (headings marked) from the same source
   `describe_page` uses. Then the messaging lens judges what is written, not what
   it was told.
3. **The messaging lens covers comprehension:**
   - every person and business named is introduced before it is relied on;
   - "I" / "we" are anchored to someone the visitor has met;
   - a second brand or history is explained where it first appears;
   - a quote, a testimonial and a call-out are visually distinct.
4. **No hand-off to absent checks.** The "checked elsewhere" line names only the
   faults REQ-429 actually checks. Everything else in style (spacing scale and
   consistency, section widths and edges, crops through faces or subjects, column
   heights) is the critic's to judge, until DOC-67's laws of craft measure it.
   After that, it judges against the site's own design system.
5. **Up to five findings**, most damaging first, so a page with many basic faults
   is not reviewed as three.

## Added 2026-10-24: the cold read is the reader for the whole page

Root cause, as understood with the operator: every role that checked Dina's page
was an insider. The consultant asked the questions, the builder kept the words
verbatim, and the critic was given the notes. Each checked **fidelity** (did we use
what the client gave us?), and none checked **comprehension** (does a stranger
follow it?). The layout failed the same way: each role did its part, and nobody
owned the page as a visitor sees it.

So the cold read is not only a messaging aid. It is **the stand-in for the
visitor across the whole page**: copy (REQ-446's rules), the order of sections,
and what the layout makes a visitor see first, miss or misread (crowding, a face
cropped away, a section that looks like a different site). Its findings are judged
against the client's goals in the informed stage.


## What landed (2026-10-24)

**A review is two turns of one critic session.**
1. **The cold read** (`criticFor`, `coldReadIntro`/`coldReadPage`): the site's
   shared header and footer words once, then for each reviewed page its words in
   reading order (`pageReadingText`, `segments.ts`, which walks the same L1 tree
   `describe_page` maps: headings marked `#`/`##`, a link with where it goes,
   `[a picture]`, `[the site's menu]`, a component's words where its slot is; no
   alt text or addresses), then its pictures at three widths. Nothing from the
   plan. The rubric asks the five cold-read questions (who runs it, who speaks,
   what can I do, what is unexplained, what the layout makes me see/miss/misread),
   with no report yet.
2. **The informed review**: `criticBrief`, headed `# The informed review`, in the
   same session, so the cold read's answers are in its history. The rubric says
   the cold read is the customer: a gap between what a stranger concluded and
   what the client asked for is a finding, and the cold read is never corrected
   from the brief. The pictures stay live (`image_turns: 2` on the critic role).

**Rubric** (`critic_priming`): the messaging lens adds the four comprehension
checks, and "a name is proof only once the visitor knows who it is". Purpose asks
whether the order of sections tells a stranger's story. Style owns the craft
nothing measures (spacing scale and consistency, shared section widths and edges,
crops that keep faces and subjects whole, matching column heights, a logo sized to
read). "Checked elsewhere" is `{checked_elsewhere}`, filled by `criticRole` from a
new `said` phrase on every `LAYOUT_CHECKS` entry ([[REQ-429]]). The `edges` phrase
says it only catches a section that departs from an edge most sections share, so
sections at five widths remain the critic's. The cap is `{max_findings}`, filled
from `MAX_FINDINGS`, which is now **5**; a sixth finding is dropped when the report
is read.

**Metering**: the critic's two requests share the review's minted turn; the second
turn's `request_spend` rows are numbered on from the first's (the key is
`(turn_id, request_index)`), so both are recorded.

## Design decisions

- Two turns of one session rather than two sessions: the pictures are sent once,
  the cold read reaches the informed stage as the conversation itself, and
  REQ-438's metering and attribution are unchanged.
- The page's words come from the stored L1 page (the `describe_page` source),
  not OCR of the pictures.
- Superseded REQ-427/BUG-258/REQ-438 pins: the critic is no longer "one turn,
  its first message only". Its request count per review is 2, and the brief is in
  the second request. Those UATs are updated to match. BUG-258 now also asserts
  that every picture is still an image in the second request.
