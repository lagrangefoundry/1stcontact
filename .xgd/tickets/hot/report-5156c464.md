## Notes for the Editor

**One cause behind findings 1–3.** When REQ-149 D7 removed the sha-addressed
draft channel, the clean-URL tests were collapsed by substituting the published
URL in place of the draft URL rather than by deleting the now-duplicate case.
The result is doubled requests and one assertion that survived as a tautology. A
single pass over `tests/reconciliation-clean-page-urls.test.ts` cleans all three
together. None of them weakens the evidence — they are dead weight, not false
positives — which is why they are warnings and not violations.

**Why every AC passed on substance.** The evidence in this capability is unusually
strong and worth not eroding:

- The two criteria that are claims about a *runtime* rather than a function
  (AC-1418 "no filesystem on the path", AC-1422 "the database refuses the second
  claim") are proved inside workerd against real D1 and real R2, where there is
  no filesystem to fall back on and the uniqueness is a real `PRIMARY KEY`. In
  Node both would have passed whether true or not.
- Fakes appear only at R2 and D1 — the one boundary this repo does not own — and
  the instrumented bucket is what turns "rejects before it reads" (AC-907),
  "a warm hit skips the store" (AC-911) and "no candidate was offered" (AC-923)
  into *observations* rather than claims.
- No test constructs a snapshot by hand: published bytes come from a real
  `cmdPublish` / `/api/publish`, so an assertion about what is served is an
  assertion about the product's own render.
- The two source-text assertions in AC-1418 (`builder.ts` has no `/api/publish`,
  the CLI has no `case 'deploy'`) are *not* the structural-test anti-pattern —
  they are exactly what that AC's own Verification prescribes ("confirm the
  builder's publish route has no handler of its own... and that no deploy command
  remains"), and they sit on top of a fully behavioural test that byte-compares
  the two front doors' resulting revision trees.

**Test execution in this session — a sandbox artifact, not evidence rot.** Running
the Node-side files here produced 8 failures, every one of them
`Error: listen EPERM: operation not permitted 0.0.0.0` thrown from
`tools/generate/src/cli/serve.ts:42` / `startBuilder`. The failing tests are
exactly the ones that must bind a real listening socket (AC-1420, AC-1421 via
`startBuilder`; AC-915, AC-917, AC-918, AC-919, AC-920, AC-922 via `startServe`).
No assertion failed. `reconciliation-published-site-serving.test.ts`, which drives
the Worker's `fetch` directly and binds nothing, passed in full. A future reader
should not mistake a red run in this sandbox for a coverage or correctness
problem — and equally should not treat this run as having *verified* those eight;
they are judged here on the substance of their assertions, which are sound.

**Carried-forward uncertainty, recorded not re-flagged.** STORY-95's Technical
Context already states that the end-to-end check against a live R2 bucket and the
apex custom-domain provisioning have never been run in session. That is a
deployment-wiring gap the story itself discloses, not a UAT coverage gap: the
serving rules are proven against real bindings inside workerd. No finding raised.
