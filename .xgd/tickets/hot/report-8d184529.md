survey detected the shared *vocabulary*, which is the intended design (one handle
form, one listing, no parallel picker), not duplicated ownership.

### Boundary check on the one near-duplicate pair

AC-1025 (STORY-100, "a region's current image is always among its options") and
the third bullet of AC-1028 (STORY-101, "the handle already in place is always
among the options") read alike. They are **distinct observations of the same
rule at different layers**, verified by different tests at different entry points:

- `test_UAT_AC1025_...` (tests/reconciliation-copy-edit-image-selection.test.ts:307)
  seeds a region whose handle names nothing in the store, calls `readFields`, and
  proves the alt-text-only save leaves `src` untouched — a server-side correctness
  property, provable with no browser.
- `test_UAT_AC1028_...` (tests/req118-image-selection.test.ts:177, :406) resolves a
  click on a rendered `[data-l1-segment="image"]` and proves the modal obtains its
  choices over the same copy transport a copy edit uses — a gesture property,
  unprovable from the CLI.

The enum assertions AC-1028 shares with AC-1024 are corroboration at the browser
layer, not a second owner of the narrowing rule. This is acceptable overlap in
assertions, not in ownership.
