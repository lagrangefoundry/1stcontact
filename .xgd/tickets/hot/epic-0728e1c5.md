## What the client asked for

> "I think we need a settings tab where this kind of stuff gets managed."
>
> "A settings tab (not on your list) including business/site/subdomain
> management (1stc.site) on setting page"

## The principle

**Everything the product knows a business by — its name, its site, and the
address that reaches it — is visible in one place, and changeable by the person
whose business it is.**

This epic owns the *surface* and the *record*. It does not own the work: the
assistant drives domain setup in conversation, and Settings is where the result
is visible and where you go back to check or change it. That split was agreed in
[[CHAT-48]] — "we need both" — and it matters, because most of what this tab
shows is configured exactly once and then read many times.

## What exists today, verified

- **There is no settings tab.** The strip is
  `TABS = [SITE_TAB, LIBRARY_TAB, PEOPLE_TAB]`
  (`apps/control-app/src/builder/config.js:90`).
- **The tab strip is uniformly business-scoped, deliberately.** The Account
  surface is *not* a tab — it sits in the header's trailing slot and opens as a
  modal (`openAccountSurface`, `apps/control-app/src/builder/business.js:253`).
  The reasoning is recorded at `apps/control-app/src/builder/app.js:194-209`
  ([[REQ-179]]): the account is the one surface that is not business-scoped, so
  a tab for it would be the single place where the shell's business switcher is
  present and silently does not apply, and *"a control that is present and
  ignored reads as a bug"*.
- **Business identity** lives in `tenants.name` ([[DOC-40]] §2), where it can
  change; the tenant id is opaque and permanent.
- **No label or subdomain concept exists.** [[DOC-45]] §7 specifies the shape
  (assigned at provision from the slugified business name, discriminator on
  collision, freely changeable); nothing implements it.

## Why Settings is legitimately a tab

Business name, sites, subdomain label and custom domain are all properties of a
**business**, and a business switcher applies to every one of them. So Settings
satisfies the rule the strip is built on, and needs no exception. This is worth
stating because the sibling billing epic does **not** satisfy it — see its
boundary note, and §"Boundaries" below.

## Scope

1. **The tab itself** — a fourth entry in `TABS`, business-scoped like the rest,
   blocked along with the others when a grant lapses.
2. **The business record** — name, and whatever else identifies the business to
   the product rather than to its customers.
3. **Sites** — the list, and per-site settings. [[DOC-40]] §2.3 commits to
   several sites per business, so this cannot be written as though there is one.
4. **The `1stc.site` label** — allocation at provision, display, and change.
   The reserved-label lists, uniqueness constraint and revocation path are
   specified in [[TODO-6]] §2–3 and implemented here.
5. **Custom domain status** — a read-only view onto state the DNS and registrar
   epics own. Settings shows *what the address is and whether it is working*; it
   does not perform the cutover.

## Decisions taken, and why

**Settings is a record-and-status surface, not a workshop.** [[DOC-46]]'s
position is that the client is not operating a tool — *"nothing for you to
design, either. The work is mine."* A furniture restorer who must find a Domains
section and choose between DNS flavours is having the Wix experience. So the
conversational path is primary and this tab is the durable record of what it
did.

**The label is assigned, never demanded up front** ([[DOC-45]] §7). The default
must exist and must visibly ask to be changed, rather than blocking a customer
on a choice they are not ready to make. It is also what makes [[DOC-45]] §4 safe
— a site with no address would be unreachable once the path grammar is deleted.

**Old labels are retained and never re-issued.** A customer who put
`alice.1stc.site` on a van and then renames needs the old one to keep working,
and re-issuing it to someone else would hand over whatever inbound links and
reputation the previous holder built. See [[TODO-6]] §3.

## Boundaries

- **DNS records and nameservers** → the DNS management epic. Settings links to
  it; it does not embed a record editor.
- **Buying, transferring and renewing a domain** → the registrar epic.
- **Whether the address actually resolves** → the DNS checks epic. Settings
  renders that verdict, it does not compute it.
- **Billing** → the billing epic, which must **not** become a second tab. The
  full argument is in that ticket; the short form is that
  `apps/control-app/src/builder/app.js:270` makes the tab strip the *entitled*
  product, so tabs are blocked when a grant lapses — which is precisely when a
  billing surface has to stay reachable.

## Open questions

1. **[[DOC-45]] §11 item 4 — is the label per site or per business?** §3 of that
   document says a host names a site, which implies per site, with a business
   holding several labels. This epic needs the answer before allocation is built.
2. **One settings page with a site selector, or a page per site?** Falls out of
   (1).
3. **Does `tenants.name` carry any uniqueness?** [[DOC-45]] §11 item 1
   recommends none — *"a list the operator reads is not a namespace"* — but the
   label derived from it does, which is the only place the two meet.

## Correction, 2026-09-12 — the billing boundary note was wrong

This ticket's Boundaries section said billing "must not become a second tab",
citing `apps/control-app/src/builder/app.js:270`. **Withdrawn.**

The argument mistook which billing surface was in question. [[DOC-42]] §1 says
the surface where someone *pays* is the User Portal, and [[DOC-42]] §5 makes
Portal access membership rather than a grant, so it cannot be blocked. The
billing **tab** is where a business owner *configures* what they charge their own
customers — which is business-scoped, satisfies the [[REQ-179]] rule this tab
strip is built on, and is a legitimate fourth-and-fifth tab alongside this one.

So `blockTabs` blocking the billing tab on a lapsed grant is correct behaviour,
not a deadlock. Nothing in this epic changes; the cross-reference does.

See [[EPIC-9]].


## Correction, 2026-09-13 — the label is chosen and final, and the open questions are settled

Scoping this epic's children ([[CHAT-48]] follow-on) reversed two decisions above
and answered all three open questions. The text above is left as written; this
section is what supersedes it.

**"The label is assigned, never demanded up front" — withdrawn.** [[DOC-45]] §7
has been rewritten and now says the opposite: the customer **chooses** the
hostname, may take as long as they like over it, and **publication** is what
requires one. The old rule rested on [[REQ-190]]'s `unnamed` reasoning, which
fails here because the name it would slugify is `Unnamed business` ([[BUG-90]]) —
so the assigned default is `unnamedbusiness`, not a name that asks to be changed
but a name that says nothing, in the one namespace on this product that is
global, public and first-come. The `unnamed` argument is still right about the
*business* name, which is internal and free to change; it is wrong about a public
address.

**"Old labels are retained and never re-issued" — withdrawn, and the paragraph
no longer describes anything.** A hostname is **final**: chosen once, never
changed. Free reassignment plus recycling would let one business walk through
dozens of good names in an afternoon. Because nothing is ever relinquished,
nothing is ever re-issued, and the van keeps working because the label never
moves. A change path is expected eventually, gated by a small one-off fee priced
for friction — recorded in [[DOC-45]] §7 as future intent, explicitly not scope.

**Scope item 3, "Sites — the list, and per-site settings", is withdrawn.** It
said [[DOC-40]] §2.3 *"commits to several sites per business, so this cannot be
written as though there is one."* [[BUG-90]] has since made it exactly one, and
that ticket names this work as where the consequent question gets asked. There is
no list, no site selector, and no site name distinct from the business — so after
[[REQ-236]] there is nothing left on this tab called a "site name". Multi-site
generalises in a few years if there is demand, and not before.

**Open question 1 — is the label per site or per business?** Per business, one at
a time. With one site per business the distinction is not observable;
[[DOC-45]] §11 item 4 is updated to say so, and to say that it reopens the day
the site selector lands.

**Open question 2 — one settings page with a site selector, or a page per site?**
One page, no selector. It falls out of (1) as predicted.

**Open question 3 — does `tenants.name` carry any uniqueness?** Yes: unique
within its owning account, compared case-folded and whitespace-collapsed.
[[DOC-45]] §11 item 1 recommended none, and the reason it gave — *"a list the
operator reads is not a namespace"* — is about `sites.name`, not this. The
constraint is data integrity and not a UI guarantee: grants span accounts, so two
identically-named businesses owned by different accounts can still appear side by
side in one person's switcher. See [[REQ-237]].

**A correction to the record.** "What exists today, verified" says the business
name lives in `tenants.name`. There is a second string —
`site.config.businessName`, written into the site definition at provision and
never synchronised — and it is **not** a duplicate to be collapsed. It is what
the *site says*, sitting beside `tagline` and `contact.…` in the same schema;
`tenants.name` is what the business *is called*. They are allowed to differ, and
[[REQ-237]] leaves the site's copy alone and reports the divergence instead.

**"A record-and-status surface, not a workshop" — corrected.** This epic argues
from [[DOC-46]] that the conversational path is primary and the tab is the
durable record of what it did. That is right for the hostname, which is public,
final and deserves a conversation. It is taken too far as a general rule: **some
things are simply easier done directly than through discursive communication.**
Correcting a typo in your own business name is a text field, not a dialogue, and
a product that insists on the dialogue has added the friction it exists to
remove.

So the settings pane holds **editable fields** — click one, change it — and the
assistant holds an API to the same operations, for the customer who would rather
ask. Neither is a fallback for the other, and neither wraps the other: both are
ordinary callers of one API. The hostname field behaves as a registrar's does —
type a name, press return, told taken or available — and the assistant reaches
the same availability check, which is what lets it propose names that are
actually free rather than guess. See [[REQ-238]] and [[REQ-239]].
