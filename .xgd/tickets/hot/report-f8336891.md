      inlines the new titles into the caretaker reminder, and advances the cursor.
      A map is a description, not a notification, so the delta travels separately
      from the landscape. The rules that make it usable are requirements rather than
      polish: an empty delta emits nothing at all rather than ''nothing new''; the
      delta is capped at roughly 400 characters with an exact count that is never
      truncated (''41 documents added, including …''), and a single oversized title

      over-wide sweep and never a turn; chat tickets are excluded from the delta alone

      is never announced to itself; and the delta is placed last in the reminder because
      stable material must precede volatile material for the seeded prefix to stay
      prompt-cached. The cursor starts where the landscape''s coverage ends — the
      awareness map''s build timestamp — so the map and the cursor are exactly complementary,
      which is also what makes a resumed session''s first turn report what arrived
      while the client was away with no separate resumption path. The behavioural
      claim is the one that matters: upload a document mid-session and on the next
      turn the assistant knows it exists and can answer from it, without waiting for
      a map rebuild.'

    component: 'Assistant pane: markdown engines settle before the transcript paints'
    item_type: upgrade
    story_points: 1
    dependencies: []
    description: Extends STORY-104's replay with the ordering rule it was missing.
      The markdown engines are lazily imported from a CDN behind the components' seams,
      and until one is present renderSafe deliberately degrades to escaped source
      — correct when an engine is absent, wrong when it is merely late. chat.js used
      to fire the loaders and not wait for them while showSite replayed the transcript
      as soon as the session fetch answered, so a cold load lost the race and painted
      every turn as escaped source permanently, because each message is rendered once
      and there is no way to re-render it; a warm load was served from cache, won,
      and looked correct. A new builder/markdown.js owns both engines for the whole

**A judgment call on BUG-40, stated because it drives the item count.** BUG-40 is
5 points and touches eleven criteria, which reads like several items. It is one,
because reading those criteria shows their text already states the shipped rule:
the earlier reconciliations of BUG-38, REQ-145, REQ-147, REQ-149 and the palette
work already moved the *criteria*, and BUG-40 was moving the *tests* to catch up.
The exception is AC-1331, whose `--skip-preflight` clause the new assets stage
made half-true, and which item 4 modifies. Had I taken the commit at face value
and made an item per re-pinned AC, seven of the eight would have been no-ops.

**Why REQ-155 and REQ-156 are features and not upgrades.** Both look like
refactors from the diff — a port and a codec swap, with the artifacts and the
verdicts explicitly required not to move. But reconciliation asks what the matrix
fails to say, and it says nothing at all about where a capture bundle lives or
how bytes become pixels. Those are capability buckets that never existed, not
changed behaviour in ones that did. The reuse-first bias was applied where it
actually bites: item 3 pulls REQ-156's preflight consequence out into an upgrade
of STORY-79 rather than letting the codec story restate install gating.

**BUG-42 splits across two items, deliberately.** Its own framing is "one rule,
applied at both surfaces" — but the two surfaces belong to two capabilities
(capability-e9324eb7's Library and capability-44a04848's assistant pane) and the
two failures are different: the Library never rendered markdown at all, while the
transcript rendered it correctly on a warm load and lost a race on a cold one.
The Library half folds into item 8 because REQ-172 rebuilds that same pane in the
same commit range and the two would otherwise collide on STORY-144; the
transcript half is item 9 on its own. `markdown.js` is named in both, once as the
shared seam it introduces and once as the module the reader re-exports from.

**Item 8 is the largest upgrade and was nearly two.** REQ-172 and BUG-42's Library
half both land on STORY-144 within days, and the second is a technical
precondition of the first (the reader and the description cell paint through the
same `renderSafe` seam and wait on the same `markdownReady`). Splitting them
would produce two items modifying one story with an ordering dependency between
them, which is worse than one 3-point item.

**Uncertainty.** Item 10 targets two stories because the signal is produced on

story cycle finds the reload needs a criterion of its own on STORY-99, that is a
