# Review the session prompts and turn reminders — and make the chat summary one of them

## The role is misnamed, and the name is the bug

[[DOC-4]] asks for a platform for "creating **and** operating" small-business web
presences. The code declares one role, `CARETAKER_ROLE`, named after the second
half — and uses it for the first. `CARETAKER_SYSTEM` opens *"You are the
caretaker of a website your user owns"*, and in most sessions today there is no
site yet and nothing to caretake.

The document set never had one role. [[DOC-33]] stage 10 hands off *"a live site,
the ledger rendered as a plain-English design record, and an introduction to the
caretaker"*, who **inherits** the ledger — so the caretaker is the ongoing tier's
role, distinct and later. What runs today is the consultation ([[DOC-33]]), under
the personas, modes and registers of [[DOC-35]].

This is what turns the ticket from a prompt audit into a derivation. The three
texts below do not need reviewing against a system that moved underneath them;
they need **deriving from a role that has not been declared yet**.

## The three texts

- `CARETAKER_SYSTEM` (`tools/generate/src/cli/ai/roles.ts`) — the preamble: who
  the assistant is, how it changes the site, how it speaks to the client.
- `CARETAKER_PURPOSE` (`tools/generate/src/cli/ai/host-core.ts`) — the role
  purpose KM's priming carries as its middle section, answering *"what should I
  go looking for in this corpus"*.
- `caretakerReminder` (`roles.ts`) — re-applied through the system channel on
  every turn, never written to the transcript.

Each was written for the state of the system at the time. [[REQ-158]] gave the
session a system knowledge base, [[REQ-159]] gave it the client's own corpus, and
[[REQ-160]] puts two landscapes and a per-turn change delta in front of it.

## 1. The role: `consultant`

**Not `concierge`.** A concierge accommodates — it fetches what was asked for.
[[DOC-33]] §7.4 and [[DOC-35]] §8.5 make strategic pushback non-negotiable and
explicitly **non-modal**, and the defining act of this role is telling a client
their stated problem is not their actual problem ([[DOC-35]] §4.3 — the client
who says *"I want a better website"* and means *"I want more customers"*). A
concierge that pushes back is a bad concierge. `consultant` also matches the
document set's own vocabulary: [[DOC-33]] is the *Consultation* Playbook and
sells "the consultation", free and paid.

**One role now.** `caretaker` is retired from this slot and **reserved**, not
deleted — it is the ongoing tier's role in [[DOC-33]] §10/§11 and its grant will
be narrower when there is an ongoing tier to serve. Declaring it today is exactly
the speculative structure [[DOC-33]] stage 1 forbids.

What the rename touches: `CARETAKER_ROLE`, the `caretaker` key in
`instances.json` (the capability grant — unchanged in content), the role name
`host-core.ts` registers sessions under, and the test helpers that name it. The
grant itself does not change. **Open:** whether existing sessions keyed to the
old role name need a migration or can be abandoned at alpha volumes.

## 2. The preamble, derived from the role

Two lines are actively wrong for the consultant; most of the rest survives.

- *"They are not technical."* Replace with [[DOC-35]] §9.4's asymmetry rule:
  fluency is loud, confusion is silent, so default to the careful register and let
  demonstrated fluency escalate it — never de-escalate on silence. A flat
  assumption is wrong for a substantial part of the known population
  ([[DOC-35]] §5.3) and patronising in a way fluent people do not forgive
  ([[DOC-35]] §9.3).
- *"Make the smallest change that answers the request."* This is the caretaker's
  instruction and it contradicts [[DOC-33]] §7.1 head-on: refining a single
  proposal drifts toward the model's default, which is the templatey pull the
  product exists to escape. The rule is two or three **structurally distinct**
  options at every significant decision. Minimal-change survives for
  adjustment-level work only.

Unchanged and still correct: tools-only with no other path, a malformed change
refused whole, the typed element tree plus vetted components, never naming
framework concepts to the client, read before you write, changes private until
published.

**The playbook does not go in the preamble.** [[DOC-33]] §12 says so itself, and
it is the same layering [[REQ-126]] set out: anything that changes when the
method changes belongs in the corpus, not in hand-written priming. The preamble
states that the role has a method and is expected to consult it.

What may need to move *out*, re-running [[REQ-126]]'s pass: anything the
projected manual or the declaration's `overview` now states.

## 3. The purpose, naming both landscapes

`CARETAKER_PURPOSE` currently frames the assistant purely against the framework
documents — *"you will need to know how this system builds and describes sites"*
— which was the whole truth when there was one KB. The consultant also goes
looking for the client's own material and prior decisions ([[REQ-159]]), and for
consultation knowledge: the playbook, the personas, the design rubric. It should
name both landscapes and say what each is **for**, because priming step 2 exists
to give the agent a basis for choosing what to search.

## 4. The reminder

The constraint stands: it rides every request, and a reminder that restates the
preamble is a second copy of the preamble. The habit that genuinely decays by
turn thirty for *this* role is options-not-iterations — a model slides into
refine-the-one-proposal without noticing. That is the candidate to add, alongside
what [[REQ-131]] and [[REQ-160]] already push (the draft change signal and the
corpus delta).

## 5. The summary gap is the ledger gap

[[DOC-10]] §8 reserved the `chat` ticket body for an AI-maintained summary.
**Nothing writes it.** [[DOC-33]] §3.1 supersedes that framing: the body is the
**ledger** — what was decided, **why**, what was rejected, and whether it is open
or locked.

That is not cosmetic, because the body is what makes a conversation *knowledge*:

- The knowledge component indexes `title` and `body` (`ticketText`). The
  transcript is a comment and is not indexed. So a chat ticket with an empty body
  contributes an essentially content-free vector to the project KB.
- Its title is whatever the archive minted — the session id. Below the enumerate
  floor, which is every new client, the awareness map lists that verbatim.
- [[DOC-38]] §6 and [[DOC-39]] §7 both rest on every project-KB entry having an
  AI-written title over an AI-written body.

Two things fall out of choosing the ledger over a summary:

1. **It fixes retrieval structurally rather than by instruction.** A summary has
   to be *told* to lead with the words someone would search by ([[DOC-38]] §6); a
   ledger names the client's business, positioning and palette because that is
   what it records.
2. **It answers the trigger question differently: at decision points, not on a
   clock.** A grown-by-N-characters threshold is right for indexing a transcript
   ([[DOC-39]] §4.2) and wrong for a ledger — an entry is written when a decision
   locks, which is an event the role already knows about. `append_body` has
   compare-and-set and retry ([[DOC-33]] §3.3), so a gate commit writes the delta,
   not the document.

The title problem follows: the consultant renames the chat ticket once it knows
the business, rather than the archive guessing at creation time.

## Blocking dependency: the system corpus is empty

**Zero `doc` tickets carry `doc_kind: system_kb`.** The export gate landed
([[REQ-164]]) but nothing is tagged, so the shipped corpus is projections only.
Pointing the consultant's purpose at the playbook does nothing until [[DOC-33]]
and [[DOC-35]] are in it — and they cannot simply be tagged: [[DOC-39]] §3.1/§3.5
require a system-KB document to be addressed to the AI as its reader, actionable
in a conversation, and free of rationale for our engineering choices. Both
documents currently carry open questions about themselves. That is an authoring
pass, not a field flip.

## Out of scope

- The archive itself and the chat ticket's creation — [[REQ-160]].
- The awareness map and the enumerate floor — [[REQ-159]], [[DOC-39]] §7.
- Declaring a separate `caretaker` role for the ongoing tier — reserved, parked.

## Acceptance

- A role named for the job it does is declared, its grant unchanged, and
  `caretaker` no longer names the build session anywhere in the source.
- The three texts are derived from that role, and each change is justified against
  the layering [[REQ-126]] set out — preamble for what is stable, projection for
  what tracks the surface, reminder for what decays — rather than by taste.
- The preamble carries no restatement of the playbook, and the purpose names both
  landscapes.
- A chat ticket carries a ledger in its body and a title that is not its session
  id, both written for retrieval.
- The ledger is maintained on a stated trigger, and the cost of that trigger is
  stated with it.

## Open

- **Scope split.** The rename and the three texts are inseparable. The ledger is a
  write path, a trigger and a title rename; the system-KB authoring pass is its
  own piece of work. Whether those two leave this ticket is undecided.
- **Session migration** under the role rename (above).
- **Where the mechanics live.** Options-not-iterations, show-don't-ask,
  ask-facts-not-diagnoses, the one-push rule, never-defer, and the two channels
  ([[DOC-33]] §7) are the role's method. Preamble (stable, cached) or corpus
  (changes with the playbook)? The [[REQ-126]] test says corpus for anything that
  moves with the method — but a habit the model must not drift from is a preamble
  concern. This is the sharpest remaining layering question.
