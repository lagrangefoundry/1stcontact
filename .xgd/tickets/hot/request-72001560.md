# Review the session prompts and turn reminders — and make the chat summary one of them

## The role is misnamed, and the name is the bug

[[DOC-4]] asks for a platform for "creating **and** operating" small-business web
presences. The code declares one role, `CARETAKER_ROLE`, named after the second
half — and uses it for the first. Its system prompt opens *"You are the caretaker
of a website your user owns"*, and in most sessions today there is no site yet
and nothing to caretake.

The document set never had one role. [[DOC-33]] stage 10 hands off *"a live site,
the ledger rendered as a plain-English design record, and an introduction to the
caretaker"*, who **inherits** the ledger — so the caretaker is the ongoing tier's
role, distinct and later. What runs today is the consultation ([[DOC-33]]), under
the personas, modes and registers of [[DOC-35]].

That turns this ticket from a prompt audit into a derivation. The texts below are
not reviewed against a system that moved underneath them; they are **derived from
a role that has not been declared yet**.

## Retire, do not rename

`caretaker` is **retired**, and `consultant` is a **new role** — not the same
role under a different name. The distinction is the whole point: a rename carries
the existing prose forward and the existing prose is what is wrong. A new role
gets texts written for it, and the caretaker's are deleted rather than edited.

`caretaker` is not reused for anything else. It is the ongoing tier's role in
[[DOC-33]] §10/§11 and its grant will be narrower when there is an ongoing tier
to serve; declaring it today is the speculative structure [[DOC-33]] stage 1
forbids.

**Open:** whether the retired role's definition stays in the source as a dormant
entry or is removed, and whether sessions keyed to it need a migration or can be
abandoned at alpha volumes.

## The anatomy: what a session actually receives

Four texts, not three. Named here as they will be referred to throughout.

**The system prompt** — a hand-written string on the `Role`, ~40 lines, currently
`CARETAKER_SYSTEM` in `tools/generate/src/cli/ai/roles.ts`. It is **not** part of
the priming document. It is the largest hand-written text in the session and the
least reviewed.

**The priming** — one document assembled by KM (`assemblePriming`, in
`@lagrangefoundry/knowledge`), three sections in a fixed, load-bearing order:

1. `# What exists` — the landscape maps, one per KB, plus honest gaps.
2. `# Your purpose` — the role purpose verbatim, currently `CARETAKER_PURPOSE`.
3. `# How to search` — the mechanism, which this host supplies as the projected
   tool manual (`box.manual()`), the list of searchable KBs, then a closing
   trigger: *"Prime yourself now: pick the territories above that bear on your
   purpose, search them, and read what you find before starting work."*

**The reminder** — `caretakerReminder()`, re-applied through the system channel
every turn, never written to the transcript.

## The governing constraint

**Nothing duplicative or irrelevant reaches the context.** Every section below is
justified against that and not against completeness. Concretely: the priming's
purpose section is short and points at documents; the reminder is a handful of
lines; anything that is a method rather than a habit lives in the corpus and is
*referenced*, not restated.

## 1. `# Your purpose` — short, with role-specific references

Currently four lines of prose that name no documents, and framed purely against
the framework corpus — *"you will need to know how this system builds and
describes sites"* — which was the whole truth when there was one KB. [[REQ-158]]
gave the session a system KB, [[REQ-159]] gave it the client's own corpus.

It becomes a short statement of what the consultant is here to do, plus
**role-specific references**: the documents this role in particular must read.
That is the shape the section was built for — the trigger immediately after it
already says *"pick the territories above that bear on your purpose"*, and a
purpose that names no territory gives that instruction nothing to bite on.

## 2. `# How to search` — a summary, not the full manual

The mechanism is currently the **whole projected manual**, and it is large. The
declaration carries 27 operations across 9 groups; the renderer emits, for every
granted operation, its summary, its long description (20 of 27 have one), every
parameter with type and necessity and description, the return shape's fields, and
every error code with its message — plus a 2,107-character overview.

That is a reference work, and a reference work does not belong in priming. What
priming needs is:

- **what tools are available**,
- **what they are intended for**,
- **a summary guide**,
- **how to get more info**.

The declaration already carries the material for exactly that level: groups have
client-facing titles ("Looking at the site", "Changing what is on a page",
"Changing the site's colors") and descriptions, and every operation has a
one-line summary. A summary manual is group title + group description + one line
per operation, dropping parameters, return shapes and error codes. The full entry
for a named operation is then fetched on demand.

**This is an upstream change.** The renderer lives in `@lagrangefoundry/ai`
(`toolbox/manual.js`), outside this repo, and it holds two properties that a new
render level must not break: the manual is a **pure projection** — *"a usable
manual generates with no prose written in any configuration"*, and *"there is no
merged host-authored preamble, because that is prose creeping back toward
configuration"* — and every string is **byte-for-byte the Python peer's**, so a
summary level lands in both languages or in neither.

The in-repo shortcut — passing a hand-written `mechanism` summary instead of the
projection — is rejected. That is the drift [[REQ-126]] exists to prevent: a
hand-written inventory is precisely the text still describing last month's
surface six weeks later, and it is worse than no inventory because the model
believes it.

**Gaps found:** 2 of the 9 groups carry no description, so a summary-only render
would show them as a bare title. Those descriptions need writing.

## 3. The system prompt

**Open — the largest undecided question.** See "Open questions" below.

Whatever survives, two of its current lines are wrong for the consultant:

- *"They are not technical."* [[DOC-35]] §9.4's asymmetry rule replaces it:
  fluency is loud, confusion is silent, so default to the careful register and let
  demonstrated fluency escalate it — never de-escalate on silence. A flat
  assumption is wrong for a substantial part of the known population
  ([[DOC-35]] §5.3) and patronising in a way fluent people do not forgive
  ([[DOC-35]] §9.3).
- *"Make the smallest change that answers the request."* The caretaker's
  instruction, and it contradicts [[DOC-33]] §7.1 head-on: refining a single
  proposal drifts toward the model's default, which is the templatey pull the
  product exists to escape. The rule is two or three **structurally distinct**
  options at every significant decision. Minimal-change survives for
  adjustment-level work only.

Still correct wherever it ends up: tools-only with no other path, a malformed
change refused whole, the typed element tree plus vetted components, never naming
framework concepts to the client, read before you write, changes private until
published.

## 4. The reminder — a handful of lines, pointers not text

It rides every request, so a reminder that restates anything is a second copy of
that thing. [[DOC-33]] §7's rules of interaction — options rather than iterations,
show rather than ask, ask for facts not diagnoses, push back once then execute,
never defer — are **too big for the reminder**. They go in the corpus, and the
reminder carries a **pointer**: *"don't forget the rules of interaction in
DOC-33."*

Alongside what [[REQ-131]] and [[REQ-160]] already push: the draft change signal
and the corpus delta.

**The pointer is blocked** on the dependency below: a reminder naming [[DOC-33]]
does nothing while [[DOC-33]] is not in a corpus the consultant can search.

## 5. The summary gap is the ledger gap

[[DOC-10]] §8 reserved the `chat` ticket body for an AI-maintained summary.
**Nothing writes it.** [[DOC-33]] §3.1 supersedes that framing: the body is the
**ledger** — what was decided, **why**, what was rejected, and whether it is open
or locked.

That is not cosmetic, because the body is what makes a conversation *knowledge*:

- The knowledge component indexes `title` and `body` (`ticketText`). The
  transcript is a comment and is not indexed. So a chat ticket with an empty body
  contributes an essentially content-free vector to the project KB.
- Its title is whatever the archive minted — the session id. Below the enumerate
  floor, which is every new client, the awareness map lists that verbatim.
- [[DOC-38]] §6 and [[DOC-39]] §7 both rest on every project-KB entry having an
  AI-written title over an AI-written body.

Two things follow from choosing the ledger over a summary:

1. **It fixes retrieval structurally rather than by instruction.** A summary has
   to be *told* to lead with the words someone would search by ([[DOC-38]] §6); a
   ledger names the client's business, positioning and palette because that is
   what it records.
2. **It answers the trigger question differently: at decision points, not on a
   clock.** A grown-by-N-characters threshold is right for indexing a transcript
   ([[DOC-39]] §4.2) and wrong for a ledger — an entry is written when a decision
   locks, which is an event the role already knows about. `append_body` has
   compare-and-set and retry ([[DOC-33]] §3.3), so a gate commit writes the delta,
   not the document.

The title follows: the consultant renames the chat ticket once it knows the
business, rather than the archive guessing at creation time.

## Blocking dependency: the system corpus is empty

**Zero `doc` tickets carry `doc_kind: system_kb`.** The export gate landed
([[REQ-164]]) but nothing is tagged, so the shipped corpus is projections only.
Every "reference it rather than restate it" decision above — the purpose's
reading list, the reminder's pointer to the rules of interaction — is inert until
[[DOC-33]] and [[DOC-35]] are in the corpus.

They cannot simply be tagged. [[DOC-39]] §3.1/§3.5 require a system-KB document
to be addressed to the AI as its reader, actionable in a conversation, and free
of rationale for our engineering choices. Both documents currently carry open
questions about themselves. That is an authoring pass, not a field flip.

## Out of scope

- The archive itself and the chat ticket's creation — [[REQ-160]].
- The awareness map and the enumerate floor — [[REQ-159]], [[DOC-39]] §7.
- Declaring a `caretaker` role for the ongoing tier — reserved, parked.

## Acceptance

- A `consultant` role is declared with texts written for it; `caretaker` is
  retired and no longer names the build session anywhere in the source.
- The priming's purpose section is short and names the documents this role must
  read.
- The priming's mechanism section carries a summary guide — what tools exist, what
  they are for, how to get more detail — not the full reference, and it is
  **projected**, not hand-written.
- The reminder is a handful of lines and references the rules of interaction
  rather than restating them.
- Nothing appears in two places: each rule is in exactly one of corpus, priming,
  system prompt or reminder, and the choice is justified.
- A chat ticket carries a ledger in its body and a title that is not its session
  id, both written for retrieval.
- The ledger is maintained on a stated trigger, and the cost of that trigger is
  stated with it.

## Open questions

- **Does the consultant have a hand-written system prompt at all?** The
  alternative is that it dissolves: what the role *is* goes in the priming's
  purpose section, what must not decay goes in the reminder, the method goes in
  the corpus, and the surface stays projected — one hand-written text per part and
  nothing restating anything. Against: the system prompt is the text with the most
  weight in the model's attention, and habits placed only in a corpus fire only
  when the model thinks to search.
- **Scope split.** The retirement and the four texts are one unit. The ledger is a
  write path, a trigger and a title rename; the system-KB authoring pass and the
  upstream summary-manual projection are each their own work. Whether they leave
  this ticket is undecided.
- **Retired-role disposition and session migration** (above).
