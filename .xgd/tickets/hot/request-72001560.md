# Review the session prompts and turn reminders — and make the chat summary one of them

## Why

Three pieces of text decide what the builder's assistant does, and they have
grown by accretion rather than by review:

- `CARETAKER_SYSTEM` (`tools/generate/src/cli/ai/roles.ts`) — the preamble: who
  the assistant is, how it changes the site, how it speaks to a non-technical
  owner.
- `CARETAKER_PURPOSE` (`tools/generate/src/cli/ai/host-core.ts`) — the role
  purpose KM's priming carries as its middle section, answering *"what should I
  go looking for in this corpus"*.
- `caretakerReminder` (`roles.ts`) — re-applied through the system channel on
  every turn, never written to the transcript.

Each was written for the state of the system at the time, and the system has
moved underneath all three. [[REQ-158]] gave the session a system knowledge base,
[[REQ-159]] gave it the client's own corpus, and [[REQ-160]] puts two landscapes
and a per-turn change delta in front of it. `CARETAKER_PURPOSE` in particular
still frames the assistant purely against the design documents — *"you will need
to know how this system builds and describes sites"* — which was the whole truth
when there was one KB and is now half of it.

## The summary is the concrete gap

[[DOC-10]] §8 homes a session in a `chat` ticket and reserves that ticket's
**body for an AI-maintained summary**, deliberately keeping it apart from the
transcript, which lives in a `chat_transcript` comment. [[REQ-160]] wires the
archive that creates the ticket. **Nothing writes the summary.**

That is not cosmetic, because the summary is what makes a conversation
*knowledge*:

- The knowledge component indexes `title` and `body` (`ticketText`). The
  transcript is a comment and is not indexed. So a chat ticket with no summary
  contributes an essentially content-free vector to the project KB.
- Its title is whatever the archive minted — the session id. Below the enumerate
  floor, which is every new client, the awareness map lists that verbatim.
- [[DOC-38]] §6 and [[DOC-39]] §7 both rest on every project-KB entry having an
  AI-written title over an AI-written body, and name *"the maintained summary"*
  as the chat entry's. After [[REQ-160]] that is still not true.

**This is a prompting job before it is a plumbing one**, which is why it belongs
with the review above rather than in [[REQ-160]]. The summary has to be written
by the assistant, in the same turn loop, under an instruction that has to sit in
one of the three texts. Deciding *which* — and what it says, and how often it
fires — is the work.

## What to look at

- **Whether the summary is maintained per turn, on a threshold, or at session
  end.** Per turn is a model call and a ticket write on every exchange; session
  end never fires, because a builder conversation is resumed rather than closed.
  A grown-by-N-characters threshold is what [[REQ-159]] already does for
  transcript indexing, and the two clocks are the same clock.
- **Whether the summary is written for a reader or for retrieval.** [[DOC-38]] §6
  is emphatic that a description exists so the material can be found and should
  lead with the words someone would search by. A summary that reads beautifully
  and never names the client's business has failed the only job it has. The chat
  ticket's *title* is the same problem in miniature.
- **What the reminder should carry now.** [[REQ-131]] added the draft change
  signal and [[REQ-160]] adds the corpus delta; both ride every turn. The
  reminder's own rule is that it stays short, because a reminder that restates
  the preamble is a second copy of the preamble. Whether it can also carry a
  summary instruction without breaking that is exactly the question.
- **Whether `CARETAKER_PURPOSE` should name both knowledge bases.** It is the
  priming's step 2 and it frames what the agent goes looking for. It currently
  frames only the system corpus.
- **What the preamble no longer needs to say.** [[REQ-126]] moved two rules out
  of it on the principle that anything which changes when the surface changes
  belongs in the projected manual. Worth re-running that pass.

## Out of scope

- The archive itself and the chat ticket's creation — [[REQ-160]].
- The awareness map and the enumerate floor — [[REQ-159]], [[DOC-39]] §7.

## Acceptance

- The three texts are reviewed against the system as it now is, and each change
  is justified against the layering [[REQ-126]] set out rather than by taste.
- A chat ticket carries a summary in its body and a title that is not its session
  id, both written for retrieval.
- The summary is maintained on a stated trigger, and the cost of that trigger is
  stated with it.
- The project KB's enumerated landscape reads usefully for a client whose corpus
  is mostly conversations.
