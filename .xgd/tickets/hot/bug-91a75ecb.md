## Symptom

Reported by the operator, 2026-09-18, second incident in a row.

A long prompt was typed into the builder's assistant pane and sent. The reply
never appeared and the pane offered the `↺` resend control on the prompt bubble.
The first time this happened the operator pressed `↺` and got a duplicated turn
(that is `lagrange-framework` BUG-58). **This time they reloaded the page
instead — and the prompt was gone.** Not the reply: the text they had written.
There was nothing to recover it from.

The operator was choosing between two recoveries, and both destroy something.

## Why the text is unrecoverable

**The composer deletes its persisted draft at submit, before anything durable
has the text.** `webui-chat`'s `submitWith` (`input.js`) calls `clear()` *before*
running the handler, and `clear()` → `setMarkdown('')` → `sync()` →
`store.removeItem(draftKey)`. The stated reason is sound on its own terms —
*"the text has been accepted by the session the moment it is submitted… leaving
it in the box would invite sending it twice."* But "accepted by the session" is
doing unearned work: at that instant the text has been accepted by *a fetch that
has not yet resolved*.

From submit onward the browser holds exactly one copy of the prompt: the DOM
bubble in the panel. A reload discards it.

**And the origin's copy is not durable while the turn is open.** `ai.ts` already
states this exactly, as the residual exposure left over from BUG-46:

> AN EVICTION MID-TURN LOSES THE WHOLE TURN, not a partial one. […] between
> `turn_start` and `turn_end` the durable copy has that turn missing ENTIRELY.
> […] the junction is RAM scoped to one isolate, so an isolate evicted mid-turn
> still loses that turn.

The junction is `lib.memoryJunctions()`. `turn_start` carries the user's content
and is appended the moment the turn opens (`manager.js` `_recordContext` →
`log.append(TURN_START, …)`), so the prompt *is* recorded — into RAM. The archive
cuts at `closedPrefix`, the last boundary where no turn is open (framework
BUG-19), so an open turn contributes nothing durable at all.

Put together: **a prompt whose turn never reaches a drained `turn_end` exists
only in one isolate's RAM and one browser's DOM, and a reload destroys the
second.**

## Why this is not BUG-46, and not BUG-58

- **BUG-46** made *a reload mid-turn* non-destructive — the panel repaints from
  the junction and reattaches. That holds only while the isolate lives.
- **`ai.ts`'s documented residual** is that an eviction loses the *turn*. That
  was written as a statement about the assistant's work.
- **What this ticket adds** is that the same loss takes the *operator's own
  writing* with it, and that is a different class of harm. A lost reply can be
  regenerated by asking again. A lost prompt cannot be regenerated by anyone —
  it was composed by a person, once, and this one was long.
- **`lagrange-framework` BUG-58** is the upstream cause of the operator being in
  this position at all: a dropped stream reads as a dead turn, so they are
  offered a resend that duplicates. Reload is the other option, and it is this
  one. Fixing BUG-58 makes this rarer; it does not make submitted text safe.

## What good looks like

The operator's text must survive both recoveries. Sketches, not a decision:

- **Keep the submitted draft until the turn is durably recorded**, rather than
  until it is handed to a fetch — a "sent, not yet acknowledged" copy the
  composer can restore on mount when the replayed transcript does not contain
  it. This is the smallest change that closes the hole and needs no origin work.
  Note it is `webui-chat`'s composer, so it is a framework change or a host-side
  wrapper; decide which.
- **Make the junction durable** — a Durable Object per session, which DOC-21
  §15.4 already names as the route back and which `ai.ts` says fits the
  `Junctions` port with no library change. Closes the loss at its source, and is
  much the larger piece of work.
- **Stop offering a reload as the recovery** by fixing BUG-58, so the situation
  arises far less often.

The first and third are independently worth doing. The second is the real fix and
should be costed separately.

## Reproduction

Not yet reproduced deliberately — both occurrences were in ordinary use and the
triggering condition (isolate eviction, or a dropped SSE, mid-turn) is not
directly controllable from outside. A harness that opens a turn, drops the SSE,
and evicts or restarts the isolate before `turn_end` would demonstrate it. Worth
building as part of the fix rather than before it.

## Operator note

Two incidents, two recoveries offered, both lossy. Worth treating the pairing
itself as the defect: whichever way the operator turned, they lost something.
