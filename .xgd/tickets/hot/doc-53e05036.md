## What this document is for

The consultant can hand construction to a cheaper worker. It almost never does, and
when it does the result is usually unusable. This document says why, what to change,
and in what order — and it argues for a stronger version of the feature than the one
we shipped: **the primary session stops writing L1 at all, and construction becomes
something it commissions rather than something it performs.**

The measurements below come from every delegation that has ever run — nine worker
sessions, seven of which took a turn — recovered from the local dev D1
(`turn_spend.attributed`, plus the `chat_transcript` and `tool_transcript` comments
on each session's chat ticket). EPIC-20 carries the full transcripts and the
arithmetic; this document carries the design.

## The finding that reframes the feature

**The worker builds well. It reports badly.**

Across seven delegations: **80 element writes over 266 tool calls, and 17 refused
calls of which 15 were a single platform bug** (REQ-300, since fixed). On the five
runs not blocked by that bug, Haiku 4.5 drove a sixty-operation L1 surface with
0–1 refusals each. Every write that landed, stayed. None has been found wrong.

And yet **one delegation in seven returned an answer the caller could act on.**

| worker | calls | `set_l1` | reported | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q73amk` | 28 | 4 | yes | blocked by REQ-300 |
| `tql0pf` | 26 | 4 | yes | blocked by REQ-300 |
| `u4ej95` | 13 | 2 | yes | check-pairing artifact |
| `qen037` | 51 | 22 | **no** | **silent** |
| `ya6wkq` | 54 | 18 | yes | answered invented checks |
| `msskzt` | 70 | 25 | yes | **accepted** |
| `3bje6q` | 24 | 5 | **no** | **silent** |

Plus two worker sessions (`hcd48w`, `g62erw`) that hold a session header and zero
turns.

So the thing standing between us and much cheaper construction is not the model's
competence at construction. It is a **reporting channel that loses its payload**.
That is a narrower and more tractable problem than "the delegate isn't good enough",
and it is why this document proposes widening the delegate's role rather than
retiring it.

### The economics, for scale

| | element writes | measured spend | per write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consultant (Opus 5), 2026-09-25 → 27 | 94 | $50.76 | **$0.54** |
| Workers (Haiku 4.5), 5 metered runs | 37 | $1.70 | **$0.046** |

**~12× cheaper per element write.** The consultant figure is whole-turn cost, so it
is an upper bound rather than an estimate. Over the window the local meter covers,
workers did **27% of the element writes for 2.9% of the spend**, at quality we
cannot distinguish.

## The four faults, precisely

### F1 — The worker is handed the consultant's memory, and it derails it

`host-core.ts` resolves `const ledger = deps.ledger(slug)` **per site**, and
`registerMemoryProviders` registers `session.summary` on the single
`PrimingProviders` registry every role is assembled from. The comment beside the
worker toolbox says a worker composes *"no ledger… no session context"* — that is
true of its **tools** and false of its **priming**.

So a worker on its first turn is handed the consultant's standing note and reads it
as a resumption:

> *"Thank you for the standing note. I see I'm in the middle of a multi-section
> spacing task. Let me pick up where I left off."* — `3bje6q`, turn 1

It then re-narrates its whole state on every subsequent iteration — *"I'm picking up
mid-engagement. My standing note shows… I've completed:"* followed by the full list
— **14 times** in `3bje6q`, **34 times** in `qen037`, 9 in `ya6wkq`, 5 in `tql0pf`.
`builder_reminders` holds two entries, `site.line` and `site.digest`, and **no
`act-rather-than-narrate` line**, which the consultant's reminder set does have.

The cost is measurable: `3bje6q` spent **14,421 output tokens to make five element
writes** — about 2,900 output tokens per write, nearly all of it status narration.

### F2 — A report can be lost, by two unrelated mechanisms that look identical

**`MAX_TOOL_ITERATIONS = 50`.** `qen037` made **51 tool calls**, hit the cap, and
was cut off mid-sentence — *"Let me continue systematically with the remaining
divider rules."* 22 writes had landed and two items of the brief remained.

**A report emitted as prose.** `3bje6q` did the whole job — five `get_l1`/`set_l1`
pairs, all five rows written, every check genuinely verified in its own narration —
and then emitted a **complete, well-formed `<invoke name="ReportResult">…</invoke>`
block as message text**. Not truncated; the closing tag is present. The host saw no
tool call.

Both reach the caller as `outcome: silent` — empty summary, empty `changed`, every
check `unreported` — while real writes sit committed on the site. That is the worst
shape this mechanism can produce, because the caller's only recovery is to
re-inspect everything, at Opus rates, which is the exact cost delegation exists to
remove.

Note what F2 is **not**: a content problem. `msskzt`'s report was excellent — six
checks quoted back verbatim, 27 items in `changed`, a 334-character summary. The
report is good when it arrives.

### F3 — The caller has no independent account of what happened

Everything in a delegation result is the worker's word. The surface says so: the
`delegate` operation declares `provenance: "untrusted"`. There is no field the host
itself wrote. So a worker that reports nothing leaves nothing, and a worker that
reports wrongly is not contradicted.

This is also the gap under the checks the consultant actually writes. **Four of the
seven briefs ever written asked for a containment check** — `u4ej95`, `qen037`,
`ya6wkq`, `msskzt` — all of the form *"the page `home` has zero changes recorded
against it during your work"*, two of them naming the mechanism (*"verify with
list_changes"*). All four asked the worker to audit its own containment, and none
got an answer the caller could rely on.

### F4 — Delegation is stated once, and the grant does not match the briefs

`templates/delegation-method` is `priming.json` entry **[2] of 7, before the cache
boundary**. The consultant's `reminders` list has nine entries and **none is about
delegation**. So the instruction to delegate is read once and sits ~186k of prefix
behind the model by the end of a sitting — while `act-rather-than-narrate`, a single
corrective line, *is* in the per-turn tail, because standing behaviour needs
repeating. Delegating is standing behaviour and is not repeated.

Separately, the grants do not line up with what briefs ask for. `builder` has
`ReadSite, AuthorPages, ManageComponents, MeasureDrawings, DrawImages`; the
consultant additionally has **`ManagePages`, `WriteConfig`, `ManagePalette`**.
`tql0pf`'s brief told it to *"add four palette colours and use the named colours
everywhere"* — to a role that cannot create one. REQ-300 blocked it before the grant
could, so we have no observed refusal, but the brief was already asking for
authority the role does not hold. The surface is explicit that this produces *"a
worker that is refused, not a worker that obeys"*.

## The design

Four changes, and one of them is the keystone.

### 1. The host derives a diff, and returns it beside the self-report

**The keystone.** At the end of a delegation the host returns, alongside whatever
the worker said, **a structural diff of the L1 tree it changed** — derived by the
host, from the store, with no worker involvement.

**How.** Snapshot the draft document when `delegate` begins; diff the tree when it
ends; return the changed paths with their old and new values. Deliberately **not**
an accumulation of per-write records: the brief template itself warns that
*"addresses regenerate after every write"*, so path-keyed per-write diffs can
misalign against a tree that has moved under them, whereas a single before/after
tree diff is immune to reordering by construction. Both snapshots and the diff are
host-side and cost no tokens; only the diff enters the conversation. `list_changes`
already returns `now`, and `set_l1` already returns `{changed, message, now}`, so the
watermark gives a free early-out when nothing changed at all.

**Not the change journal.** `site_changes` looks like the obvious source and is the
wrong one. Its `before`/`after` is a **text rendering** bounded at
`JOURNAL_TEXT_LIMIT = 300`, and `journal.ts` says so by design: *"where the change is
textual, the before and after text"*. Measured over the real log: of **337 `l1.set`
records, 276 (82%) have `before == after`**, because a padding, colour, font-size or
layout edit does not change an element's text — and that is most of what a worker
does. All five of `3bje6q`'s records are `before == after`. The journal tells you
*which paths were touched*; it cannot tell you what changed inside them.

**What it costs.** Measured on `3bje6q`'s writes, where the transcript holds both
sides:

| | bytes | tokens |
|---|---|---|
| The element after the write | 2,582 | ~645 |
| Its journal record | ~750 | ~190 |
| **The true structural diff** | **287** | **~72** |

The whole diff for one write is one line:

```
0.8.1.responsivePadding: <absent> → {bottomPx:{keyframes:[320:29, 375:29, 768:45, 1024:64]},
                                     topPx:{keyframes:[320:5, 375:5, 768:7, 1024:10]}}
```

**15% of the element's size.** Across the run, five writes ≈ 1.4 KB ≈ **360
tokens** for a complete, exact, field-level account. The largest delegation in the
corpus (`msskzt`, 25 writes) would be a few thousand tokens at most. Against a
delegation that costs $0.30–$0.60 and a caller turn carrying ~190k of prefix, this
is free.

**Why it is the keystone, and not merely useful.** Three things follow from it that
nothing else gives:

- **It is the first trusted field in a delegation result.** Everything else is
  `untrusted` by declaration. This is the host's own record.
- **It survives a silent worker, and nothing else does.** For `3bje6q` the caller
  would have received *five elements changed at these paths, these fields, these
  values* instead of an empty summary and five `unreported` checks. That converts
  both silent failures from *total loss, re-inspect everything* into *the work
  landed, here it is, the account is missing*.
- **It answers most of the accept list mechanically.** Of `3bje6q`'s five checks,
  the diff settles four with no trust in the worker at all: the 1024 keyframe is
  visible; *"no fontSizePx, colour, width, text or src was changed"* and *"reveal,
  sticky, stacked and zoom are still present"* are provable **from the diff's shape
  alone**, because nothing but `responsivePadding` appears in it. And the whole
  containment class from F3 — *"page `home` has zero changes"* — becomes a fact
  about the diff rather than a question put to the worker.

### 2. The report becomes unlosable

Two mechanisms, two fixes, and the first is that they must stop looking alike.

- **Cap exhaustion is not silence.** A worker that reaches
  `MAX_TOOL_ITERATIONS` must come back saying so, with what it had done, not as
  `outcome: silent`. A caller can act on *"it ran out of turns"*; it cannot act on
  *"it said nothing"*.
- **A worker that ends its turn without reporting is asked once.** `3bje6q` had the
  entire report composed — it simply put it in the wrong channel. One further turn
  recovers it. Discarding it is the expensive choice.

Both live in framework code (`api_tools.js`, `delegation_toolbox.js`), beside the
already-landed BUG-68.

**What is deliberately not here:** changing the report's *shape*. It is already
right. `msskzt` proves the contract works when the payload arrives.

### 3. The worker gets a worker's context

Stop delivering the consultant's ledger to a worker, and give `builder_reminders`
the `act-rather-than-narrate` line the consultant has. Small, ours, and independent
of everything else — which is why it goes first.

This is also the cheapest route to one half of F2: `qen037` spent its fifty
iterations partly on re-narration and ran out before it could report. A worker that
does not re-narrate does not exhaust the cap on the same job.

### 4. The primary stops writing L1

The end state. The consultant's grant loses its write groups; construction is
commissioned, never performed. The builder's grant widens to cover what briefs
actually ask for — `ManagePages`, `ManagePalette`, `WriteConfig` — because
*read-only primary* is a line we can hold and explain, while *read-only except
pages, palette and config* is not.

And the prose changes to match: delegating stops being an option mentioned once
before the cache boundary and becomes the method, **with an entry in the per-turn
tail**, for F4's reason.

**The analogy that makes this natural**, and the reason it is not a downgrade: this
is how image generation already works here. The consultant does not draw; it writes
a detailed prompt and a picture comes back. Nobody experiences that as a loss of
control, because the brief is where the judgement lives and the rendering is where
the labour lives. L1 construction has the same shape — large payload, small
decisions — and the measurements say the cheap model renders it correctly.

## Why this order, and what gates the last step

**3 → 1 → 2 → 4.**

Step 4 is last and **gated**, not merely sequenced. Today two delegations in seven
lose their account entirely. Remove the primary's write tools before the diff lands
and every construction goes through a channel that drops its answer ~29% of the
time — and the primary cannot even repair a bad write itself, because it no longer
has the tool. Step 1 is what makes step 4 safe: with a host-derived diff a silent
worker is still a legible worker, and the failure mode becomes *an incomplete
account of work we can see* rather than *work we cannot see*.

**The gate.** Ten consecutive delegations in which the diff and the self-report do
not contradict each other, and no delegation returns `silent` with writes in its
diff. That is a fact about recorded runs rather than a judgement, so it can be
checked rather than argued.

Steps 3 and 1 are independent of each other and both independent of 2, so the order
above is about risk rather than dependency — except that 4 depends on 1, and 1
depends on a framework hook (see the tickets).

## What this design is not

- **Not a new backend or model choice.** Which model a worker runs is
  `delegation.json` (`workers.builder.backend: claude_builder` → `claude-haiku-4-5`,
  `max_tokens` 32000) and stays there. Nothing here names a model.
- **Not returning the worker's conversation.** The surface's absence entry on this
  stands: the worker's turns must not enter the caller's context, or the tokens are
  moved to the expensive side rather than saved. The diff is the host's record, not
  the worker's transcript — which is exactly why it is admissible.
- **Not nested delegation.** One level, unchanged. The `builder` role is granted the
  delegation surface scoped to no roles, so the floor is enforced in the grant.
- **Not a change to the check contract.** BUG-68's `compound_check` and
  `unasked_check` refusals have landed and are working: `3bje6q`'s brief carried
  five discrete one-sentence checks, which is the behaviour they were for.
- **Not a rollback of the feature switch.** `delegation.json`'s `enabled: false`
  remains the rollback, and it must keep working after all four steps.

## How we will know it worked

- Element writes performed by workers as a share of all element writes — today
  **27%** over the metered window, target approaching 100% after step 4.
- Cost per element write — today **$0.54** (consultant) against **$0.046**
  (worker).
- Output tokens per element write inside a worker — today **~2,900** for
  `3bje6q`; step 3 should move this most.
- Delegations returning `silent` — today **2 of 7**; target zero, and zero
  *tolerated* once the diff exists.
- Delegations whose diff contradicts their self-report — today unmeasurable, which
  is the point.

## The tickets

Six, and the seams are chosen so that nothing cheap is blocked behind anything
expensive.

**This document** — the design. No code.

**1 · Upstream, `lagrange-framework` — a delegation result must account for the
work whether or not the worker reports.** Three things that serve one property, so
one ticket: a host hook the delegation machinery calls to obtain an
externally-derived account of the work, carried on the result as trusted; cap
exhaustion reported as itself rather than as `silent`; and a worker that ends its
turn without reporting asked once before its account is discarded. Cross-repo, so
this is a ticket there and **no code here**. Items 2 and 5 below depend on the hook.

**2 · The diff provider** *(ours, blocked on 1's hook)*. Snapshot the draft either
side of a delegation, diff the tree, render the changed paths with old and new
values, and feed it to the hook. Explicitly not built on `site_changes` — §1 above
says why, with the 82% measurement.

**3 · The worker's context** *(ours, unblocked, ships first)*. Stop delivering the
consultant's ledger to a worker; add `act-rather-than-narrate` to
`builder_reminders`. Kept separate from 2 despite both touching `host-core.ts`,
because folding it in would block the cheapest fix in this document behind an
upstream dependency.

**4 · The builder's grant** *(ours, unblocked)*. Widen `builder` to `ManagePages`,
`ManagePalette`, `WriteConfig` so the role covers what briefs already ask for. Ships
before 5 so that a widened worker is exercised while the consultant can still
write — which is the only window in which a grant gap shows up cheaply.

**5 · Delegate-first prose** *(ours, blocked on 2)*. Rewrite
`templates/delegation-method` for a consultant that commissions rather than
performs, state what the diff is and that it is trusted, and add a per-turn tail
entry so the method is repeated rather than stated once.

**6 · The read-only primary** *(ours, gated on the §"Why this order" gate)*. Remove
the consultant's L1 write groups. One config change, held until the evidence is in.

Kept separate on purpose: 5 and 6 both edit `instances.json`/`priming.json` and could
be one ticket, but 6 waits on ten recorded delegations. A ticket that sits half-done
for a week is worse than two tickets.
