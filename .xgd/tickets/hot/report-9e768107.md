Findings 1 and 2 were two levers on one defect, as the assessor said: the story
body already states that the slug match runs "against the entire ISO 639-1
language registry", but no AC claimed the registry was *whole* and no test could
tell the shipped 184-code registry from a curated top-twenty subset. Both are now
closed — one new AC, one new UAT, no production code touched.

## What the new UAT asserts

Real entry point throughout — `validateSite` via the file's existing `errorsFor`
helper, the same call every other slug criterion uses. Nothing mocked.

1. **Two-directional membership sweep.** All 676 two-letter segments `aa`–`zz`
   are validated; the refused set is asserted **equal** to a canonical ISO 639-1
   list transcribed into the test from the standard (not imported from
   `packages/site-schema/src/locale.ts` — a test reading the module's own list
   would agree with a curated subset just as happily). Equality both ways: a
   missing real code is an unguarded collision; a reserved non-code is a tax on
   ordinary page names with no collision behind it.
2. **Closed-registry anchor.** `expect(new Set(ISO_639_1).size).toBe(184)` — the
   assessor's suggested count anchor, safe because ISO 639-1 is frozen. `sh`,
   `in`, `iw`, `ji` are withdrawn and correctly absent from both lists.
3. **Rare codes refused identically to `de`.** `ki`, `nv`, `io`, `cu`, `za` —
   none exercised by any other criterion — each refused at `/pages/0/slug` with
   the slug, the locale reason and both alternatives in the message; each paired
   against a same-shaped non-code (`kx`, `nq`, `iq`, `cq`, `zq`) that validates,
   so the refusal is attributable to membership and not to shape.

## Falsifiability verified, not assumed

The registry in `packages/site-schema/src/locale.ts:292` was temporarily reduced
to a ten-code curated subset (`de fr en ga pt es it nl zh ja`) and the suite
re-run. Result: **1 failed | 10 passed** — the new UAT was the *only* test that
caught it (`expected [ 'de', 'en', 'es', 'fr', 'ga', …(5) ] to deeply equal
[ Array(184) ]`), reproducing exactly the blind spot finding 2 described. The
file was then restored (`git diff` on it is empty) and the suite re-run: **11
passed / 11**.

## Finding 3 (warning, AC-1428) — no change, as directed

## The near-finding the assessor rejected — left alone

The country derivation table's breadth (66 rows, 8 RTL countries of which 2 are
exercised) remains unpinned, per the assessor's explicit instruction. The
asymmetry holds: ISO 639-1 is closed and frozen, so pinning it costs nothing in
future maintenance; the country table grows by a one-row data edit, so pinning
its breadth would tax exactly the property that makes it a data edit. AC-1435's
"validate every row, however many there are" is the right shape there and was
not changed.
