## Symptom

First-Draft-Test Dina's Diner 4 (site_99aa2f2e…), header rendered at 375/768/1280/1440 px with the
real renderer and Chromium:

- **The emblem starts small and on the left.** Dina's logo is a detailed emblem (script name, DINER
  bar, swash; ~1.66:1, eight colours). The operator asked for it centred, about 50% larger, with the
  address top left (REQ-462). The run drew it on the left at 64 px tall (106×64), later 80 px, with
  the menu links in 18 px `body` beside it.
- **The address was squeezed above the links.** The builder's header (00:00 UTC, 2026-10-26) put the
  address in 14 px caption type stacked over the nav, inside the right-hand group — not the top-left
  address the operator described.
- **On a phone, Get directions disappeared.** In that header the builder wrapped the nav in a group
  with the address. Below 640 px the whole button was hidden, against the consultant's own brief
  ("at 375px both the logo and Get directions are visible").
- **The current phone header looks unplanned.** In the later flat header (logo, nav, button), at 375 px the
  "Menu" toggle sits alone top right and Get directions wraps under it beside the logo.

## Cause

1. **The consultant wrote the brand from scratch, so the logo-based seed never ran.**
   `read_design` showed a blank starter design (`brand.name: h2`), with nothing derived from the
   logo. The consultant then wrote `brand: {header: {arrangement: logo_left, links: body,
   location: true}, logo: {header: 64, footer: 80}}` itself. REQ-462's `proposedBrand` (emblem →
   `logo_centred`, ×1.5 desktop / ×1.25 phone) and BUG-286's `headerLinks` (`lead` beside a logo
   ≥72 px) only fill what a design leaves unsaid (`seededDesign`/`seededHeader`). The consultant left
   nothing unsaid, and nothing told it the logo is an emblem.
2. **`arrangeHeader` only recognises a menu that is one of the root's own items.**
   (`packages/site-schema/src/l1/design.ts`). `isMenu` tests `kind === 'nav'` on the root's
   children. A group holding the nav isn't the menu, so it becomes `main` (the first non-menu item
   after the logo), and the real action — the button — gets `fromFull` and is hidden below
   `L1_HEADER_FULL_FROM_PX`.
3. **Nothing checks that a header keeps its main action on a phone,** and nothing lays out the phone
   row on purpose. The logo, menu toggle and button just wrap.

## Fix

1. **The design starts from the logo.** When the site has a logo and the design has no brand yet, the
   host puts the brand the logo suggests (`proposedBrand` + `headerLinks`: for an emblem, centred,
   larger, links set a step up, the address leading the secondary details when the location is part
   of the pitch) into the design before the consultant writes one. `read_design` shows it as the
   starting brand. The consultant can still change it, but on purpose. A `set_design` that replaces a
   seeded brand with a contradicting one gets a note saying what the logo suggested, so it isn't
   done by accident.
2. **A group holding the menu counts as the menu.** In `arrangeHeader`, a root item that contains
   the site's nav is treated as the menu: it stays visible on a phone, and it is never taken as the
   main action. The main action is the first item after the logo that is neither the menu nor a
   group holding it, so a header's button is never hidden on a phone.
3. **The phone header is laid out on purpose.** At phone widths the header is one row: logo at the
   start, then the menu toggle and the main action together at the end, with no wrapping onto a
   second line. If they don't fit beside the logo, the action goes before the toggle and the logo
   shrinks to its phone size instead.
4. **A layout check flags a header that loses its main action on a phone.** If a header's link or
   button (other than the menu) is visible at desktop and hidden or off-screen at 375 px, the host's
   layout check reports it, and so do header items that wrap onto a second row at 375 px.

5. **The phone menu button is a hamburger by default.** On a restaurant site, the word "Menu"
   on the navigation toggle collides with the food "Menu" page inside it, so a hungry visitor
   tapping "Menu" gets a list of pages instead of prices. The nav's folded button becomes a typed
   choice, `icon` (☰), `label` (the word) or `icon_label`, defaulting to `icon`. The renderer draws
   the three bars itself (no icon markup in site data, per the structured-only security policy).
   The button keeps `menuLabel` (default "Menu") as its accessible name and exposes whether it is
   open, and its tap target is at least 44×44 px.

## What changed

1. **The design starts from the logo** (`tools/generate/src/cli/ai/host-core.ts`, `seedBrand`). When the
   site shows a logo and its design has no `brand.logo` (a blank starter design with only `brand.name`
   counts), `read_design` first writes the brand the logo proposes: `brand.logo` from `proposedBrand`,
   and `brand.header` from the seeded design (arrangement by the logo's character, links by
   `headerLinks`, `location: true` when the positioning card is location-led). If the links name a
   role the design lacks (`lead`), that role is written too, from the seeded design, so the brand is a
   system. The read answers `seeded: { brand, why }`, and `why` says it is the starting brand and the
   consultant's to change. A design that sizes the logo but has no `brand.header` still gets BUG-286's
   arrangement, as before.
2. **A `set_design` that changes the brand and departs from the logo's is written, with a `note`**:
   a short list of lines naming the arrangement the logo suggests (and that it is an emblem) and/or a
   header logo under 80% of the proposed desktop size. A write that does not touch `brand` gets no note.
3. **A group holding the menu is the menu** (`packages/site-schema/src/l1/design.ts`, `arrangeHeader`).
   It stays visible on a phone, what else it holds (the address) shows only from 640 px, and it is
   never taken for the main action, so a header's button is never hidden on a phone.
4. **The phone header is one row**: the arrangement's groups no longer wrap; the main action, a text,
   is kept on one line (`nowrapFromPx: 0` unless it says how it wraps); in a header part a folded nav
   sits last in its row below its collapse width (CSS `order`), so the row reads logo · action · menu
   button at every phone width; and below 640 px the header logo, and a group holding only it, may give
   way (shrink, drawn whole with `object-fit: contain`) rather than the row wrapping or the page
   scrolling sideways (`packages/framework/src/l1/render.ts`).
5. **Layout check `phone_header`** (`layout-check.ts`/`.json`, below `belowPx` 640): `header_action_hidden`
   when every link or button in the header outside the menu (the logo's link aside) is hidden or off
   screen; `header_wraps` when the header's logo, menu button, actions and words sit on more than one row.
6. **The menu button is a hamburger by default**: nav gains `menuButton: 'icon' | 'label' | 'icon_label'`
   (absent → `icon`). The renderer draws three bars itself (spans, `aria-hidden`); with `icon` the
   `<summary>` carries `aria-label` = `menuLabel` (default "Menu"); with a label the words are its name.
   It stays a native `<details>` disclosure (open state exposed natively, no script), at least 44×44 px.

## Design decisions made during implementation

- **Phone order is fixed, not conditional:** the menu button always ends the phone row (action before
  it), which is the ticket's fallback order and the conventional one; it avoids per-fit layout logic.
- **"Visible at desktop, hidden at 375" is checked at the phone width alone**, since the layout check
  runs each width separately: a header that has actions but shows none of them on a phone is reported.
- **The seeded brand is written to the store, not just shown**, so it survives into renders and the
  consultant changes it with an ordinary `set_design`.
- **The write note is a list of short lines** because write confirmations clip each string to 200
  characters (REQ-361); one sentence was cut before the size.
- Superseded expectations: REQ-422's phone-collapse UAT pinned a "Menu" word button (now three bars
  named "Menu"); BUG-276's refusal UAT read the design before writing (reading now seeds the brand, so
  it writes first); BUG-286's links-role render check saw 22px only via the old word button, so it now
  renders with a nav entry.
