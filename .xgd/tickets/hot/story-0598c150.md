## Story

**As a** site owner whose pages will one day show prices and appointment times,
**I want** every amount and every moment to become text through one shared,
locale-aware formatter that refuses ambiguous or lossy inputs, **so that** the
price a visitor is shown is exactly the price they will be charged, an
appointment reads as the right hour in the right place even across a daylight-
saving change, and nothing wrong is baked into a published snapshot that can
never re-render itself.

## Description

A site declares where its business is (CAP-104: a country, and a locale, a
currency and a timezone derived from it). This story is what reads those
declared facts and turns a stored value into text.

**Money** is held as an integer count of a currency's smallest unit plus an ISO
4217 currency code, and displayed by deriving from that pair. The scale is the
currency's own minor-unit count, not a fixed two: a currency with no minor unit
and one with three exist, and a fixed divisor undercharges the first
hundredfold and overcharges the second by a thousand. The decimal is exact past
the point where floating-point division silently drops a unit, because a shown
price is a legal claim. Placement, grouping and separators come from the locale;
the symbol and the decimal count come from the currency; neither can answer for
the other, which is why they stay two arguments.

**Time** is an unambiguous instant plus an IANA zone id, displayed by deriving
from that pair. A zone-less wall-clock string is refused, because accepting one
would silently reinterpret it as whichever zone the machine that ran the render
happened to be in and bake that machine's property into an immutable snapshot.
An explicit numeric offset is admitted, because at a formatting boundary the
distinction that matters is ambiguous versus unambiguous. An instant plus a zone
survives the weeks in which two regions have left summer time on different
dates, where a stored fixed offset is an hour wrong.

**And the standing determinism conflict is resolved rather than left open.** The
platform's build-determinism rule previously read as an unqualified prohibition
on reading the clock during a render — which a module whose whole job is
time-varying content could not satisfy. The resolution: render output stays
byte-deterministic, and time-varying content is resolved on the client or at
request time and is never derived from the render clock. The prohibition
therefore stands untouched; what changed is that showing a date is no longer
mistaken for breaking it. The seam offers no way to format "now" — the rule
expressed as an API rather than as something to remember.

**In scope**: the two formatting operations, their input contracts and their
refusals; the pass-through of presentation options; the byte-determinism of the
render artifact; the absence of any clock-reading form; the recording of the
resolution as a contract a module author will find.

**Out of scope**: what a site declares (CAP-104 / STORY-122); the payments and
calendar capabilities themselves, which do not exist — the seam exists ahead of
them deliberately, so neither has to invent its own answer. No caller in the
product formats money or a date today.

## Technical Context

- Depends on Site Locale Identity (STORY-122, CAP-104) for the resolved locale,
  currency and timezone a caller passes in, and for the shared judgement of
  which IANA zone ids are real. This story does not re-derive any of them.
- Both operations delegate wholly to the platform's internationalization
  library for symbol, separator and date order. Nothing here hand-authors a
  currency symbol, a decimal separator or a date order — the correctness claim
  is that the right inputs reach a standard formatter, not that the formatting
  itself is reimplemented.
- The determinism claim is asserted on the artifact (the same source rendered
  twice, compared file by file, through both render paths) *and* structurally
  (no source on the framework's render path reads the ambient clock), because
  the artifact is what ships and never re-renders.
- The seam is published from the framework's public surface, so a behavior
  module reaches it the same way it reaches any other framework facility.

## Reconciliation Decisions

- **Negative amounts** (decided at reconciliation, 2026-08-31): the intent
  states the money contract in terms of scale, exactness and refusals and is
  silent on sign. The landed code carries the sign through the exact-decimal
  construction so the locale's own negative convention applies, and a refund or
  credit is therefore the same representation with a sign rather than a second
  shape. Formalized as part of the exactness criterion; sign handling is not an
  edge case for a payments capability, it is half of a ledger.

- **Presentation options pass through, but the load-bearing facts do not**
  (decided at reconciliation, 2026-08-31): the intent names only that the
  zone-name display is passed through, and is silent on the general contract.
  The landed code accepts caller presentation options for both operations while
  fixing the facts that make the operation what it is — that the value is money
  in the stated currency, and that the moment is shown in the stated zone — so
  an option cannot quietly turn a price into a bare number or move a booking to
  another zone. Formalized because a caller-supplied options bag with no stated
  boundary is exactly the hole through which a module reintroduces its own
  answer.

- **A syntactically well-formed but impossible instant is refused** (decided at
  reconciliation, 2026-08-31): the intent names the two refusals it cares about
  (zone-less, and unknown zone) and is silent on an instant that has the right
  shape but is not a real date-time. The landed code refuses it rather than
  formatting whatever the runtime makes of it. Formalized with the other instant
  refusals: the whole reason to validate at this boundary is that the failure is
  unrecoverable once published, and "shaped correctly" is not the same claim as
  "real".

No contradiction between intent and code was found for this item. Every
behaviour the code exhibits either matches a statement in the intent or is one
of the three gaps recorded above.
