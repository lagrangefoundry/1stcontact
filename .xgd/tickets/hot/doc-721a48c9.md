## Seed lessons — 2026-07-01 (from the founding design conversation)

### A. "Expensive vs template" tells (rubric seeds)
- **Text over imagery.** Expensive sites lay text over background images with an overlay for legibility. Solid-colour-only heroes read cheap/template. *(Biggest gap found; drives [[REQ-14]].)*
- **Distinctive typography.** Serif display faces / considered pairings (faelan.com: Fraunces + Cormorant Garamond + Playfair) read expensive; `system-ui`/default reads template.
- **Motion.** Purposeful entrance / scroll-reveal / hover is the single biggest "alive vs static-template" signal — on every polished site. *([[REQ-16]].)*
- **Considered, limited colour.** Dark palettes + glow/gradient accents (Composio, PlayerZero); bold gradients (Cartesia); or generous whitespace that lets content breathe (Cohere). Confidence + restraint.
- **Human/organic touches.** Hand-drawn illustration to counter sterile-AI sameness (Anthropic).
- **Alternating band surfaces.** Subtle alternating section backgrounds (gigabytealchemy.ai: cream ↔ darker cream) read as *designed*.
- **Art-directed heroes.** Layered/overlapping imagery with per-image edge treatments (circle crop, torn edge, soft/feathered edge), slight rotation, overlay, hover micro-interaction (faelan.com). Free positioning + treatments = "designed, not templated." *([[REQ-15]].)*

### B. Structural / style-scope lessons
- **Colours are relative to their band.** Text colour is chosen against the section's background ("white text *because* dark image"); global tokens can't hold this — reason per style-scope band. *([[DOC-13]].)*
- **Real sites are vertical stacks of style-scope bands.** Most small-business sites (bakery, gigabytealchemy.ai) are conventional band-stacks → covered by standard modules. "Wow" is a *separate axis* (the ceiling corollary, [[REQ-19]]).
- **Edge treatments are cheap.** Circle = `border-radius`; soft = radial `mask-image`; torn = just a pre-torn PNG asset (mirror the byte — no primitive needed).

### C. Comps (taste reference set)
- **Tier 1 — expensive, template-free (flagship target):** sycamore.so, Composio (dark/motion), Cartesia (bold type/gradient/motion), PlayerZero (dark/glow), Cohere (minimal/breathing), Anthropic (hand-drawn/human), Stability (image-led/confident).
- **Tier 2 — WebGL/3D frontier (bespoke, later):** F1/OFF+BRAND (3D helmet + cinematic scroll, Awwwards Site of the Year 2025), Bruno Simon (drive-a-car Three.js+physics portfolio), Lusion, Immersive Garden, Messenger (WebGL planet). Three.js dominates the top end.

### D. Import / reproduction lessons
- **Static fetch is blind on modern sites.** Wix/Shopify hide colours behind `var()` chains and serve hero images as lazy placeholders → must render (headless browser) and read *computed* styles. *([[DOC-13]].)*
- **Import bar is perceptual** — "indistinguishable to the eye," not pixel-perfect; the eyes loop is the acceptance test. Free-valued *structured* positions are what make indistinguishable reproduction possible. *([[REQ-21]].)*

## Open design questions
- **"A site like X"** — reproduce style/effects/structure (yes); content/brand (no). Prompts + tooling must enforce form ≠ content. *([[DOC-15]] §7.)*
- **Homogenization vs template-free** — encode principles + *diverse* references, never a house style. *([[DOC-16]] §7.)*


## Import lessons — 2026-07-02 (gigabytealchemy pass, added to §D)

- **Transcribe, don't reconstruct.** The single biggest repro failure mode: building the page from a hand-authored config + spot-checking the *screenshot*, instead of reading values out of the captured DOM. Six value-level deltas (gold `#F5E6A3` subhead rendered white; wordmark `text-7xl` shipped smaller; wordmark gradient vertical vs the author's `linear-gradient(90deg …)` gold→orange; `border-l-4 border-emerald-400`/`border-amber-400` callout bars rendered bold; footer `text-slate-400` vs cream) were **all explicit inline styles / utility classes in `raw.html`** and were still missed. Runbook: [[DOC-19]].
- **`raw.html` > `capture.json` > screenshot.** The rendered DOM has the exact values; `capture.json` is *partial* (dropped the gradient, the 7xl size, the callout bars — recorded only 2 of 6); the screenshot is for judgment only. Capture-side fix + values-diff = [[REQ-31]].
- **Screenshots hide value-level deltas.** Near-neighbour colours (gold-vs-gold), exact sizes (7xl vs 4xl), gradient *direction*, subtle left-bars, and opacity scrims are invisible at a glance. Vision judges composition/intent; a values-diff catches the numbers. Reserve each for its job.
- **Chunk by fidelity layer, not by module** — structure → typography/scale → colour/treatment. The diff shrinks monotonically and each layer is independently verifiable.
- **Design signatures are author-intentional.** The wordmark's gold→orange horizontal sweep was a deliberate choice; even when the framework can't yet express a treatment, *notice and flag it* rather than silently substituting a default. Backlog: [[REQ-32]].

## Strategic research reports — 2026-08-10 (from the CHAT-134 next-stages discussion)

Two commissioned research reports landed as standalone docs — full detail there, distilled headlines here:

- **[[DOC-31]] — Premium, Non-Template Web Design — Differentiation Audit.** For every recurring differentiation pattern found across award-winning/agency/startup/DTC sites, classifies it as a *technical ceiling* (Wix/Squarespace/Framer structurally cannot do it — e.g. WebGL/3D, custom shader work, continuous scroll-linked motion, site-wide custom cursors) vs. a *taste/ambition gap* (the platforms could do it, their users just never do — e.g. locked restrained palettes, typography-as-narrative-device, invented content architecture instead of the generic hero→cards→testimonials→footer sequence). Headline finding: the taste-gap category is doing most of the differentiation work even among the most acclaimed sites — restraint and a *system* (not more spectacle) is the highest-leverage, lowest-technical-risk lever, especially for conversion-first clients. Also includes Checklist A/B/C of concrete, citeable anti-patterns (template DNA, cosmetic-only customization, custom-coded-but-undesigned) worth turning into an automated critique pass against our own generated output, not just competitors'.
- **[[DOC-32]] — Why People Pay For Design — Independent-Designer Economics & AI-Website-Builder Pricing Benchmarks.** People buy back their own time/risk, an outcome (not an artifact), differentiation/trust signaling, and — specifically relevant to us — *direct, no-hand-off access* to whoever actually does the work. Independent designers price flat/tiered (82%), not hourly, sweet-spot $2,500–$5,000 for a small-business site. Existing AI builders (Wix/Squarespace/Framer/Durable/10Web/Dorik/Hostinger) all use flat-subscription + metered-AI-credits, $10–$40/mo, zero live human involvement. B12 is the one direct analog (live AI + human, $1,999 one-time add-on) — confirms our $200–$1,000 band is a deliberate discount off a real comparable, not an invented price point. Recommends: scope-tier the paid session (not hourly), meter the $30–$80/mo tier by credits (not "unlimited"), and lead sales positioning with the boutique "direct access" narrative rather than "AI website builder" so we're compared against $2,500+ designers, not $20/mo Wix. Explicitly flags that hitting the 90%-net-positive target still needs our own internal token/cost model — this report gives market structure, not our unit economics.
