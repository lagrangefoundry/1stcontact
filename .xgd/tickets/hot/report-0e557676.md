## Notes for the Editor

- **Nothing here is a matrix/intent alignment defect.** Both warnings are about
  the *robustness of the evidence*, not about what the matrix claims. Finding 2
  is a stale phrase in one Verification section that the test already contradicts
  correctly; finding 1 is a test-harness choice. Hence PASS.

- **Finding 1 is the one worth acting on.** It is not an environment excuse: the
  same repository, in the same sandbox, already reaches the same routing table
  without a socket, and the file that does so documents *why* ("the difference
  between 'the guards hold' and 'we could not ask'"). As things stand, the four
  criteria that are specifically about the guards being enforced **where the
  write happens, against a client with no check at all** — the load-bearing claim
  of this whole capability — are the four that report nothing on a developer
  machine, and the suite costs 121 s to tell you so. Two of the four (AC-1233's
  refusal naming the count; AC-1238's draft channel serving the new colour with
  no rebuild) happen to be corroborated in-process by the popup suite; AC-1235
  (rename refusals at the origin) and AC-1237 (the closed operation vocabulary
  and the full re-taken census on every write) are corroborated nowhere.

- **Quality of the evidence elsewhere is high and worth preserving.** Three
  patterns in particular: the colour arithmetic is *imported* from the module the
  renderer resolves through rather than re-implemented in the test; AC-1251 takes
  the expected refusal text from the store itself instead of hard-coding a copy;
  and every "changes no state" claim is checked by comparing the whole draft —
  `site.json` and every page — byte for byte. Any future edit to these files
  should keep all three.

- **Run evidence for this check** (this machine, this commit):
  `tests/reconciliation-palette-popup-surface.test.ts` — 12 passed / 12.
  `tests/reconciliation-palette-management.test.ts` — 8 passed, 4 skipped,
