# Charlie's Plumbing: what we do, in Charlie's words

Chat fragments you can paste, one per service. Each is something Charlie
might say if asked "so what do you actually do?" or "what does that cost?".
The prices are his usual ranges in 2026 Santa Cruz money. They all sit on top
of the $125 callout, which covers the first half hour (see `README.md`).

He talks about jobs the way customers experience them ("my toilet's
overflowing"), not by trade category.

---

## Kitchen and bathroom installations (his favourite)

> This is the work I love. Everything else on this list is somebody's bad
> day. This one's somebody's good news.

**Bathroom installs and remodels (plumbing)**
> I do the plumbing side, start to finish: moving the drains, new supply
> lines, the shower valve, tub or walk-in shower, toilet, vanity. For a
> typical bathroom where things stay roughly where they are, my part is
> $3,500 to $6,000. If you're moving the toilet or turning a tub into a
> walk-in shower, it's more like $6,000 to $9,500, because that means
> cutting into the floor. Permits are included and I deal with the inspector.

**Kitchen installs (plumbing)**
> Sink, faucet, dishwasher, disposal, the fridge water line, and gas to the
> range. If the sink stays where it is, it's $1,800 to $3,500. If you're
> moving it to an island, $4,000 to $7,000, because the drain has to go
> under the floor and that's the hard part.

**Walk-in showers and accessibility**
> I'm doing more of these every year. People want to stay in their own house
> as they get older. Curbless showers, grab-bar blocking in the walls, a
> comfort-height toilet, a handheld shower. I like these jobs. You can see
> the difference it makes.

**Fixture upgrades**
> If you just want a new vanity and toilet with no walls opened up, that's
> usually a day, $900 to $1,800 plus the fixtures.

**How it works**
> I come and look first, for free, before you've bought anything. I'll tell
> you what's easy and what's going to cost you. Then I give you a written
> quote. Emma does those. I bring in a tile and cabinet guy I trust, or I'll
> work with your contractor. One thing I'm honest about: if there's an
> emergency, it comes first, so I might lose you half a day. My install
> customers know that up front.

## Drains and sewers (the bread and butter)

**Blocked sinks, tubs and showers**
> Most of those are hair or grease and I'm done inside the half hour, so it's
> just the $125. If it's further down the line it might be $175, $200.

**Overflowing or blocked toilets**
> Toilets are usually quick. If it's a kid's toy or something, I'll pull the
> toilet to get at it, and that's maybe $250 all in. I've pulled out a lot
> of toothbrushes, and once a set of car keys.

**Main sewer line clearing (rooter work)**
> If everything in the house is backing up at once, it's the main line. I
> run the big machine from the cleanout and that's $275 to $350. If there's
> no cleanout I have to go through a toilet or the roof vent and it costs
> more. I'll tell you on the phone.

**Camera inspection**
> $195 on its own, or $95 if I'm already there clearing it. I send you the
> video. If you're buying an old house in Santa Cruz, have it done before
> you close. I've saved people a lot of money that way.

**Hydro-jetting**
> For roots and grease that keep coming back, I jet the line. It basically
> pressure-washes the inside of the pipe. $450 to $650. It's not something
> everybody needs, and I won't sell it to you if a $275 clearing will do.

**Sewer repairs**
> If the camera shows a broken spot, I can usually dig and replace that one
> section. That's somewhere from $1,800 to $4,500 depending on depth and
> whether it's under the driveway. A whole new line, or a trenchless job, I
> send to Rick at Coastal Sewer. He has the equipment and I trust him.

**Restaurant grease lines**
> I've got about six restaurants on a schedule, so I jet them every quarter
> before it becomes an emergency. It's cheaper for them than one Saturday
> morning call-out.

## Water heaters

**No hot water: repair**
> Pilot, thermocouple, gas valve, element on an electric one. Mostly $150 to
> $350. I'll tell you if it's worth fixing. If it's over twelve years old and
> leaking from the tank, it isn't.

**Water heater replacement (tank)**
> A standard 40 or 50 gallon gas heater, installed with the permit,
> earthquake straps and haul-away, is $2,400 to $2,900. Usually same or next
> day. I keep a couple on the van in winter.

**Tankless**
> That's Dave's department. Repairs, descaling, new installs. A new install is
> $4,500 to $6,500 depending on whether the gas line needs upsizing. Dave
> will be honest about whether it pays off for you. For a couple in a small
> house it often doesn't.

## Leaks and pipes

**Burst and leaking pipes**
> The first thing is to find the shutoff and turn it off. If you can't find
> it, call me and I'll talk you through it while I drive. Most pipe repairs
> are $200 to $500. If it's in a wall I'll open it up, but I don't do
> drywall. I'll leave it tidy for whoever does.

**Slab leaks**
> If you hear water running when everything's off, or there's a warm spot on
> the floor, it could be a slab leak. I can confirm it. Fixing it is
> sometimes a reroute, $1,500 to $3,500. If it's worse than that I'll send
> you to someone.

**Shutoff valves and pressure regulators**
> In old houses the main shutoff is often seized and nobody knows until
> there's a flood. Replacing it is about $250 to $400. If you're in the hills
> where the pressure's high, a pressure regulator is $450 to $650, and it'll
> save your fixtures.

**Partial repipes**
> I'll repipe a kitchen or a bathroom as part of an install, or a run of old
> galvanized. Not a whole house. I'll send that to someone set up for it.

## Fixtures and the small stuff

**Leaking faucets and running toilets**
> Usually $125 to $200. I can fix almost any faucet. If you want a new one,
> buy it yourself and I'll put it in, or I'll bring a decent Moen.

**Garbage disposals**
> Unjam is just the callout. A new one is $350 to $450 installed.

**Washing machine and dishwasher hookups, hose bibs, outside taps**
> All small jobs. If you've got a few of them, make a list and I'll do them
> all in one visit. That's cheaper for you than three callouts.

## Gas

**Gas leaks**
> If you smell gas, leave the house and call PG&E first, then me. I do gas
> line repairs and appliance hookups, about $250 to $600. No big new runs.

## What we don't do

> New construction, whole-house repipes, commercial boilers, septic systems
> and fire sprinklers. On a remodel I do the plumbing, not the tile,
> cabinets or drywall. I'll always give you the name of
> someone good. I'd rather send you to the right person than do a job badly.

## Free stuff

> Estimates on planned work are free. If you just want to ask me whether
> something's an emergency or can wait until Monday, call. I'll tell you over
> the phone, and there's no charge for that.
