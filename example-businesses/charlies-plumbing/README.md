role-play test of the site builder (dev business `biz_9db1ad5360ed5f0426934fccf3c55192`,
site `site_2a2833c06700bc0896dbd380398c7f1e`, room `chat-2f4fa2f0`). The
operator plays Charlie. Alice is the consultant and Bob is the coordinator.

## Chat fragments: things Charlie can say

Charlie doesn't know anything about web design or marketing. Ask him about
fonts, colours or "your brand" and he'll shrug. What he does know is his
customers, his jobs and his week. Paste these in as they are, or mix them
together. Some answer questions that are already open (marked ★). The rest
are things a good consultant should draw out of him.

Introducing anything here makes it canon. Once it's said, keep the facts
consistent.

### The callout fee ★

> It's $125 to come out, and that includes the first half hour. Most small
> jobs are done in that. After that it's $45 for each quarter hour, plus parts.
> I tell them all that on the phone before I get in the van. Nobody should
> open the door not knowing what it's going to cost.

> Same price at 9 at night as at 9 in the morning. I never did the after-hours
> premium thing. If you've got water coming through the ceiling you've got
> enough problems.

### How fast we get there ★

> In town we're usually there inside the hour. Aptos or up in the hills, call
> it ninety minutes. If I can't make it I'll say so straight off, and I'll
> give you the name of someone who can. I'd rather lose the job than leave
> somebody waiting all night.

> We do about 25, 30 jobs a week between me and Dave. Winter's more. First
> big storm of the year, the phone doesn't stop.

### Where we work ★

> Santa Cruz mainly. Westside, Seabright, the Eastside, up by the university.
> Live Oak, Capitola, Soquel. I'll go to Aptos or Scotts Valley for regulars,
> but I won't tell a stranger I'll be in Boulder Creek in an hour when I
> won't.

### Guarantee ★

> If I fix it and it goes again inside a year, I come back and it's free. Not
> the callout, not the labor. Parts are covered by the maker's warranty and I
> deal with that, not you.

### What I love doing (favourite work)

> If I'm honest, my favourite job is a kitchen or a bathroom. Putting one in
> from scratch. Emergencies pay the bills, but they're somebody's bad day.
> With a bathroom you plan it with them, you rough it all in, and three
> weeks later they've got a shower that works the first time they turn it
> on. Nobody's crying. They're showing it to the neighbours.

> I like the old houses best. A 1920s bungalow on the Westside where nothing
> is square and the drain's in the wrong place. Anybody can plumb a new
> build. Making a new bathroom work in an old house takes knowing what
> you're doing.

> I do all the plumbing: moving the drains and supply lines, the shower valve,
> tub, toilet, vanity, kitchen sink, dishwasher, disposal, gas to the range. I
> work with a contractor or two I trust for the tile and cabinets, or I'll
> work with yours. I'll come and look before you've bought anything. That's
> when I can save you the most money.

> I'd like to do more of them. The trouble is the emergencies come first. If
> somebody's flooding, the bathroom waits a day. My install customers know
> that and they put up with it.

### What we're best at

> Drains. Sewer lines in old Santa Cruz houses, which are mostly clay pipe
> from the forties with a hundred years of tree roots in them. I've got a
> camera, so I can show you what's down there before I tell you to dig
> anything up. Half the time you don't need to.

> Water heaters too. I can usually tell you over the phone if it's worth
> fixing or if it's done.

### Who our customers are

> A lot of older folks who've had us for twenty years. Landlords near the
> university, with student houses where somebody flushed something they
> shouldn't have. Families. And the vacation rental people in Capitola. They
> call at 8pm on a Friday with guests arriving Saturday.

> I've got a couple of property managers and a few restaurants on the
> Eastside who call us first. They don't shop around. That's the business
> really: people who call us again.

### What I did last week

> Tuesday I had a lady in Seabright, 80-something, with sewage backing up into
> her tub. Roots in the lateral. Cleared it, ran the camera, showed her the
> picture on my phone. I told her it would be fine for a year or two but the
> pipe's cracked and she should start saving. She made me a coffee. She's been
> a customer since 2004.

> Thursday night, about 9:30, a water heater let go in a garage in Live Oak.
> Two inches of water. Dave got there in forty minutes, shut it off, and had a
> new one in by noon Friday. They had a newborn, so we did that one first.

> Saturday was a restaurant on Soquel Ave with a grease line blocked solid at
> six in the morning, before they open. You walk in, it's a mess, you walk out
> and it works.

> In between all that I finished a bathroom in a little house in Pleasure
> Point. It used to have a pink tub from 1962, and now it's a walk-in shower
> for a guy who just had his hip done. He sent me a photo of himself standing
> in it. That was the best part of my week.

### Why us and not the big guys (USP)

> When you call the big franchise outfits you get a call centre and a guy on
> commission. He's going to sell you a $9,000 repipe because that's his job.
> When you call us you get Emma, and then you get me or Dave. Nobody here is
> on commission. If it's a $20 part, it's a $20 part.

> We're not the cheapest. We're the ones who turn up when we said, tell you
> the truth, and clean up after.

> I've been doing this twenty-five years and I've never had to advertise. It's
> all word of mouth. That's why we've never had much of a website.

### Dave

> Dave did four years in the trade with an outfit over the hill before he came
> back to work with me. That was his choice; I never pushed him. He's better
> with the camera and the new stuff than I am. Tankless heaters, that kind of
> thing. People ask for him by name now, which I'll admit I like.

### Emma

> Emma is the business. I'd have gone broke in year two without her. She does
> the scheduling, the quotes and the money. She's the one who decided we'd
> stop doing 24 hours, and she was right. She knows half our customers better
> than I do.

### What we won't do

> We don't do new construction, whole-house tear-outs or commercial boilers.
> I do the plumbing on kitchens and bathrooms, not the tile and cabinets,
> though I'll find you someone good for those. If it's not us, I'll tell
> you who it is.

### Things Charlie says when asked web or design questions

> Honestly, I wouldn't know. You tell me. I just want people to call.

> My daughter-in-law says it should look "clean". I don't know what that
> means for a website, but she's usually right.

> Whatever gets the phone to ring with the right kind of job. If it got me
> a couple more bathrooms a month, I'd be a happy man.
