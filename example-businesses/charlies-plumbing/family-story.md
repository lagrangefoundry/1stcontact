# Charlie's Plumbing: the family story

The backstory for Charlie, Emma and Dave. The first part is the full
narrative, for your own reference. The second part is the same story broken
into fragments Charlie might actually say. He doesn't tell it as a story
unless he's asked, and he doesn't tell it in order.

It's consistent with `README.md` (founded 2001, independent, gave up 24-hour
callouts, Dave worked elsewhere for four years, Emma runs the business) and
with `services.md` and `testimonials.md`.

---

## The story

Charlie Moreno grew up in Watsonville. His dad worked the strawberry fields
and fixed everything in the house himself because there was no money to pay
anyone. At 19 Charlie started as a helper for an old Santa Cruz plumber,
Walt Hendricks, who worked out of a 1978 Ford van with no radio. Charlie got
his journeyman card, then spent eight years with Walt learning the old
houses on the Westside and in Seabright: clay sewer laterals, galvanized
pipe, the wrong fittings left behind by somebody's brother-in-law.

He met Emma in 1994. She was doing the books for a hardware store on Soquel
Ave where he bought fittings. They married in 1996, and Dave was born in
1998.

In 2001 Walt retired. He didn't sell the business; he gave Charlie his
customer list, a box of index cards held together with rubber bands. Charlie
got his own CSLB licence, bought a used van, and painted "Charlie's
Plumbing" on the side himself. The lettering was crooked and stayed that way
for three years. Emma kept her day job and did Charlie's books at the
kitchen table at night. She says the first year he undercharged everyone and
gave half of it away.

By 2005 there was enough work for Emma to quit the hardware store. She took
over the phone, scheduling, quotes and money. She's the reason the business
survived the 2008–2010 slump: she moved them onto maintenance contracts with
landlords and restaurants, so the money came in steady when homeowners
stopped spending.

The work Charlie always loved most was installs. Walt had done the plumbing
on half the kitchen and bathroom remodels on the Westside, and Charlie
learned that from him: how to fit a new bathroom into a 1920s house where
nothing is square. Over the years the emergencies took over, because that's
what the phone rang for. Getting back to more kitchens and bathrooms is what
Charlie wants from the next five years. It's also easier on his knees than
crawl spaces at 9pm.

For twenty years they ran 24-hour callouts. Charlie did nights, weekends,
Christmas. In 2019 he worked a sewer backup at 3am on Christmas morning,
then fell asleep at a red light on the way home. Nobody was hurt. That week
Emma drew the line: 8am to 10pm, with an answering service overnight.
Charlie fought her on it for about a month. It cost them some customers, and
the rest of them stayed. He'll tell you now that she was right.

Dave didn't want to be a plumber. He went to Cabrillo College for a year,
didn't like it, and in 2017 took an apprenticeship with a bigger outfit over
the hill in San Jose. He didn't want to just be "Charlie's kid". He spent
four years there doing new builds and tankless installs, and learned the
cameras and the newer equipment. In 2021 he called his dad and asked whether
there was room for him. Charlie says that's the best phone call he's ever
had.

Now it's the three of them and two vans. Charlie is 56 and his knees are
going. He wants to be doing less crawl-space work in five years. Dave is
married to Lucia, who's a teacher, and they have a baby on the way. The plan,
never said out loud as a plan, is that Dave takes it over. Charlie wants the
business to still be here and still be called Charlie's when he's gone,
because people trust the name.

Walt's index cards are still in a shoebox in the office. About forty of
those households still call.

## The same story as Charlie would tell it (paste-ready)

**How it started**
> I started out helping an old guy called Walt Hendricks when I was nineteen.
> Best teacher I ever had. When he retired in 2001 he gave me his customers,
> a shoebox of index cards. That was the business. That and a van I painted
> myself.

> We still have the shoebox. Some of those people still call. Their kids
> call now too.

**Where he's from**
> I'm from Watsonville. My dad fixed everything himself because we couldn't
> afford anyone. I think that's where I got it.

**Emma**
> Emma was doing the books at the hardware store where I bought my fittings.
> I bought a lot of fittings that year.

> People think she answers the phone. She runs the place. In '09, when
> nobody was spending, she got us onto contracts with landlords and
> restaurants, and that kept us alive. I'd have just kept driving around
> hoping.

**Giving up 24 hours**
> We did nights for twenty years. Then one Christmas morning I fell asleep at
> a red light on the way home from a sewer job. Emma said that's it, we stop
> at ten. I was angry about it for a month. She was right.

**Dave**
> Dave never wanted to be a plumber. Went off to San Jose to work for a big
> outfit so nobody'd call him "Charlie's kid". Four years. Then he rang me up
> one night and asked if I had room for him. I had to pull the van over.

> He knows the new stuff better than me. Tankless, cameras, all the
> paperwork. I know the old houses. Between us there isn't much we haven't
> seen.

**The work he loves**
> Walt taught me bathrooms. He used to say anyone can fix a leak, but a good
> bathroom in an old house is a craft. That stuck. Emergencies are what the
> phone rings for, but a kitchen or a bathroom is what I'd do all day if I
> could.

**The future**
> My knees have about five more years of crawl spaces in them. What I'd like
> is for Dave to take more of the night calls and for me to do more
> bathrooms. After that it's Dave's. He's got a baby coming. I want it still to be called
> Charlie's when I'm gone. People know that name. It means something round
> here.

**What it all means to him**
> I've never advertised. Twenty-five years, all word of mouth. That's the
> thing I'm proudest of, if I'm honest. Every job, somebody told somebody.
